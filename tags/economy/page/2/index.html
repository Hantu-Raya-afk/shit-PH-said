Anwar Says He ‘Won’t Betray the Mandate’ — While Quietly Planning Snap Elections to Dodge Fuel Subsidy Cuts

#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened

On May 1, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told a Labour Day crowd that his government “will not betray the people’s mandate” as oil prices surged to US$115 per barrel — up from US$70 just three months earlier. He pledged to maintain the subsidised RON95 price at RM1.99 per litre. Meanwhile, Bloomberg reported on April 24 — citing unnamed sources — that Anwar is considering calling a general election by the third quarter of 2026, potentially as early as October, specifically to get ahead of politically toxic fuel subsidy cuts. Malaysia’s monthly fuel subsidy bill has surged nearly 10-fold to approximately RM7 billion since the US-Israeli war with Iran began in February, threatening to blow up the government’s budget deficit targets. Second Finance Minister Amir Hamzah Azizan acknowledged recent reforms haven’t fully eliminated the subsidy burden.

Anwar Unveils RM710M ‘PACE Package’ on Labour Day — Two Days After His Treasury Ordered RM5.4 Billion in Cuts to Health and Education

2026-05-01
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened

On May 1, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim took the stage at the National Workers’ Day celebration at Unifi Arena, Bukit Jalil, to unveil the RM710 million PACE Economic Resilience Package — billed as a measure to “strengthen jobs, skills and wage resilience.” He acknowledged that oil prices had surged from US$70 to US$115 per barrel and pledged the government would “continue taking necessary measures” to face global economic uncertainty. This was delivered just two days after the Treasury Secretary-General Johan Mahmood Merican issued an internal directive on April 29 ordering sweeping budget cuts across all ministries: a 10% reduction on essential services, 20% cuts to statutory bodies, a hiring freeze, and a proposed RM5.4 billion in cuts to health and higher education — including RM3.06 billion slashed from healthcare alone.
