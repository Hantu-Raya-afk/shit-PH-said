📌 What Happened

On April 16, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry banned two books under the Printing Presses and Publications Act for allegedly containing “communist elements and ideologies.” One of the banned titles is linked to the memoirs of Shamsiah Fakeh, a prominent figure in Malaysia’s Malay Left history. Ramanan Says ‘No Issue’ Giving Bestinet Another Migrant Worker Contract — His Own PKR MPs Demand an Investigation

Said by: R. Ramanan, Human Resources Minister

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened

On April 16, Human Resources Minister R. Ramanan told The Star that his ministry sees “no issue” with Bestinet Sdn Bhd — the company founded by tycoon Aminul Islam — developing a new foreign worker recruitment system called TURAP (The Universal Recruitment Advanced Platform). This came weeks after Bloomberg published an investigation into endemic corruption in Malaysia’s migrant worker recruitment from Bangladesh, naming both Bestinet and Aminul. Ramanan initially rejected the Bloomberg report as “unverified and inaccurate,” but then told The Star his ministry was open to the system. By April 20, nine PKR backbenchers — including Wong Chen (Subang), Nik Nazmi (Setiawangsa), and Lee Chean Chung (Petaling Jaya) — issued a joint statement demanding an investigation into Ramanan’s ministry over the Bestinet connection.

Saifuddin Nasution Bans Three Books as ‘Communist Threats’ — Then Reverses After Backlash, Proving PH’s ‘Reform’ Is Just Improv

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister & PH Secretary-General

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened

On April 15–16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry under Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — who is also PKR member and PH Secretary-General — issued prohibition orders banning three books: Shamsiah Fakeh’s memoir Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10, Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10), and a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong titled Mao Zedong: China dalam Dunia Abad ke-20. The ministry cited “communist elements and ideologies” that posed a danger to national security. The Mao Zedong book was published under Selangor state government funding — a state led by Anwar’s own PKR. Within a week, after a firestorm of criticism from civil society groups, publishers, and PH’s own coalition MPs — including DAP’s Jamaliah Jamaluddin, who is Shamsiah Fakeh’s granddaughter — Saifuddin announced the government was revoking all three bans.
