On April 16, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry banned two books under the Printing Presses and Publications Act for allegedly containing “communist elements and ideologies.” One of the banned titles is linked to the memoirs of Shamsiah Fakeh, a prominent figure in Malaysia’s Malay Left history. Home Ministry Bans Books on Malaysia’s Communist History as ‘Dangerous’ — Then Reverses Course After Its Own Coalition Screams

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry issued a prohibition order against two books published by independent publisher Gerakbudaya: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen ke-10 — a memoir of Shamsiah Fakeh, a Malay feminist and anti-colonial fighter who joined the Communist Party of Malaya — and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah. A third book, a Malay-language translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was also banned. The ministry claimed the books contained “communist ideologies” that could “undermine public harmony and order.” Days later, after swift backlash from civil society groups including PEN Malaysia and Article 19, PH’s own coalition members, and Shamsiah Fakeh’s granddaughter Jamaliah Jamaluddin — a Selangor state exco member under PKR — Home Affairs Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail announced the bans were being revoked.

On April 16, Human Resources Minister R.
