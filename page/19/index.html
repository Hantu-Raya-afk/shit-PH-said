Saifuddin Nasution’s Ministry Bans 3 Books on ‘Communist’ Grounds — Then Reverses After Backlash, Proving the Madani Government Censors First, Thinks Later

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry — under PH’s Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — issued prohibition orders on two books published by independent bookstore Gerakbudaya: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Regimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi Regimen 10: Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah, both dealing with the Malayan Communist Party’s anti-colonial struggle. A third book — a Malay translation of NYU professor Rebecca Karl’s academic work on Mao Zedong — was banned around April 22. The bans were issued under the Printing Presses and Publications Act, which requires no explanation. After a week of fierce backlash from civil society, ARTICLE 19, PKR’s own MP Tan Kar Hing, and political veteran Rais Yatim, Home Minister Saifuddin announced on April 23 that the ministry was in the process of revoking all three bans and pledged to consult publishers before future bans.

Saifuddin’s Home Ministry Bans Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Scrambles to Reverse It After PH’s Own Coalition Revolts

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened

On April 16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry — led by PKR’s Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — issued prohibition orders against two books, declaring them a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” One of the banned books was a memoir of the grandmother of a political ally of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. A separate ban was also placed on a Malay-language translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong, which had been funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government. Civil society groups including Article 19 and Gerak Budaya immediately condemned the bans. Gerak Budaya, the independent publisher that received the prohibition order, announced it would challenge the decision through legal channels.

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-15

📌 What Happened

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil announced on April 15, 2026, that the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) had flagged 441 posts about the global energy crisis for removal from social media, opened 54 investigation papers against citizens, submitted 12 cases to the Attorney General’s Chambers, charged one person in the Sepang Sessions Court, and compounded five others at RM25,000 each — all under Section 233 of the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 for posting about fuel price increases, diesel costs, electricity tariffs, and energy policy during Malaysia’s worst energy crisis in years, with oil hitting US$115 per barrel.
