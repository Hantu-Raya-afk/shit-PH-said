Fahmi Delays WFH Decision for Civil Servants Until After Hari Raya — The Government That Told You to ‘Reduce Energy Usage’ Can’t Even Decide on Working From Home

2026-03-17
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister (Government Spokesperson)

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-17

📌 What Happened

On March 17, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil announced after a post-Cabinet meeting that the government’s decision on implementing work-from-home for civil servants would be delayed until after Hari Raya Aidilfitri. This came just four days after FMT reported on March 13 that the decision was supposed to be made on Tuesday, March 17. Fahmi said “several aspects still need to be refined” and the matter would go to the National Economic Action Council (MTEN) via the Chief Secretary to the Government. He stressed frontline services like healthcare and education would not be affected.

Johari Says Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — Then His Own Ministry Says He ‘Misspoke,’ and PH’s Own MPs Demand Answers

2026-03-17
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-17

📌 What Happened

On Sunday March 16, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani told reporters the Malaysia-US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade was “null and void” following the US Supreme Court’s decision striking down Trump’s tariff powers — “It is not on hold. By Monday, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil told reporters after Cabinet that MITI would “continue engaging” Washington, while eight PKR backbenchers issued a joint statement demanding the government clarify the deal’s status and warning the agreement was “one-sided.”

Amir Hamzah Rules Out Stimulus Package Amid Oil Crisis — Government’s Plan Is to ‘Wait and See’

2026-03-16
#Economy 

Said by: Amir Hamzah Azizan, Finance Minister II

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-16

📌 What Happened

On March 16, Finance Minister II Datuk Seri Amir Hamzah Azizan told reporters after launching the Malaysia Co-Investment Fund engagement day in Kuala Lumpur that the government will not introduce an economic stimulus package despite the escalating West Asia conflict. His reasoning: “We do not yet know whether the crisis in West Asia will continue or not. If it does not continue, I think there is no need to implement something like that.” His stated approach: “Look at the short term and ensure the stability that we have.” He confirmed the government is now spending RM3.2 billion a month on fuel subsidies — RM2 billion on petrol and RM1.2 billion on diesel — and encouraged the public and businesses to “reduce energy use.”
