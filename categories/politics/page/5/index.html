Cabinet Finally Discusses Azam Baki Report — Punts Decision to Chief Secretary, Refuses to Reveal Findings

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister / Cabinet

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened

On March 11, 2026, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil announced that Cabinet had received and discussed the findings of the special committee — chaired by Attorney General Mohd Dusuki Mokhtar — investigating MACC chief commissioner Azam Baki’s shareholding controversy. Cabinet’s decision: hand the matter to Chief Secretary to the Government Tan Sri Shamsul Azri Abu Bakar to determine “next steps.” Fahmi refused to disclose the report’s contents. Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened

Mohamad Hasan Says Travel Agencies Must Bring Home Stranded Umrah Pilgrims — 'It Cannot Be Left to the Government Alone'

Said by: Mohamad Hasan, Foreign Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened

Foreign Minister Datuk Seri Mohamad Hasan, speaking at a press conference in Putrajaya on March 11, declared that travel companies and agencies managing umrah packages must take full responsibility for bringing home pilgrims stranded by the West Asia conflict. PH Backbenchers Call for Snap Election Dressed Up as 'Reform' — Hold GE16 With All State Polls, Save Money, They Say

Said by: Azli Yusof (Amanah-Shah Alam) / Jimmy Puah (PKR-Tebrau)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened

On March 11, two Pakatan Harapan backbenchers — Shah Alam MP Azli Yusof (Amanah) and Tebrau MP Jimmy Puah (PKR) — called for all state assembly elections to be held concurrently with the 16th General Election. Police Question C4 Anti-Corruption CEO Under Section 124B — Anwar's Government Uses 'Plot to Topple' Probe to Interrogate Civil Society

Said by: Bukit Aman (Royal Malaysia Police) / C4 Center (Pushpan Murugiah, CEO)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened

On March 10, 2026, the Center to Combat Corruption and Cronyism (C4) CEO Pushpan Murugiah was summoned to Bukit Aman and questioned for approximately 90 minutes under Section 124B of the Penal Code — activities detrimental to parliamentary democracy. The questioning was linked to the police investigation into an alleged plot to topple the government, reportedly because Pushpan’s name appeared in an email deemed relevant to the probe. But the interrogation went far beyond that email. Police questioned C4’s sources of funding, whether the organisation had received instructions to issue statements, and — remarkably — challenged C4’s right to comment on matters of national governance. C4 confirmed that several other political commentators and academics had also been summoned to Bukit Aman under the same investigation.
