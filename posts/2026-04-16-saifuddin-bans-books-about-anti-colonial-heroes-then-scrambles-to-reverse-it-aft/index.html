Saifuddin Bans Books About Anti-Colonial Heroes, Then Scrambles to Reverse It After His Own Coalition Revolts

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, Home Affairs Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — a PKR man, from Anwar’s own party — ordered a ban on two Malay-language books under the Printing Presses and Publications Act: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Regimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi Regimen 10: Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah. Both recount the struggles of Malayan Communist Party members in their fight against British colonialism. A third book, a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was also prohibited. Days later, after fierce backlash from civil society, press freedom groups, and — crucially — PH’s own coalition members, Saifuddin announced he was “in the process of revoking” the bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a government that came to power promising reformasi, intellectual freedom, and an end to authoritarian-era censorship. Instead, it reached for the same colonial-era legislation that BN and Mahathir used for decades to suppress inconvenient history. Malaysia has already banned 27 books as of March 2026 — more than all 24 banned in the whole of 2025. The trend is accelerating under Anwar, not reversing. When your own PKR MPs publicly remind you that you used to be a university lecturer who championed open discourse, you know the hypocrisy has become impossible to hide.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar built his entire political mythology on being a man of ideas — the intellectual reformist jailed for daring to challenge power. Now his own Home Minister is banning memoirs of anti-colonial women fighters and academic texts funded by his own party’s state government. The reversal makes it worse, not better: it shows the ban was never about national security, it was a reflex — the authoritarian muscle memory of a government that talks reform but governs like its predecessors. You don’t get credit for putting out a fire you started.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s book-banning habit is bipartisan and deeply entrenched. BN banned Chin Peng’s memoirs, books on May 13, and anything that challenged the official Malay-nationalist narrative. Anwar’s government has not only continued the practice but expanded it — 27 bans in three months of 2026 alone. The Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984, a Mahathir-era instrument of control, remains untouched despite PH’s manifesto pledges to reform media laws. The pattern is clear: every Malaysian government discovers that censorship is easier than argument.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised intellectual freedom. You promised Malaysia would no longer ban books like a frightened authoritarian state. Instead, your Home Minister — from your own party, Anwar — is banning memoirs of anti-colonial heroines and academic texts your own state government funded. Your own MPs had to publicly embarrass you into reversing the decision. This isn’t reform. This is BN governance with better PR. If you can’t even let Malaysians read their own history, what exactly are you reforming?
