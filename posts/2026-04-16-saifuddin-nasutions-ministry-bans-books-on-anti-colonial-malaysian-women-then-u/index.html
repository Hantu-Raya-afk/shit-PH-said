Saifuddin Nasution’s Ministry Bans Books on Anti-Colonial Malaysian Women, Then U-Turns After Backlash — Reform Government Can’t Even Get Censorship Right

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 15-16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry under Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail issued a prohibition order banning two books: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Rejimen Ke-10 — a memoir of a Malay feminist who fought British colonialism — and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah. The ministry declared both books a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” Independent publisher Gerakbudaya received the ban notice, and the ministry also placed the publisher SIRD under investigation. A third book, a Malay translation of a work on Mao Zedong, was subsequently added to the ban list. After widespread public backlash from civil society, academics, lawmakers, and even PH’s own allies, Saifuddin announced around April 23 that the government would reverse the bans and introduce a new review process requiring consultation with publishers before future bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a government that rode into power on promises of reform, freedom of expression, and an end to authoritarian-era censorship — and it just banned books about Malaysian women who fought British colonial rule. The Shamsiah Fakeh memoir had been in circulation for over two decades, partly supported by government-linked entities. The ban exposed a PH administration willing to deploy the same Printing Presses and Publications Act that it once condemned under BN and PN. The reversal, while welcome, only confirmed that the ban was an act of political cowardice or bureaucratic incompetence — not principled governance. As SCMP noted, this episode sits within a broader pattern of Anwar’s government tilting rightward to court Malay-Muslim voters, sacrificing its reformist identity in the process.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A reformist government that bans books about anti-colonial heroines and then un-bans them a week later isn’t reformist — it’s rudderless. If the books were truly dangerous, why reverse? If they weren’t, why ban them in the first place? The answer is obvious: the Home Ministry acted on political reflex, not national security. Banning historical memoirs that have been freely available for 20 years under the guise of “preventing communist ideology” is the same playbook BN used for decades. PH didn’t dismantle the censorship apparatus — they inherited it and are now discovering they quite like using it. The investigation into publisher SIRD adds an even more chilling dimension: this wasn’t just censorship, it was intimidation of independent publishing.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s book-banning problem didn’t start with PH, but PH has enthusiastically continued the tradition. A DW investigation in October 2025 found that more books were banned in 2025 alone than in the previous six years combined — under a supposedly reformist government. The Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984, which gives the Home Minister sweeping power to ban publications, has been criticised by every opposition coalition that has ever existed in Malaysia — including PH when they were in opposition. Anwar himself was once a university lecturer who championed intellectual freedom. PKR MP Tan Kar Hing reminded the Dewan Rakyat of Anwar’s own lecturing days when questioning the ban. The pattern is clear: PH criticises authoritarian tools in opposition, then wields them in government.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians a New Malaysia. You promised reform of oppressive laws. You promised intellectual freedom and respect for civil liberties. Instead, your Home Minister banned a memoir about a Malay woman who fought for independence — a book your own government helped publish decades ago — and only reversed course when the backlash became politically inconvenient. Saifuddin’s new “consult publishers first” process is damage control, not reform. Real reform means repealing the Printing Presses and Publications Act, not creating a friendlier process for wielding it. Every banned book under your watch is a receipt for the reformist credentials you’ve already cashed in.
