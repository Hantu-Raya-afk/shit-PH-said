Amirudin Hails Failed PM Term Limit Bill as Proof of Anwar’s ‘Commitment to Reform’ — The Bill Lost by Two Votes Despite a Supermajority

2026-03-03
#Politics 

Said by: Amirudin Shari, Selangor Menteri Besar and PKR Deputy President

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-03

📌 What Happened#

On March 2, 2026, the Constitutional Amendment Bill 2026 — which would have limited the Prime Minister’s tenure to 10 years — failed to secure the required two-thirds majority in the Dewan Rakyat, falling short by just two votes (146 for, against 148 needed). The next day, Selangor Menteri Besar Amirudin Shari praised the failed effort as reflecting Anwar’s “commitment to reform” and urged Malaysians not to let “one incident” dampen the reform agenda, as reported by The Star on March 3.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This was supposed to be the centrepiece of PH’s institutional reform agenda — a manifesto promise from 2022 designed to prevent another Mahathir-style 22-year reign. Anwar commands a supermajority in a 222-seat house and still couldn’t whip enough votes. The defeat triggered a chain reaction: the government immediately delayed the Attorney General/Public Prosecutor separation bill, and by March 14, the South China Morning Post reported widening “reformist cracks” in the coalition. A government that can’t pass its own signature reform bills isn’t reforming — it’s performing.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Calling a failed constitutional amendment “commitment to reform” is like calling a missed penalty kick “commitment to winning.” You don’t get credit for trying when you have the numbers and still lose. PH has the largest parliamentary majority in a decade. If you can’t pass your own flagship bill with those numbers, the problem isn’t the opposition — it’s your own coalition discipline, your whip operation, or your willingness to trade reform for political favours. Amirudin’s spin is classic PH: dress up failure as virtue, call it bold, and ask the rakyat to be patient for the hundredth time.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a well-established PH pattern of overpromising and underdelivering on institutional reform. The IPCMC (Independent Police Complaints Commission), promised since 2018, was watered down into the toothless IPCC. The promised freedom of information act never materialised. Undi18 implementation was delayed by years despite bipartisan support. Each time, the explanation is the same: the spirit of reform is alive, just give us more time. Three years into government, the reform scorecard is defined more by what didn’t happen than what did.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised the rakyat a two-term PM limit in your 2022 manifesto. You had a supermajority. You tabled the bill yourselves. And you still lost. That’s not bad luck — that’s governance failure. If Amirudin wants to talk about “bold steps,” here’s one: explain exactly which coalition MPs voted against or abstained, and tell the public what was offered or withheld in the background. Reform isn’t a press statement. It’s a floor count. And yours didn’t add up.
