Akmal Admits Fuel Supply Only Secured Until June — Economy Minister Goes on Bloomberg to Say ‘We’re Still Negotiating’

2026-04-30
#Economy 

Said by: Akmal Nasrullah Mohd Nasir, Economy Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-30

📌 What Happened#

On April 30, 2026, Economy Minister Akmal Nasrullah Mohd Nasir went on Bloomberg Television — interviewed by Haslinda Amin — and admitted that Malaysia can only sustain its fuel supply until the end of June 2026. He confirmed that Petronas “is having conversations with potential suppliers” but stressed that negotiations must secure “the right price.” He also noted the government has already reduced the subsidised fuel quota from 300 litres to 200 litres per Malaysian and introduced civil servant work-from-home arrangements to manage demand. He claimed the government “still has flexibility” to manage subsidies and the fiscal deficit.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The Economy Minister of a petrostate just went on international television to admit his country’s fuel reserves run out in two months — and the backup plan is still at the negotiation stage. This is not a reassurance; it is a controlled admission of crisis. Malaysia is spending nearly RM7 billion a month on fuel subsidies, has already rationed petrol to citizens, ordered civil servants to work from home to save energy, and imposed austerity across all ministries — and the man in charge of the economy is telling Bloomberg viewers that Petronas hasn’t closed a supply deal yet. The signal this sends to markets, investors, and ordinary Malaysians is unmistakable: the government is managing a crisis it does not yet have the tools to resolve.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is the same government that told Malaysians the energy situation was “under control” and that austerity was just “prudent management.” Now the Economy Minister is on international TV conceding that supply is only locked in for eight more weeks. The framing — “we’re working to secure supply beyond June” — is spin. Translated from ministerial: we don’t have enough fuel and we’re hoping someone sells it to us at a price we can afford. Meanwhile, the government has refused to retable Budget 2026, refused a stimulus package, cut RM5.4 billion from health and education, and told citizens to “reduce energy usage.” The rakyat is being asked to ration while the government’s supply strategy is still a PowerPoint in a Petronas boardroom.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a well-worn Madani pattern of drip-fed crisis admissions. In March, Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah ruled out a stimulus and told Malaysians to cut energy use. In April, Bloomberg reported Akmal himself saying June-July would be a “critical period.” The government went from “we’re monitoring” to “reduce usage” to “supply runs out in June” in the space of six weeks — each time acting as though the new revelation were merely an incremental update rather than an escalating emergency. This is crisis management by euphemism: rebrand rationing as “demand management,” call austerity “non-critical adjustments,” and hope nobody notices the trajectory.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised competent governance. You promised Malaysia would not be run like a crumbling petro-kleptocracy anymore. And yet here we are: the Economy Minister on Bloomberg admitting the country has two months of fuel and no confirmed resupply, while the government’s response has been to ration petrol, cut hospital budgets, and tell people to use less electricity. If Petronas cannot close a supply deal before June, what is the contingency? If the deal falls through, who is accountable? The rakyat deserves a straight answer — not another minister on international TV projecting calm while the cupboard is bare.
