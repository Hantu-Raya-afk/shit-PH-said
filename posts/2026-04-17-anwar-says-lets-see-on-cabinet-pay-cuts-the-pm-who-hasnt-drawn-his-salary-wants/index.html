Anwar Says ‘Let’s See’ on Cabinet Pay Cuts — The PM Who Hasn’t Drawn His Salary Wants Credit for Sacrifice While His Ministers Keep Theirs

2026-04-17
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-17

📌 What Happened#

On April 17, after Friday prayers at Surau Bomba in Cyberjaya, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim was asked whether Malaysia would follow Indonesia in cutting ministerial salaries amid the worsening global energy crisis. His answer: ‘Let’s see.’ Anwar told reporters that ministers’ salaries are ‘already relatively low’ and that ‘what matters is that they serve well.’ He said the government is ‘prepared to consider’ further pay reductions if the economy deteriorates, but pivoted to his own gesture of not drawing a salary since 2022. Two days later, Fahmi Fadzil confirmed Cabinet members are ‘ready for further pay cuts’ — without actually committing to them.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Malaysia’s monthly fuel subsidy bill has ballooned to RM7 billion under the energy crisis triggered by the US-Iran conflict. The KLCI remains depressed. Fahmi himself announced ‘soft austerity’ measures on April 29, ordering ministries to scale down events and cut operating expenditure — while refusing to retable Budget 2026. Indonesia, facing similar pressures, already moved to cut ministerial pay. Anwar’s ’let’s see’ is the verbal equivalent of a non-answer: it sounds open-minded while committing to nothing. When the PM frames a potential 10-20% pay cut on salaries he himself doesn’t draw as a major sacrifice, while ordinary Malaysians face subsidy rationalisation and rising costs, the disconnect is structural.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar hasn’t drawn his PM salary since 2022 — a gesture he frequently reminds the public about. But a PM who doesn’t take his pay offering to ‘consider’ cutting pay he doesn’t receive is not sacrifice — it’s theatre. The existing 20% Cabinet pay cut was announced in 2022 as temporary, pending economic recovery. That recovery hasn’t arrived, and the cut has become the new baseline that Anwar frames as already-generous. Meanwhile, Amir Hamzah (Finance Minister II) explicitly ruled out an economic stimulus in March, telling Malaysians to ‘reduce energy usage.’ The message from PH to Malaysians: tighten your belts. The message from PH to themselves: ’let’s see.’

📜 Previous Cases#

This pattern of performative austerity is a PH staple. In March, Fahmi announced civil servant WFH — then delayed the decision until after Hari Raya. Mat Sabu told ministry staff to grow their own vegetables. Amir Hamzah told Malaysians to ‘reduce energy usage’ instead of offering stimulus. Every crisis measure from PH comes wrapped in hedging language — ‘may consider,’ ‘will review,’ ’let’s see’ — while the actual policy response is to wait and hope the crisis passes. Indonesia’s Prabowo government, facing the same global conditions, moved faster and with less fanfare.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians you would be a government of action, not slogans. ‘Let’s see’ is not action — it’s the phrase of a government that wants credit for considering sacrifice without actually making one. Your PM doesn’t draw his salary and uses that as a shield against every question about Cabinet austerity. Your ministers are still on the 2022 pay cut that was supposed to be temporary. Your Finance Minister told the rakyat to ‘reduce energy usage’ while your government bleeds RM7 billion a month in subsidies. Lead from the front or admit you’re managing optics, not a crisis.
