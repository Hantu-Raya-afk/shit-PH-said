Saifuddin’s Home Ministry Bans Three History Books — Then U-Turns After Backlash, Still Using the Colonial Law PH Promised to Repeal

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry issued a prohibition order on two books published by Gerakbudaya — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Regimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi Regimen 10: Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — declaring them a national security threat for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” A week later, a third title — the Malay translation of an academic book on Mao Zedong, Mao Zedong: China dalam Dunia Abad ke-20 — was also banned. On April 23, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail announced the government would reverse all three bans after fierce backlash from civil society groups, publishers, academics, and opposition lawmakers, and introduced a new process where he would personally review future bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This fiasco exposes the hollow core of Anwar’s reformist brand. The Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 — the very law used to ban these books — is a Mahathir-era censorship tool that PH once vowed to repeal. Two and a half years into power, the law remains untouched, and the Home Ministry is still wielding it to ban books about Malaysia’s own anti-colonial history. The U-turn did not fix the structural problem — it just proved the government acts first and thinks later, with no institutional safeguards against censorship. As the South China Morning Post noted on May 2, this episode is one of several signs that “Anwar’s reform agenda faces its sternest test.”

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH campaigned on media freedom, academic liberty, and institutional reform. They once denounced BN for using this exact law to silence critics and publishers. Now they are the ones banning books about Shamsiah Fakeh — a Malay anti-colonial fighter and women’s rights activist — because her memoir contains “communist elements.” The reversal only came after the backlash became politically embarrassing. If civil society had not pushed back, these books would still be banned. International press freedom group Article 19 called for the PPPA to be repealed entirely. A government that bans books by accident and then takes credit for unbanning them is not a reformist government — it is an incompetent one performing damage control.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long, ugly history of book bans under the PPPA 1984, stretching from memoirs by former communist leaders like Chin Peng, Suriani, Abdullah CD, and Rashid Maidin to academic and historical texts on left-wing movements. PH’s own 2018 election manifesto explicitly promised media reforms including reviewing the PPPA. Eight years and two governments later, the law stands untouched. Gerakbudaya, the publisher targeted in this latest round, has previously faced similar challenges. The pattern is unmistakable: PH inherits authoritarian tools, keeps them on the shelf, and then acts shocked when its own bureaucracy reaches for them.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised to dismantle the laws that BN used to control the press and silence dissent. Instead, your Home Ministry is banning history books about Malaysia’s own independence struggle, and your solution is for the minister to “personally review” future bans — as if the problem is a workflow issue and not the existence of an authoritarian censorship law. Repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984. You have had since November 2022. Every day that law remains on the books is a day you chose the convenience of censorship over the reform you sold to Malaysian voters.
