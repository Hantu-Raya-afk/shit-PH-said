PH’s Tenure Limit Bill Fails by Two Votes — Their Own Government Couldn’t Be Bothered to Show Up

2026-03-02
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister / Pakatan Harapan

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-02

📌 What Happened#

On March 2, 2026, Malaysia’s Parliament voted on the Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026, which sought to amend Article 43 to limit any prime minister’s tenure to a cumulative maximum of 10 years — a flagship reform promise from Anwar Ibrahim’s Pakatan Harapan since GE15. The bill needed a two-thirds supermajority of 148 votes. It received 146. Forty-four MPs abstained, 32 were absent, and zero voted against. The government bloc holds at least 153 seats — comfortably more than enough. Among the absent government-side MPs were BN’s Hishammuddin Hussein, Suhaimi Nasir, and Saravanan Murugan; GPS’s Henry Sum Agong; GRS’s Jeffery Kitingan; and PBM’s Larry Sng. PH’s Deputy Speaker Alice Lau was quick to note that all PH MPs attended — a defence that tells you everything about how PH processes failure.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This was not a minor policy tweak. It was the constitutional reform Anwar personally championed — a direct response to Mahathir’s 22-year grip on power, a centrepiece of PH’s GE15 manifesto, and a test of whether this government can deliver structural change. PH has spent three years telling Malaysians they are different from the old regime. When the moment came to prove it — with enough seats in the chamber to pass the bill — the government couldn’t get its own people to show up. Not a single MP voted against the bill. It didn’t die because of opposition resistance. It died because eight government-side MPs didn’t care enough to be present.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH’s immediate spin — ‘All our MPs showed up’ — is the political equivalent of a team captain blaming the bench after losing a game by one point. PH leads this government. PH chose these coalition partners. PH holds the whip. If BN, GPS, and GRS members can skip a constitutional amendment vote without consequence, that is a failure of PH’s political management, full stop. The bill fell two votes short with 153+ government seats available. That is not bad luck. That is bad leadership. Three years of reformasi rhetoric, and the government could not marshal 148 people into a room for the reform they built their brand on.

📜 Previous Cases#

This fits a pattern. The Independent Police Complaints and Misconduct Commission (IPCMC) was promised since the 2018 Pakatan government and never materialised. Local government elections — promised and shelved indefinitely. UNDI18 implementation was delayed for years before being forced through by court order. The Anti-Party Hopping Law was passed but immediately diluted with coalition-level exceptions that rendered it toothless. PH has a consistent record of announcing reforms, riding the goodwill, and then failing to deliver when it counts. The tenure limit bill is the latest exhibit in that gallery.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians you would fix the system. You had the numbers. You had the bill drafted. You had the parliamentary chamber booked. And you still failed. Don’t hide behind ‘all PH MPs were present’ — that just means you couldn’t even manage your own coalition three years into government. You put this reform in your manifesto. You told urban voters this was the reason to trust you. Now explain to them how 153 seats wasn’t enough to get 148 votes. This was your reform. This was your test. And you failed it — not because anyone stopped you, but because your own house couldn’t be bothered.
