Rafizi Plays Victim as MACC Closes In on RM1.1 Billion Arm Holdings Deal — ‘No Money Involved,’ He Says, as If That Settles It

2026-05-01
#Politics 

Said by: Rafizi Ramli, Former Economy Minister (PKR, Pandan MP)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

Former Economy Minister Rafizi Ramli announced on May 1, 2026 that he has been summoned by the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission (MACC) to give a statement on May 4 at its Putrajaya headquarters, in connection with a probe into the government’s RM1.1 billion joint venture with British chip giant Arm Holdings signed in March 2025 during his tenure. Rafizi claimed the investigation was triggered by a report from Perkasa — an NGO he dismissed as having “never previously shown interest in corruption issues” — and said he expects to be charged with criminal breach of trust or providing misleading information to Cabinet. He insisted “no money is involved” and framed the probe as politically motivated, noting it has been MACC’s “top priority” since February.

🔥 Why It Matters#

A RM1.1 billion deal with a foreign chip giant, signed during a period when Malaysia was aggressively courting semiconductor investment, deserves rigorous scrutiny — not dismissal. Rafizi’s pre-emptive victimhood strategy is politically calculated: by getting ahead of potential charges, he frames any prosecution as persecution before the facts are even tested in court. This matters because PH built its brand on demanding accountability from others. If the same standard isn’t applied to their own, the entire anti-corruption narrative — the one Anwar used to justify his “plot to destabilise” rhetoric in Parliament — collapses into selective enforcement.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Rafizi’s defence boils down to: no money changed hands, so there’s nothing to see. But breach of trust and governance failures don’t require someone to pocket cash — they require proof that a minister misled Cabinet or rushed a billion-ringgit decision without due process. Dismissing the complainant (Perkasa) instead of addressing the substance of the complaint is textbook deflection. And the claim that this is his “13th court case” is framed as a badge of honour rather than a pattern that should concern voters. PH spent years demanding that UMNO politicians answer to MACC. Now that the spotlight turns inward, suddenly the institution is weaponised?

📜 Previous Cases#

Rafizi has a long history with the courts — he was previously convicted and sentenced to 18 months’ jail over the SRC International exposé, later pardoned. He’s positioned legal trouble as proof of his reformist credentials. But the Arm Holdings deal is different: this isn’t a whistleblower case. It’s an investigation into whether a sitting minister pressured Cabinet to approve a massive deal that critics say lacked proper due diligence. Malaysia has a pattern of mega-deals gone wrong — from 1MDB to the littoral combat ships — and the common thread is always rushed approvals and insufficient oversight, regardless of which coalition is in power.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia that under your government, no one would be above the law. You said MACC would be independent. You said accountability would be the cornerstone of MADANI. So when MACC investigates one of your own over a billion-ringgit deal, the correct response is not to cry political persecution before the first charge sheet is even filed. Let the process work. Answer the questions. Show Malaysians that PH’s commitment to transparency isn’t just a slogan you deploy against your enemies and retract when it’s your turn in the chair.
