Saifuddin Nasution Bans Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Reverses After Backlash, Admits He Didn’t Even Know About It

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry declared a ban on two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — for allegedly containing communist elements and threatening national security. A third book, Mao Zedong: China dalam Dunia Abad ke-20, a Selangor state-funded academic translation, was also prohibited. One week later, on April 23, Home Affairs Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail announced he was in the process of revoking all three bans. He admitted the previous book-banning process had forced him to “firefight” because he only learned about the bans after enforcement had already taken place. He announced a new mechanism where Special Branch reports would no longer be enforced immediately — the minister would personally review them first.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a reformist government banning books first and asking questions later — the exact kind of authoritarian reflex Pakatan Harapan spent years in opposition condemning. The fact that one of the banned books was the memoir of Shamsiah Fakeh, grandmother of a DAP assemblyman and ally of Anwar, made the ban politically absurd as well as ideologically incoherent. The reversal under pressure from PH’s own coalition members — not from any principled internal review — exposes a Home Ministry that operates on autopilot from the security apparatus, with the minister as a rubber stamp rather than a decision-maker. This is not reform. This is reactive damage control dressed up as process improvement.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A Home Affairs Minister in a government that campaigned on civil liberties and institutional reform did not know his own ministry was banning books. Let that sink in. Saifuddin’s defence — that Special Branch reports were being enforced without his sign-off — is not a defence. It is an admission that his ministry was running on BN-era autopilot for over two years and he never bothered to fix the process until it blew up in his face publicly. The “new mechanism” he announced is just basic ministerial oversight that should have existed from Day One. You don’t get credit for installing a smoke detector after the house has already burned down.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long and inglorious history of book bans under the Printing Presses and Publications Act. Previous governments banned works on May 13, reformasi, governance, sexuality, and religion — anything that complicated the official narrative. PH promised to be different. Yet under their watch, the same Special Branch-to-gazette pipeline continued unchecked. This episode echoes the Sheraton Move era’s broken promises: PH leaders talked about press freedom and intellectual discourse in opposition, then allowed the bureaucratic machinery of censorship to keep running once in power. Gerakbudaya, a publisher that maintains a public “Hall of Banned” titles, has documented how this pattern predates and survives changes in government.

🎯 PH Callout#

You spent decades telling Malaysians that BN’s book bans were the mark of an insecure, authoritarian regime. You promised institutional reform, transparency, and respect for intellectual freedom. Two and a half years into power, your Home Affairs Minister doesn’t even know when his own ministry bans books — and the only reason the bans got reversed is because your own coalition partners complained publicly. That’s not reform. That’s embarrassment management. If your “new mechanism” is the minister personally reviewing bans before they’re enforced, congratulations — you’ve just discovered what ministerial responsibility means. The rest of us expected you to know that before you took office.
