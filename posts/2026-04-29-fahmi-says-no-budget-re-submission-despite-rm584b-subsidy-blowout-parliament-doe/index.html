Fahmi Says No Budget Re-Submission Despite RM58.4B Subsidy Blowout — Parliament Doesn’t Need to Know How You Spend an Extra RM43 Billion, Apparently

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister & Government Spokesperson (PKR)

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, Communications Minister and government spokesperson Datuk Fahmi Fadzil confirmed that the government has no plans to re-submit the 2026 budget to Parliament, even as the national subsidy bill has ballooned from the RM15 billion originally approved to a projected RM58.4 billion — a 290% surge driven by the US-Iran conflict disrupting the Strait of Hormuz. A Treasury directive issued the same day by secretary-general Datuk Johan Mahmood Merican ordered all ministries, departments and agencies to review their operating expenditure and propose spending cuts by May 15, including a 10% reduction on services and supplies and a 20% cut on statutory bodies.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This isn’t a minor budget overrun — it’s a RM43 billion deviation from what Parliament approved. The government is now spending nearly four times what it budgeted on subsidies, while simultaneously slashing health, education, and agency budgets by billions. In any functioning parliamentary democracy, a fiscal shift of this magnitude would trigger a supplementary budget or emergency parliamentary session. Instead, Fahmi treated it as routine housekeeping: “This is in line with the government’s intention to streamline programmes.” The government is rewriting the fiscal plan of an entire nation through internal directives, and Parliament is being told it doesn’t need to weigh in.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH spent years in opposition screaming about “backdoor governments” and executive overreach. They demanded transparency, parliamentary supremacy, and fiscal accountability. Now they’re managing a RM43 billion fiscal deviation through a Treasury memo and a press briefing. No supplementary budget. No parliamentary debate. No vote. Fahmi — the man tasked with government communications — essentially told the nation that the biggest fiscal crisis since the pandemic will be handled in-house, thank you very much. When BN pulled this kind of stunt, PH called it kleptocratic governance. When PH does it, it’s “streamlining.”

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time PH has bypassed Parliament on critical fiscal decisions. During COVID, the government used emergency ordinances to approve massive spending without full parliamentary scrutiny — a practice PH itself had criticised under the Muhyiddin administration. Anwar, when in opposition, specifically called for Parliament to reconvene to approve emergency spending. The Madani government also quietly increased the national debt ceiling in 2023 without a full public debate. The pattern is consistent: demand transparency in opposition, govern by fiat in power.

🎯 PH Callout#

You ran on “Reformasi.” You promised Malaysia Madani — transparent governance, institutional reform, parliamentary sovereignty. You said power belongs to the rakyat, exercised through their elected representatives. Now those representatives are being told they don’t need to approve a RM43 billion spending change. If this budget is sound, table it. If these cuts are necessary, debate them. If you can’t defend your fiscal decisions on the floor of Parliament, they shouldn’t be made in a Treasury memo. Put it to a vote, or stop pretending you believe in democracy.
