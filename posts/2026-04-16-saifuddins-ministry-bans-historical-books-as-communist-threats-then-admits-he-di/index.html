Saifuddin’s Ministry Bans Historical Books as ‘Communist Threats’ — Then Admits He Didn’t Even Know About It

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry announced the prohibition of two Malay-language historical books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — published by Gerak Budaya, citing “communist elements and ideologies” that allegedly threatened national security. The ban triggered immediate backlash not just from civil society groups like Article 19 but from PH’s own coalition members, including PKR MP Tan Kar Hing, who publicly reminded Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim of his own lecturing days at world universities. A week later, on April 23, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail admitted he only learned about the bans after enforcement had already taken place, and said he was in the process of revoking them — along with a third banned book, a Mao Zedong translation funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not an isolated incident — it is a pattern. By March 2026, the Anwar government had already banned 27 books, surpassing the 24 books banned in all of 2025. Under a prime minister who built his political brand on reformasi, intellectual freedom, and democratic openness, Malaysia’s censorship machinery is not just intact — it is accelerating. The South China Morning Post, in a May 2 analysis, placed the book bans alongside broader policy failures as evidence that Anwar’s reform agenda faces its “sternest test.” When your own backbenchers are publicly embarrassing you over book bans, the problem is not the books — it is the government.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A minister who does not know what his own ministry is banning is either incompetent or covering his tracks. Saifuddin’s “I only found out after enforcement” excuse is the bureaucratic equivalent of “the dog ate my homework.” This is the Home Affairs Ministry — responsible for immigration, internal security, and the Printing Presses and Publications Act — and its minister claims enforcement actions happened without his knowledge? The U-turn only came after PH’s own MPs revolted publicly. Without that pressure, these books would still be banned. The new “mechanism” where Saifuddin personally reviews bans before enforcement is an admission that the old system was running on autopilot — under his watch, for over three years.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s book-banning spree under PH is well-documented. In 2023, the government banned LGBTQ-related books, drawing accusations of “pandering to conservatives” from the SCMP. By 2025, a CIVICUS report cited book seizures and news portal closures as evidence of creeping censorship under Anwar. Article 19 called on Malaysia to reverse the bans and repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act entirely — the same colonial-era law PH once promised to reform. Anwar himself was a university lecturer who championed intellectual freedom for decades. The irony is not subtle.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised a new Malaysia. You promised to dismantle the authoritarian apparatus your predecessors built. Instead, you are banning memoirs of independence fighters and scrambling to un-ban them only when your own MPs embarrass you in public. Twenty-seven books banned by March 2026 — more than the entire previous year — and your Home Minister says he did not even know. That is not reform. That is autopilot authoritarianism with a PR team. If you cannot control your own ministry’s censorship machine after three-plus years in power, what exactly are you in power for?
