Saifuddin Nasution Bans Three Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Reverses Course Within a Week After His Own Coalition Revolts

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry under Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail banned two books published by Gerakbudaya — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Rejimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — declaring them a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” A third book, Mao Zedong: China dalam Dunia Abad ke-20, a Malay translation of an academic study funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was also banned. By April 23, after fierce backlash from civil society groups, international press freedom organisations, and members of PH’s own coalition, Saifuddin announced the ministry was in the process of revoking all three bans and that he would personally review future book bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This wasn’t some obscure bureaucratic misstep — it was the Home Ministry of a supposedly reformist government weaponising colonial-era censorship laws against historical memoirs and academic texts. One of the banned books was a memoir of the grandmother of an ally of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. Another was funded by PKR’s own Selangor government. Malaysia had already banned 27 books by March 2026, surpassing the 24 books banned in all of 2025, revealing an accelerating pattern of censorship under the Madani government. The speed of the U-turn — just eight days — confirms the ministry didn’t even do basic due diligence before pulling the trigger.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is Pakatan Harapan, the coalition that marched into power promising democratic reform, press freedom, and institutional accountability. Instead, its Home Ministry is banning books using the Printing Presses and Publications Act — a law PH once condemned as an authoritarian relic of the Mahathir era. Saifuddin’s promise to “personally review” future bans is not a reform; it’s an admission that his own ministry operates without basic oversight. The ban-then-reverse cycle is peak government incompetence: act first, think later, then frame the reversal as magnanimity. ARTICLE 19 didn’t just ask for the bans to be reversed — they called for the entire Act to be repealed. PH promised that too. Still waiting.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a long Malaysian tradition of book banning that PH was supposed to end. In 2023, the Madani government banned Allah, Liberty and Love by Irshad Manji. Numerous memoirs by former communist-era figures — Chin Peng, Suriani, Abdullah CD, Rashid Maidin — have been available for years without incident. The Printing Presses and Publications Act has been the government’s censorship tool of choice since 1984, and PH’s 2018 manifesto explicitly called for its reform. Four years into power across two stints, the law remains untouched and actively used.

🎯 PH Callout#

You campaigned on “Malaysia Madani” — a civilised, progressive Malaysia. You promised press freedom, institutional reform, and an end to authoritarian-era laws. Instead, your Home Ministry is banning feminist memoirs and academic texts funded by your own state government, then scrambling to undo the damage when the backlash hits. Saifuddin, reviewing bans “personally” is not a system — it’s a personality cult dressed up as governance. Repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act. That was your promise. Deliver it or stop pretending you’re any different from the governments you replaced.
