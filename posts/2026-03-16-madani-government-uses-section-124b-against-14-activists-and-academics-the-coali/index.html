Madani Government Uses Section 124B Against 14 Activists and Academics — The Coalition That Promised Reform Now Wields BN’s Favourite Weapon

2026-03-16
#Politics 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister (Government Spokesperson)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-16

📌 What Happened#

The Royal Malaysia Police have summoned 14 activists, academics, and political researchers for questioning under Section 124B of the Penal Code — a provision carrying up to 20 years in prison — as part of an investigation into an alleged plot to “topple the government and sabotage national stability.” Among those questioned was C4 Centre CEO Pushpan Murugiah, grilled for 90 minutes on March 10. On March 16, a coalition of civil society groups issued a joint statement condemning the Madani government, asking: “Do we want to return to a time when the government cannot be criticised?” Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil, the government’s spokesperson, set the tone on February 27 when he called the alleged plot “a very serious matter” and said the government would “leave it to the police.” The government has made no public statement to narrow the scope of the investigation or address concerns about civil liberties since the probe widened to include civil society figures.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is no longer about an alleged plot. When police summon anti-corruption researchers, political commentators, and university academics under a law designed for threats to parliamentary democracy, the investigation itself becomes the threat. Section 124B was introduced under BN — the same regime PH spent two decades fighting. PH rode to power in 2018 on the backs of exactly these civil society groups. Now, under Anwar’s Madani government, the same law is being turned on the same people who marched, organised, and risked arrest to put this coalition in power. The message from Putrajaya is clear: criticism of the government is an “activity detrimental to parliamentary democracy.” That is not reform. That is regression.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Fahmi called the alleged plot “very serious” and washed his hands — “we will leave it to the police.” That is the same line every BN Communications Minister used when the machinery went after critics. The Madani government has a supermajority. It has the power to direct the Attorney General’s Chambers to review the scope of the investigation. It has the power to publicly state that summoning academics and NGO leaders under Section 124B is disproportionate. It has done none of these things. Instead, it has let the police sweep up 14 people — including people whose entire careers have been spent fighting for the transparency PH once promised. The silence is the statement.

📜 Previous Cases#

Under Najib, repressive security laws were wielded against the very civil society figures who now find themselves targeted again. Bersih chair Maria Chin Abdullah was detained for 10 days under SOSMA during the Bersih 5 rally in 2016. PH, then in opposition, condemned the detention as authoritarian overreach. Anwar himself, speaking from prison, called it evidence of a government that feared its own people. In 2018, PH promised to repeal or amend repressive laws including SOSMA and the Sedition Act. By 2024, none had been repealed. Now, in 2026, the same coalition is using Section 124B — a provision that criminalises vaguely defined “activities detrimental to parliamentary democracy” — against the same civil society ecosystem that once marched for them.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told us you were different. You told us Madani meant “civilised.” You told us the days of using criminal law to silence critics were over. Now your police are summoning anti-corruption researchers under a law that carries 20 years in prison — and your Communications Minister’s response is to call it “very serious” and walk away. Fourteen people. Activists, academics, political researchers. The people who built the moral case for your government. If this is Madani, it is indistinguishable from the thing it replaced. Either rein in the investigation, publicly limit the scope of Section 124B, and protect civic space — or stop pretending you ever cared about reform.
