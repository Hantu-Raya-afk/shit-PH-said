Kulasegaran Shows Up at Court for Activist Being Charged — The Deputy Law Reform Minister Who Can’t Read the Room

2026-03-17
#Politics 

Said by: M. Kulasegaran, Deputy Minister in the Prime Minister’s Department (Law and Institutional Reforms)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-17

📌 What Happened#

On March 17, 2026, Deputy Minister M. Kulasegaran — who holds the Law and Institutional Reforms portfolio in the Prime Minister’s Department — was spotted at the Jawi Magistrate’s Court in Penang accompanying activist Arun Dorasamy (real name Arumugam Dorasamy), who was being charged under Section 505(b) of the Penal Code for allegedly making statements conducive to public mischief. Kulasegaran, the DAP MP for Ipoh Barat, later claimed he had been in Penang for a meeting and merely “stopped by the court briefly” to tell Arun to “trust the legal process.” He insisted he was not inside the courtroom when the charges were read.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is the deputy minister responsible for law and institutional reforms — the person whose entire portfolio exists to strengthen public confidence in the rule of law and the independence of legal proceedings. His presence at court, regardless of how “brief” he claims it was, created the immediate perception that the executive was signalling support for an accused person. This wasn’t lost on anyone: Johor PKR Youth chief Faezuddin Puad publicly demanded an explanation, warning the move risks “damaging the image” of the Madani government. PAS information chief Ahmad Fadhli Shaari asked pointedly how Kulasegaran had “so much free time.” Even the defence lawyer for a co-accused in a related case used Kulasegaran’s presence to claim perceived bias from the authorities. When your own coalition partners are calling you out, you have a governance problem.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Kulasegaran’s excuse is laughable. He was “on his way back” from a meeting in Penang and just happened to “stop by” a magistrate’s court on the morning an activist he has longstanding ties to was being charged — to give him a pep talk about trusting the legal process. That’s not how any of this works. A deputy minister responsible for institutional reforms does not casually swing by courthouses where politically sensitive cases are being heard. The optics are the substance here: a member of the executive, from the portfolio that oversees judicial independence, physically accompanying someone being prosecuted by the state. His statement that “all parties are equal before the law” is rendered absurd by the fact that not all parties have a deputy minister escorting them to their court date.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is a pattern in PH governance: ministers and deputy ministers treating institutional boundaries as suggestions. When Anwar’s government expanded the Section 124B investigation to summon 14 activists and academics just days earlier, it was Fahmi Fadzil who waved it through as “a very serious matter” while civil society groups asked whether Malaysia was returning to an era where government cannot be criticised. Before that, PH spent years in opposition attacking BN for political interference in prosecutions and judicial proceedings. The Indira Gandhi case — which Kulasegaran himself cited as his connection to Arun — was a landmark example of institutional failure that PH promised to fix. Now PH’s own deputy law minister is creating the appearance of executive interference in the very system he’s supposed to be reforming.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised institutional reform. You promised separation of powers. You promised that the days of political interference in the justice system were over. And now your deputy minister for law reform — the person literally tasked with making the system work — is showing up at courthouses to hold hands with people being charged. If Kulasegaran cannot understand why a deputy law minister escorting an accused person to court undermines the institutional reforms he’s supposed to champion, he has no business holding the portfolio. Discipline your own people, PH, or stop pretending the “Madani” brand means anything more than a slogan on a letterhead.
