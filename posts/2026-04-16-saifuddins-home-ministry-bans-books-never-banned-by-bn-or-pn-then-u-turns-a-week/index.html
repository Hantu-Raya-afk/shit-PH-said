Saifuddin’s Home Ministry Bans Books Never Banned by BN or PN — Then U-Turns a Week Later After PH’s Own MPs Revolt

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister & PH Secretary-General (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail’s ministry banned two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — declaring them a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” The memoir of Shamsiah Fakeh, one of Malaysia’s most prominent feminist and anti-colonial leaders, was first published in 2004 by Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia and had been reprinted three times. Shamsiah’s granddaughter, Jamaliah Jamaluddin, is a DAP assemblyman and Anwar ally. Within days, Saifuddin announced he was revoking the ban on these two books plus a third — a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government. As of March 2026, the MADANI government had already banned 27 books — more than all 24 books banned in 2025.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a reformist government using the Printing Presses and Publications Act (PPPA) — the same colonial-era censorship law that Anwar himself suffered under — to ban books that previous BN and Perikatan Nasional administrations never touched. PKR MP Lee Chean Chung publicly pointed out this irony. The ban-then-reverse pattern reveals an administration that acts on authoritarian reflex, then retreats only when its own coalition members revolt. It signals a government increasingly willing to tilt right on cultural issues to court Malay-Muslim voters, even at the cost of its core reformist identity. International rights group ARTICLE 19 condemned the action as arbitrary censorship incompatible with human rights standards.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar Ibrahim spent years in prison as a political detainee. He built his political brand on Reformasi, free expression, and the promise of a Malaysia where ideas are debated, not banned. Now his own Home Minister — a PKR man, the PH secretary-general no less — bans a feminist anti-colonial memoir that survived Najib’s administration and Muhyiddin’s Sheraton Move government, and calls it a “national security threat.” The ministry’s statement that this was not about “restricting intellectual freedom” is laughable when publisher Gerakbudaya is under investigation and faces potential imprisonment under the PPPA. The fact that the government reversed course within a week doesn’t make it better — it means the ban was never legally or intellectually defensible in the first place, and it took PH’s own backbenchers to tell the Home Ministry what Anwar should have known from day one.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is part of an accelerating pattern. Malaysia banned 27 books by March 2026, outpacing the 24 banned in all of 2025, which itself was already the most in any single year since 2017. Previous bans under the MADANI government have targeted LGBTQ+ themed novels, romance fiction, poetry, and even pre-teen puberty guides. The PPPA — which gives the Home Minister “absolute discretion” to ban any publication — was a tool PH once promised to repeal. Instead, Saifuddin has wielded it more aggressively than his BN predecessors. In 2018, during PH’s first stint in government, Anwar’s allies promised media freedom reforms that never materialised before the Sheraton Move. History repeats.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Reformasi. You promised Malaysia Madani. You promised a government that trusts its citizens to think. Instead, you banned a book written by a woman who fought British colonialism — a book your own coalition partner’s assemblyman calls a family memoir — and you called it a national security threat. Even Najib’s government didn’t do that. Even Muhyiddin’s government didn’t do that. When your own MPs have to publicly shame your Home Minister into a U-turn, the problem isn’t the books. The problem is that you’ve become exactly what you said you’d replace.
