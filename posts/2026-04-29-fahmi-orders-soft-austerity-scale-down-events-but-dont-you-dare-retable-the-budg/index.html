Fahmi Orders ‘Soft Austerity’ — Scale Down Events, But Don’t You Dare Retable the Budget

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister (Government Spokesperson)

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil — speaking as government spokesman after a National Economic Action Council (MTEN) meeting — announced that all ministries, agencies, statutory bodies, GLCs, and GLICs must scale down official events and trim operating expenditure. The Finance Ministry has directed all ministries to propose budget cuts by May 15. However, Fahmi explicitly stated that Budget 2026 will not be retabled in Parliament, insisting the adjustments involve “optimising existing spending” without changing the overall framework.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Malaysia’s fuel subsidy bill has exploded from roughly RM700 million per month before the US-Iran conflict to approximately RM7 billion per month — a tenfold increase. RON95 subsidies alone went from RM300 million to RM4 billion monthly; diesel from RM400 million to RM3 billion. This is a fiscal emergency by any reasonable definition. Yet the government’s response is to trim workshop budgets and tell ministries to hold smaller kenduri. The Malaysian Reserve rightly called it “soft austerity” — because calling it what it is, fiscal paralysis, would require admitting the budget is already broken.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The government is spending RM7 billion a month on subsidies it didn’t budget for, running a fiscal deficit that’s widening by the week, and its official position is that Budget 2026 is fine as-is. Scaling down government events is theatre — it’s the fiscal equivalent of turning off the lights in one room while the house is on fire. If fuel subsidies have increased tenfold, the budget is structurally obsolete. Refusing to retable it isn’t fiscal discipline; it’s denial dressed up as stability. The real reason they won’t retable: doing so would force Parliament to confront the true cost of this crisis, and that’s a political conversation PH doesn’t want before the next election.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is the same playbook PH used in March 2026 when Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah ruled out a stimulus package and told Malaysians to “reduce energy usage.” The pattern is consistent: acknowledge the crisis exists, announce symbolic belt-tightening, but avoid any structural fiscal response that might invite political scrutiny. Malaysia’s institutional memory on this is rich — the 1997 Asian Financial Crisis, the 2008 global recession, and even the COVID-19 pandemic all triggered supplementary budgets or emergency fiscal measures. This government’s response to a RM7 billion monthly subsidy blowout is to ask agencies to hold smaller meetings.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a government of transparency, accountability, and competence. A budget built on $70 oil is now running on $30 oil reality, and your response is to downsize government events. If any previous administration tried to paper over a tenfold increase in monthly subsidies by telling agencies to “be moderate,” PH would have called it delusional. You know the budget is broken. Retable it, face the Parliament you said you’d empower, and tell Malaysians the truth about what this crisis costs.
