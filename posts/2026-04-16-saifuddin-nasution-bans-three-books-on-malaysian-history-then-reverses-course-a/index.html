Saifuddin Nasution Bans Three Books on Malaysian History, Then Reverses Course a Week Later — Reform Government Can’t Even Commit to Its Own Censorship

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 15-16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry under Saifuddin Nasution Ismail issued prohibition orders on two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — published by independent bookstore Gerak Budaya, claiming they contained “communist elements and ideologies” that endangered national security. Days later, on April 22, the ministry banned a third book, a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong that had been funded by the Selangor state government — led by Anwar’s own PKR. By April 23, facing fierce backlash from civil society, international press freedom groups, and even PH’s own coalition members, Saifuddin announced the government was in the process of revoking all three bans and would personally review future prohibitions.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a government that campaigned on reform, press freedom, and ending the authoritarian instincts of its predecessors — and within a single week, it banned three books about historical figures and academic subjects, then reversed itself after being shamed into it by its own allies. The episode crystallises PH’s rightward drift under pressure: rather than repeal the colonial-era Printing Presses and Publications Act (PPPA) as promised, Anwar’s government is actively using it to suppress historical scholarship. Malaysia’s press freedom ranking has barely budged, and book bans in 2026 have already surpassed the total for 2025. The reform mandate is being spent on performative conservatism, not institutional change.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The books in question are memoirs and academic studies about historical figures from Malaysia’s anti-colonial struggle — not recruitment pamphlets for a dead insurgency. The Communist Party of Malaya dissolved in 1989. Banning books about it in 2026 is not national security — it is intellectual cowardice dressed up as vigilance. Worse, one of the banned books is a memoir of Shamsiah Fakeh, whose granddaughter is an ally of Anwar himself. The ministry banned books linked to the PM’s own political network, panicked when the backlash came, and then tried to repackage the reversal as “reform” by promising a new consultation process. If you need a week and a public shaming to realise banning history books is a bad idea, you are not a reformist — you are an authoritarian with better PR.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long and inglorious history of book bans under the PPPA, a law inherited from British colonial rule. Under Najib Razak’s government, books critical of 1MDB and the ruling party were routinely prohibited. PH promised in its 2022 election manifesto to review and reform media laws including the PPPA. Instead, 27 books were banned as of March 2026 — already exceeding the 24 books banned in all of 2025. In October 2025, DW reported that more books had been banned under Anwar’s government than in the previous six years combined. The pattern is clear: PH talks reform in opposition and reaches for the same censorship tools in government.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians a Madani government — compassionate, reformist, modern. Instead, your Home Ministry is banning books about grandmothers who fought the British, then scrambling to undo the bans when the internet notices. You still have not repealed the PPPA. You still have not passed a freedom of information act. You are three years into government and your idea of reform is promising to “personally review” the next book ban rather than abolishing the power to ban books at all. If you cannot trust Malaysians to read their own history, what exactly is “Madani” about your Malaysia?
