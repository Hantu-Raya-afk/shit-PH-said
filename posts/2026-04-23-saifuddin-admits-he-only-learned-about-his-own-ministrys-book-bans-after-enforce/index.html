Saifuddin Admits He Only Learned About His Own Ministry’s Book Bans After Enforcement — His Fix? He’ll Read the Reports Himself Now

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry issued a prohibition order banning two books — Shamsiah Fakeh’s memoir Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10) — published by independent bookstore Gerak Budaya, declaring them a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” Days later, a third ban hit a Malay-language translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong, funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government. Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail then announced on April 23 that his ministry would reverse all three bans, admitting he only found out about the prohibitions after enforcement had already taken place — and that the previous process had forced him to “firefight.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

This isn’t a minor procedural glitch. The Home Minister of a self-declared reformist government did not know his own ministry was banning books. For over two years under PH, unelected civil servants and Special Branch officers were exercising colonial-era powers to suppress publications — and the minister responsible was completely out of the loop. The fact that one banned book was written by the grandmother of a DAP assemblyman, an Anwar ally, makes the incompetence even more embarrassing. Civil society groups, PH’s own coalition members, and analysts — including in a major South China Morning Post analysis published May 2 — have questioned what this says about Anwar’s reform credentials.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Saifuddin’s “fix” is laughable. His solution is that he will now personally scrutinise Special Branch reports before approving bans. That’s it. No proposal to repeal or amend the Printing Presses and Publication Act — a colonial-era law that gives the minister “absolute discretion” to ban any publication with no judicial oversight and no requirement to give reasons. PH campaigned on institutional reform. Two and a half years later, the same colonial toolkit is intact, and the best the Home Minister can offer is “I’ll read the reports myself from now on.” That’s not reform. That’s one man promising to be a better gatekeeper of an authoritarian power that should not exist in a democracy.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s book-banning habit is well-documented. By March 2026, the government had already banned 27 books under Anwar’s watch — five in 2023 alone. Previous bans targeted everything from books by the moderate Muslim group G25 to academic works on Malaysian history. The Printing Presses and Publication Act, inherited from British colonial rule and weaponised by successive BN governments, was one of the laws PH promised to reform when they first came to power in 2018. Eight years later, the law remains untouched. The pattern is clear: every Malaysian government, regardless of rhetoric, finds the power to silence inconvenient ideas too useful to give up.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia reformasi. You told voters you’d dismantle the authoritarian tools of the old regime. Instead, your Home Minister didn’t even know his own ministry was banning books until the media found out. Your “solution” keeps the same colonial law on the books and replaces institutional safeguards with one minister’s personal promise to pay attention. Here’s a question for PH: if you can’t even reform a book-banning law after two and a half years in power, what exactly is your reform agenda? Because right now, it looks like the deep state runs the Home Ministry and the minister shows up to apologise afterward.
