Ramanan Says ‘No Issue’ Giving Bestinet Another Migrant Worker Contract — His Own PKR MPs Demand an Investigation

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: R. Ramanan, Human Resources Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, Human Resources Minister R. Ramanan told The Star that his ministry sees “no issue” with Bestinet Sdn Bhd — the company founded by tycoon Aminul Islam — developing a new foreign worker recruitment system called TURAP (The Universal Recruitment Advanced Platform). This came weeks after Bloomberg published an investigation into endemic corruption in Malaysia’s migrant worker recruitment from Bangladesh, naming both Bestinet and Aminul. Ramanan initially rejected the Bloomberg report as “unverified and inaccurate,” but then told The Star his ministry was open to the system. By April 20, nine PKR backbenchers — including Wong Chen (Subang), Nik Nazmi (Setiawangsa), and Lee Chean Chung (Petaling Jaya) — issued a joint statement demanding an investigation into Ramanan’s ministry over the Bestinet connection.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a minister publicly defending a company that an international investigation has linked to systemic exploitation of migrant workers — and then watching his own party’s MPs publicly demand accountability from him. It exposes a government that talks about “zero migration cost” while keeping the same players at the table. The fact that PKR backbenchers had to go public with their concerns — rather than having them addressed internally — tells you everything about how seriously the Madani government takes migrant worker reform when it conflicts with entrenched business interests.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Ramanan’s position is incoherent. He rejected the Bloomberg report as “unverified,” then told The Star the ministry sees “no issue” with Bestinet running the new system — effectively confirming the report’s core claim while disputing its details. You can’t call something fake news and then endorse the substance of what was reported. Meanwhile, Bestinet has managed Malaysia’s existing Foreign Worker Centralised Management System (FWCMS) for over 15 years, during which migrant worker exploitation has remained endemic. Giving them another contract isn’t reform — it’s renewal.

📜 Previous Cases#

Bestinet’s dominance over Malaysia’s migrant worker pipeline is not new. The company has faced scrutiny for years over allegations of excessive fees, lack of transparency, and connections between its founder and political elites. Former MP Charles Santiago had previously called on MACC to investigate the proposed system. Bloomberg’s January 2026 investigation documented how Bangladeshi workers were charged exorbitant fees to work in Malaysia through a system Bestinet helped manage. Ramanan himself was previously accused by Rafizi Ramli of links to businessman Victor Chin — an allegation Ramanan denied, saying “I don’t know him” — adding to a pattern of questions about the minister’s associations.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised to end the exploitation of migrant workers. You promised institutional reform. You promised to break the stranglehold of entrenched interests over government contracts. Instead, your Human Resources Minister is defending a 15-year incumbent contractor that an international investigation linked to corruption — and it took nine of your own MPs going public to demand basic accountability. If PH cannot discipline one minister over one contract with one controversial company, what exactly is the “Madani” reform agenda for?
