Saifuddin Bans Three Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Reverses After Backlash, Admits He Didn’t Even Know About the Bans

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, the Ministry of Home Affairs issued prohibition orders against two books published by independent publisher Gerak Budaya — “Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10” and “Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah” — for allegedly containing communist ideologies. A third book, a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was also banned. The bans triggered immediate backlash from civil society groups, DAP lawmakers, and even PKR members. By April 23, Home Affairs Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail announced the ministry was in the process of revoking all three bans, admitting that the previous process had forced him to “firefight” because he only learned about the bans after enforcement had already taken place.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a government that promised to defend intellectual freedom and dismantle the authoritarian apparatus of previous administrations — and yet its own Home Affairs Ministry is banning books on autopilot without the minister even knowing. The episode exposes a fundamental governance failure: either Saifuddin has no control over his own ministry, or the ministry’s institutional culture still operates on Mahathir-era reflexes where anything labelled “communist” gets banned without scrutiny. Either way, this is not reform — it’s bureaucratic inertia wearing a reformist mask. That one of the banned books was funded by PKR’s own Selangor government makes the incompetence even more embarrassing.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Saifuddin’s defence — that he didn’t know about the bans until after enforcement — is not the exoneration he thinks it is. A Home Affairs Minister who doesn’t know his own ministry is banning books is either asleep at the wheel or deliberately maintaining plausible deniability. The “new mechanism” he announced — where he will personally review bans before enforcement — should have been in place from Day One of a supposedly reformist government. PH has been in power since November 2022. It took them three and a half years and a public humiliation to fix a process that any competent minister would have overhauled in the first week. The reversal isn’t accountability; it’s damage control.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long, ugly history of book bans under the Printing Presses and Publications Act. Gerak Budaya alone has had multiple titles banned over the years — books on May 13, reformasi, corruption, and sexuality. PH campaigned explicitly against this kind of censorship. In opposition, DAP and PKR lawmakers routinely condemned book bans as instruments of authoritarian control. Yet here they are, three years into government, banning a memoir of a historical figure whose granddaughter is a DAP assemblyman’s relative. The Sedition Act, which PH promised to repeal, remains on the books. The PPPA, which enables these bans, remains unreformed. The pattern is clear: PH inherits authoritarian tools and, instead of dismantling them, keeps them oiled and ready.

🎯 PH Callout#

PH, you promised Malaysians a new Malaysia — one where books aren’t banned, where intellectual freedom is protected, and where the Home Ministry doesn’t operate like a Cold War relic. Instead, your own ministry banned a book funded by your own state government, your minister didn’t even know about it, and you only reversed course after the internet dragged you for a week. Your “new mechanism” — the minister will personally review bans — is not reform. It’s one person standing between Malaysians and censorship, and that person admitted he wasn’t even paying attention. If you can’t control your own ministry after three years, what exactly are you reforming?
