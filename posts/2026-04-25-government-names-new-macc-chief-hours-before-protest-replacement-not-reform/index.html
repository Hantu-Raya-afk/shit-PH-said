Government Names New MACC Chief Hours Before Protest — Replacement, Not Reform

2026-04-25
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister / Shamsul Azri Abu Bakar, Chief Secretary to the Government

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-25

📌 What Happened#

On the morning of April 25, Chief Secretary to the Government Shamsul Azri Abu Bakar announced that former High Court judge Abdul Halim Aman would replace MACC chief Azam Baki effective May 13 — the day after Azam’s one-year contract expires. The announcement was timed precisely: just hours before the “Tangkap Azam Baki” rally organised by Bersih and civil society groups. Thousands turned out in downtown Kuala Lumpur that evening, despite heavy rain, demanding not just Azam’s removal but his arrest and a Royal Commission of Inquiry (RCI) into MACC misconduct. Reports of abuse within the agency first emerged in February 2026. For three months, the government refused to suspend Azam, rejected calls for an RCI, and let the clock run down on his contract.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The government’s handling of the MACC crisis is a masterclass in managed inaction. Rather than suspending Azam when the allegations surfaced in February — the standard in any functioning accountability system — PH waited three months, let his contract expire, and framed the replacement as reform. The King himself had to publicly state he would select the next chief, an extraordinary intervention that underscored how badly the government had mishandled the situation. Bersih’s chairman said it plainly: the protest was never just about one man; it was about structural reform the government has spent three years avoiding.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH built its brand on anti-corruption. “Reformasi” was the rallying cry. Anwar spent years in opposition demanding accountability from MACC under BN. Now, when his own MACC chief faces allegations of running a “corporate mafia” operation — pressuring businesses into deals, officers breaking service rules — the government’s response was to let the contract expire and call it a day. No suspension. No investigation announcement. No RCI. The timing of the replacement announcement — on the morning of a major protest — was not reform; it was damage control. If BN had done exactly this, PH would have called it a cover-up.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has been here before. In 2022, before PH came to power, Azam Baki was already embroiled in a share ownership scandal, and civil society demanded his removal. PH, then in opposition, was vocal in calling for accountability. Now in government, the script flipped entirely. The broader pattern extends to PH’s handling of other institutional watchdogs — from the delayed reform of the police complaints commission (IPCMC, now IPCC) to the glacial pace of judicial and electoral reforms promised in their GE15 manifesto. The message is consistent: PH will reform institutions right up until reform becomes politically inconvenient.

🎯 PH Callout#

You marched for reform. You campaigned on MACC independence. You promised Malaysians that the age of untouchable agency chiefs was over. Then you let Azam Baki serve out his full contract despite months of explosive allegations, refused an RCI, and announced his replacement just in time to take the wind out of a street protest. That’s not reform — that’s reputation management. If you believe MACC officers were involved in a “corporate mafia,” prove it: open an RCI, let the investigation run publicly, and show Malaysians that reformasi isn’t just a slogan you retired after winning Putrajaya.
