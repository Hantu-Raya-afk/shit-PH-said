Anwar Praises Azam Baki as Having ‘Performed Duties Well’ — After 3 Contract Extensions, Corporate Mafia Exposés, and Street Protests Demanding His Arrest

2026-04-25
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-25

📌 What Happened#

On April 25, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim publicly praised outgoing Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission (MACC) chief Azam Baki, saying he had “performed duties well” and that it was simply “time for new MACC leadership.” This came on the same day hundreds of Malaysians gathered in Kuala Lumpur for an “Arrest Azam Baki” rally, demanding not just a leadership change but systemic reforms to the anti-graft agency. Anwar announced former High Court judge Halim Aman as Azam’s replacement effective May 13, describing him as “suited” for the role — while conspicuously refusing to address the mountain of unresolved allegations against the man he was replacing.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is the culmination of a months-long pattern where the self-proclaimed “reformist” Prime Minister shielded the country’s top graft buster from accountability. Bloomberg’s February investigation exposed allegations that the MACC under Azam had colluded with businessmen to manipulate corporate decisions — the so-called “corporate mafia” scandal. Despite calls from civil society, opposition leaders, and even members of Anwar’s own coalition (including DAP and PKR lawmakers) for Azam’s suspension and a Royal Commission of Inquiry, the PM stonewalled. It took the King himself publicly announcing on April 23 that he would personally choose the next MACC chief before the government finally acted. For a coalition that rode to power on anti-corruption credibility, praising Azam on his way out is a masterclass in institutional betrayal.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar extended Azam’s contract three times — in May 2023, May 2024, and May 2025 — despite the man having already reached retirement age of 60 in 2023, and despite earlier shareholding controversies dating back to 2021. Pakatan Harapan campaigned at the 2022 General Election specifically demanding that Parliament vet the MACC chief’s appointment. That promise was never implemented. When Bloomberg’s corporate mafia investigation dropped in February, Anwar’s response was not to launch an inquiry but to call the allegations a “Zionist conspiracy” and a threat to national security. DAP Transport Minister Anthony Loke himself admitted the government’s response was “inadequate and does not meet public expectations.” And yet Anwar still sent Azam off with a pat on the back. You don’t get to be the anti-corruption coalition and give a thank-you card to the man accused of running the graft agency like a shakedown operation.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a depressingly familiar pattern in Malaysian politics: the PM protects institutional appointees until public pressure makes their position untenable, then replaces them quietly while praising their service. Najib Razak did the same with his attorney-general — replacing Gani Patail with Apandi Ali when 1MDB investigations got too close. Anwar’s three extensions of Azam mirror the patronage logic PH once railed against. The DAP, now forced to bring forward its national congress to July to decide whether reforms have moved fast enough, lost every seat it contested in Sabah’s November 2025 state polls partly because of public disgust over selective prosecution in a Sabah mining graft scandal. The coalition’s anti-corruption brand is in ruins, and Anwar’s farewell praise for Azam is the obituary.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia a new standard of governance. You promised Parliament would vet the MACC chief. You promised accountability. Instead, you gave Azam Baki three contract extensions, called investigations into his conduct a Zionist plot, refused to release the findings of your own probe, and praised him as he walked out the door with his reputation — and possibly his freedom — intact. The King had to step in to assert his prerogative over an appointment you should have handled a year ago. “Malaysia Madani” does not mean shielding your graft buster from scrutiny and then thanking him for his service. The rakyat who stood in the rain chanting “bangkit rakyat” on April 25 did not do so because they were satisfied with the pace of reform. They did so because you failed them.
