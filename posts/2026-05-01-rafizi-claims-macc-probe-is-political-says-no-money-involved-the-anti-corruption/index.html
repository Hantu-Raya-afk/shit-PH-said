Rafizi Claims MACC Probe Is Political, Says ‘No Money Involved’ — The Anti-Corruption Crusader Who Suddenly Sounds Like Every Politician He Ever Exposed

2026-05-01
#Politics 

Said by: Rafizi Ramli, Former Economy Minister (PKR, Pandan MP)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

On May 1, 2026, former Economy Minister Datuk Seri Rafizi Ramli revealed he had been summoned by the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission (MACC) to give a statement at its Putrajaya headquarters on May 4. The summons relates to an ongoing investigation into the government’s RM1.1 billion joint venture with UK-based semiconductor firm Arm Holdings, signed during Rafizi’s tenure. The probe, initiated after a complaint by NGO Perkasa, centres on allegations that Rafizi pressured the Cabinet to expedite approval of the deal. In his response posted on X, Rafizi said he expects to be charged with breach of trust, but insisted “there is no monetary motive — no money is involved,” and questioned why Perkasa — an NGO with no track record on corruption issues — suddenly filed the report.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a former PH cabinet minister being summoned by the very anti-corruption agency that PH promised to empower and make independent. The RM1.1 billion Arm Holdings deal was supposed to be a flagship achievement of Malaysia’s semiconductor ambitions — 10,000 engineers trained, seven high-end chip design blueprints acquired. Instead, it’s now the subject of a Section 23 MACC Act investigation. The optics are devastating: the man who once built his political career by exposing the National Feedlot Corporation (NFC) scandal is now on the other side of the interrogation table, and the MACC that Anwar promised to reform is the one doing the asking.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Rafizi’s defence reads like a greatest hits album of every politician he ever exposed. “No money involved” — the same dismissive framing BN politicians used before forensic accounting proved otherwise. “It’s politically motivated” — the same deflection Najib tried with 1MDB. “Perkasa has no track record on corruption” — irrelevant, because the MACC doesn’t investigate based on who files the report but on whether evidence warrants scrutiny. Rafizi spent a decade demanding that politicians face accountability regardless of their explanations. Now that the spotlight is on him, he wants special treatment. If the deal was clean, welcome the investigation. If it wasn’t, then PH has its own procurement scandal to answer for.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a depressingly familiar Malaysian pattern: reformist politicians who demand transparency in opposition but cry conspiracy when investigated in government. Lim Guan Eng faced corruption charges over the Penang undersea tunnel project and insisted they were political. Anwar himself framed multiple investigations against him as politically motivated. The NFC scandal that Rafizi exposed in 2012 saw Shahrizat Jalil’s family claim the same “political vendetta” defence. The script never changes — only the actors rotate. PH promised to break this cycle. Instead, they’re speed-running it.

🎯 PH Callout#

You ran on reformasi. You promised a Malaysia where no one is above the law, where MACC operates independently, where procurement is transparent and accountable. Now your former Economy Minister is summoned over a billion-ringgit deal and his first instinct is to claim victimhood and cry conspiracy — the exact behaviour you spent two decades condemning. If this probe is baseless, let the process prove it. If it’s not, then own it. You don’t get to champion accountability only when it targets your enemies. That’s not reform. That’s just taking turns.
