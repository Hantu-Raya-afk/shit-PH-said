Saifuddin Bans Books Then Backtracks in Days — Home Minister Admits He Didn’t Even Know About the Bans Until After Enforcement

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry — under Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — banned two books: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Regimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi Regimen 10: Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah, declaring them a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” One of the books was a memoir of the grandmother of an ally of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. A third book — a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong, funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government — was also banned. Days later, Saifuddin admitted he was in the process of revoking the bans, and confessed he had been “firefighting” — learning about the prohibitions only after enforcement had already happened.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is Anwar Ibrahim’s reformist credentials collapsing in real time. The man who lectured at Georgetown, Oxford, and St Andrews — who built his political brand on intellectual openness and democratic reform — now presides over a government that has banned 27 books as of March 2026, already surpassing the 24 books banned in all of 2025. As the SCMP reported on May 2, Anwar’s government has “tilted to the right” to win Malay voter support, and the book bans are a symptom of a deeper rot: a reform agenda sacrificed at the altar of political survival. Even Anwar’s own PKR MP, Tan Kar Hing (Gopeng), publicly reminded the PM of his university lecturing days — a pointed rebuke from within the family.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A Home Affairs Minister who doesn’t know books are being banned under his own ministry is either incompetent or lying. Saifuddin’s claim that he was “firefighting” — discovering bans post-enforcement — is an admission that his ministry operates on autopilot, rubber-stamping Special Branch recommendations without ministerial oversight. And the reversal days later doesn’t earn credit; it proves the ban was indefensible from the start. If PH can’t even manage a consistent position on whether Malaysians should be allowed to read historical memoirs, what exactly is the Madani reform project for? International press freedom organization Article 19 condemned the ban and called for the repeal of the Printing Presses and Publications Act — the same colonial-era law PH once promised to reform.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not an isolated incident. Under Anwar’s Madani government, book banning has accelerated sharply — from 5 titles in 2023 to 3 in 2022 to 27 already by March 2026. In 2023, the government banned three books including LGBTQ-themed titles, with the Home Ministry calling them a “threat to moral values.” The pattern is clear: PH inherited the BN-era censorship apparatus and, rather than dismantling it as promised, has expanded its use. The Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 — which PH once called a tool of authoritarian control — remains unrepealed and is now wielded by the same coalition that campaigned on press freedom.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised a Malaysia where ideas would be debated, not banned. You promised to repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act. Instead, your Home Minister doesn’t even know what his own ministry is banning, your government is censoring historical memoirs funded by your own state governments, and your book-banning rate has outpaced every BN-era year in recent memory. Anwar Ibrahim once stood in the world’s finest lecture halls and spoke about the power of ideas. Now his government bans books about grandmothers who fought the British. If this is Madani, it’s a Madani that would make Mahathir proud.
