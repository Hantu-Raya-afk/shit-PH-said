Anwar’s Own Reform Bill Fails in Parliament — His Supermajority Couldn’t Muster Two Extra Votes

2026-03-02
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-02

📌 What Happened#

On March 2, 2026, the Dewan Rakyat voted on the Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026, which would have limited the Prime Minister’s tenure to a maximum of 10 years — two terms. The bill was a flagship reform promise from Pakatan Harapan’s 2022 manifesto, explicitly framed as a safeguard against another Mahathir-style 22-year grip on power. It failed. The bill received 146 votes in favour — two short of the 148 needed for a two-thirds constitutional majority. Dewan Rakyat Speaker Tan Sri Johari Abdul announced that 32 MPs were absent and 44 abstained. Not a single MP voted against it. They simply didn’t bother.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Anwar Ibrahim was widely believed to command the support of 154 MPs — comfortably above the two-thirds threshold. This had never been formally tested until now, and the test was a spectacular failure. The bill’s defeat is not just a policy loss — it exposes the fragility of the unity government’s supposed supermajority. Within 24 hours, Bloomberg reported that the government quietly delayed a second reform bill: the separation of the Attorney General and Public Prosecutor roles. The signal is unmistakable — PH cannot deliver institutional reform even when it writes the bills and controls the House. Coalition discipline is a mirage.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is the government that ran on reformasi. This is the coalition that said it would enshrine democratic safeguards into the Constitution. And when it came time to vote on the most basic of those safeguards — limiting how long one person can hold the most powerful office in the country — its own MPs couldn’t be bothered to show up or raise their hands. 44 abstentions. In a unity government with a supposed supermajority. The cameras caught Anwar mid-laugh, chatting with his deputy, as the numbers were read out. If your own coalition can’t pass your own reform bill in your own parliament, you are not a reform government. You are a government of slogans.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s reform promises have a long history of dying in Parliament. The IPCMC bill to establish an independent police complaints commission was first proposed in 2005 and never passed. The promised repeal of the Sedition Act — a PH manifesto staple since the first Pakatan Rakyat era — remains unfulfilled. UNDI18, which lowered the voting age to 18, was passed in 2019 but only implemented in 2022 after prolonged delays. The pattern is consistent: PH promises democratic reform in opposition, then discovers in government that reform is inconvenient, expensive, or requires discipline it does not have.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised the people of Malaysia that you would be different. You said you would cap prime ministerial power because you saw what unchecked power did under Mahathir. You wrote it in your manifesto. You drafted the bill. You controlled the House. And you still couldn’t get it done — not because the opposition blocked it, but because your own people couldn’t be bothered to vote. If you cannot deliver a two-thirds majority for your flagship reform when you hold more than 154 seats, what exactly is the point of your supermajority? The next time you tell voters that you need a strong mandate to deliver change, remember this: you had the mandate, and you wasted it.
