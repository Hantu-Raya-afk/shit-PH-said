Government Trumpets RM1.9 Million WFH Fuel Savings in 10 Days — While Bleeding RM4 Billion a Month on Subsidies

2026-04-30
#Economy 

Said by: Wan Ahmad Dahlan Abdul Aziz, Director-General of Public Service

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-30

📌 What Happened#

On April 30, 2026, Director-General of Public Service Tan Sri Wan Ahmad Dahlan Abdul Aziz proudly announced that the government’s work-from-home (WFH) initiative — introduced on April 15 as an emergency response to soaring fuel costs from the Middle East conflict — had saved RM1.9 million in RON95 petrol subsidies over its first 10 days. That works out to 979,632 litres of petrol, or RM239,215.68 per day. Only 20,136 civil servants are currently participating in the scheme, up from 15,452 at launch. The DG described the savings as “a meaningful contribution from civil servants in helping the government manage financial pressures.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim himself disclosed in early April that the government is spending approximately RM4 billion per month on fuel subsidies — a figure that is bleeding the treasury dry and prompted the order for all federal ministries to slash their operating budgets. Against that backdrop, RM1.9 million over 10 days is not a meaningful contribution. It is a rounding error. It is 0.0016% of the monthly subsidy bill. CNA analysts had already warned that the WFH policy would have a “very small” impact on fuel savings. Yet here is the Director-General of Public Service presenting it as though the bureaucracy has made a heroic sacrifice. This is the government treating optics as policy — making 20,000 civil servants work from home three days a week so ministers can claim they’re “doing something” about a crisis that requires structural reform, not performative telecommuting.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The math does not lie. RM1.9 million saved versus RM4 billion spent per month. That is like finding 19 sen while your house burns down and calling a press conference about fiscal discipline. The WFH scheme excludes Mondays and Fridays, only covers tasks that don’t require physical presence, and involves barely 20,000 out of Malaysia’s 1.7 million civil servants. Meanwhile, the treasury has ordered RM5.4 billion in cuts to health and higher education — real, painful cuts that will affect hospitals and universities. But instead of confronting the structural impossibility of maintaining fuel subsidies indefinitely, the government is waving around a savings figure that wouldn’t cover the catering bill for a mid-sized government conference. The DG even acknowledged the figure “may not seem large” — a rare moment of accidental honesty in an otherwise absurd press event.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysian governments have a long history of symbolic cost-cutting while ignoring structural waste. During the 2022 campaign, PH promised to rationalise subsidies and target them to the bottom 40%. Instead, blanket fuel subsidies have continued, RON95 remains untouched, and the government is now spending more on fuel subsidies than it did under BN. The WFH-as-savings narrative echoes the Najib-era “potong gaji menteri” (minister salary cut) stunt — a meaningless gesture designed to signal fiscal responsibility while billions flowed elsewhere. The difference is that PH was supposed to be better. They were supposed to be the ones who did the hard structural work instead of dressing up press releases as policy.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysia the fuel crisis was “not business as usual.” You ordered RM5.4 billion in real cuts to health and education. You told civil servants to work from home to save fuel. And then your Director-General held a press conference to celebrate saving RM1.9 million — the equivalent of 12 seconds’ worth of your monthly fuel subsidy bill. If this is your idea of managing a fiscal crisis, then the rakyat should be terrified. Stop treating symbolic gestures as substitutes for structural reform. Either rationalise the subsidy, target it properly, or admit you’re stuck in the same trap as every government before you. But don’t insult the public’s intelligence by calling RM239,000 a day a “meaningful contribution” while hospitals face budget cuts.
