Fahmi Says WFH Decision for Civil Servants Delayed Until After Hari Raya — The Crisis Is Now, But the Plan Can Wait

2026-03-17
#Politics 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-17

📌 What Happened#

On March 17, 2026, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil confirmed that the Cabinet has reviewed an initial report on work-from-home arrangements for public sector employees but will not make a final decision until after Hari Raya Aidilfitri. Speaking after a post-Cabinet meeting in Putrajaya, Fahmi said the matter would be referred to the National Economic Action Council (MTEN) by the Chief Secretary to the Government, citing the need for “proper coordination” and “refinement” before implementation. He stressed frontline services like healthcare and education would not be affected, and noted the private sector is free to implement its own WFH policies.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Malaysia is in the grip of a global oil crisis triggered by the US-Iran conflict in West Asia, with fuel subsidy bills hitting RM3.2 billion per month, the KLCI below 1700, and the Cabinet itself announcing austerity measures. Thailand, Vietnam, and the Philippines have already implemented WFH policies for their public sectors to cut costs. Malaysian employers are publicly warning that the government’s “wait-and-see” approach is hurting MSMEs. Yet the government’s response to its own cost-cutting proposal is to delay it past a public holiday — treating urgency as something that can be scheduled around festive seasons.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The government announced austerity, told the rakyat to “reduce energy usage,” reviewed PdPR for schoolchildren, and then — when it came to actually implementing a concrete cost-saving measure for 1.7 million civil servants — decided it needed more “coordination” and would get back to everyone after the holidays. This is not governance; it’s procrastination dressed up as process. If the crisis is serious enough to tell citizens to cut back and consider sending kids back to Zoom school, it’s serious enough to make a WFH decision before Raya, not after. The coordination excuse rings hollow when the private sector managed to implement WFH during COVID without a national council and a festive-season deadline.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is a recurring pattern in Malaysian governance: announce urgency, then delay action. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the Muhyiddin government took weeks to finalise MCO SOPs while cases surged. The Madani government itself delayed targeted fuel subsidy reform for over two years before partially implementing it in 2024. On the current crisis, Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah already ruled out a stimulus package on March 16, telling Malaysians to “reduce energy usage.” Deputy Education Minister Wong Kah Woh floated PdPR for students within days. But the WFH decision? That joins the queue of things the government will get around to — eventually.

🎯 PH Callout#

You came to power promising a government that acts, not one that forms committees and waits for holidays to end. The oil crisis isn’t taking a break for Raya. Employers are bleeding. Your own ministers floated PdPR for schoolchildren within days. But a WFH policy for adults — something the entire world figured out in 2020 — needs more “refinement”? If “Malaysia Madani” means anything, it means making hard decisions on time, not parking them in MTEN’s inbox until the ketupat is finished. Decide now. Act now. That’s what reformists do.
