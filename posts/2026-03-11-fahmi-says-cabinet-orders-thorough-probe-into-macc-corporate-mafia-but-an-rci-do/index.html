Fahmi Says Cabinet Orders ‘Thorough Probe’ Into MACC Corporate Mafia — But an RCI? Don’t Hold Your Breath

2026-03-11
#Politics 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister and Government Spokesman

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened#

On March 11, Communications Minister and government spokesman Fahmi Fadzil told reporters after a Cabinet meeting that enforcement agencies — including the police, Securities Commission, MACC, and Inland Revenue Board — had been instructed to conduct a “thorough” investigation into allegations that certain MACC officials were involved in corporate takeover manoeuvres. Fahmi confirmed the Cabinet had received the special committee’s report on MACC chief Azam Baki’s shareholding scandal but refused to reveal its findings, saying further action would be taken by Chief Secretary Shamsul Azri Abu Bakar. When asked whether Azam’s contract extension was discussed, Fahmi said: “It was not discussed.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

Both sides of the political divide — including PH’s own coalition partner MCA Youth and DAP allies — had called for a Royal Commission of Inquiry (RCI) to investigate the “corporate mafia” allegations and Azam’s shareholding controversy. Instead, the Cabinet handed the matter to existing enforcement bodies and the Chief Secretary’s office — the very bureaucratic machinery that reports to the Prime Minister. This is not independent oversight; it is the government investigating itself with extra steps. The refusal to disclose the special committee’s findings or commit to an RCI signals that transparency remains a negotiable principle for this administration.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH ran on a platform of institutional reform, anti-corruption, and transparency. When Bloomberg exposed allegations of a “corporate mafia” operating within MACC — the very agency PH relies on to prosecute its political enemies — the government had a chance to demonstrate that its principles apply universally. Instead, Fahmi delivered a masterclass in bureaucratic deflection: “thorough probe” (by agencies already compromised), “further action by the Chief Secretary” (an appointee of the PM), and “I will not disclose the findings” (transparency, Madani-style). The opposition called for an RCI. PH’s own allies called for an RCI. The government said no. The message is clear: accountability is for others.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time PH has shielded Azam Baki. The MACC chief has been embroiled in shareholding controversies since 2021, yet Anwar renewed his contract three times after coming to power. Bloomberg’s February 2026 exposé on MACC’s alleged “corporate mafia” links was followed by a second report detailing Azam’s RM14 million shareholdings. Each time, the government’s response has been the same: form a committee, promise a probe, refuse transparency. When C4, an anti-corruption NGO, pressed for accountability, its CEO was questioned under Section 124B — a sedition-adjacent provision. The pattern is unmistakable: protect the institution, punish the critics.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a governance model built on transparency, institutional integrity, and the rule of law. Bloomberg handed you evidence of alleged corruption within your own anti-corruption agency, and your response was to hide the report, refuse an RCI, and hand the matter to a bureaucrat who answers to the Prime Minister. You called corruption “treason to the nation” three days later at a PKR convention. Either you meant it — in which case, open the books — or you didn’t, in which case stop pretending. The rakyat who voted for reform in 2022 did not vote for a government that investigates itself in private and calls it accountability.
