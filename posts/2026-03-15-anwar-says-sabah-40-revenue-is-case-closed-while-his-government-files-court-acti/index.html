Anwar Says Sabah 40% Revenue Is ‘Case Closed’ — While His Government Files Court Action to Reopen It

2026-03-15
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

At a Madani Iftar event at Universiti Malaysia Sabah on March 15, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told the audience — including Sabah Chief Minister Hajiji Noor — that the federal government’s position on Sabah’s 40 per cent revenue entitlement is ‘final’ and ‘case closed.’ In the same breath, he confirmed that Putrajaya is proceeding to the Court of Appeal — not to challenge the 40 per cent, he insists, but to seek ‘clarification’ on parts of the High Court judgment that he claims unfairly questioned the legacies of Tunku Abdul Rahman, Tun Abdul Razak, and Sabah leaders like Tun Mustapha and Tun Fuad Stephens. A stay of execution application is scheduled for March 31. The Sabah government has indicated it will oppose.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The High Court affirmed Sabah’s constitutional right to 40 per cent of net federal revenue derived from the state for the period 1974–2021 — the so-called ‘Lost Years.’ The court imposed a 180-day deadline for compliance, expiring April 15, 2026. Anwar’s framing — ‘we accept the 40 per cent but reject the judgment’s language’ — is a distinction that exists primarily in press conferences, not in courtrooms. Filing a stay application and proceeding to appeal, regardless of how you frame the purpose, delays compliance. Every day Putrajaya spends in court is a day Sabah doesn’t get paid. This is the pattern: announce the outcome you want the public to hear, then quietly use legal process to buy time.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

‘Case closed’ does not mean ‘see you in court on March 31.’ If the 40 per cent is truly final, there is no reason to file anything. Anwar’s claim that the appeal is only about defending the ‘credibility of past leaders’ is a rhetorical sleight of hand — courts don’t issue clarifications on historical reputations; they rule on legal obligations. The stay application, if granted, would freeze the 180-day compliance deadline, giving Putrajaya exactly what it says it doesn’t want: more time to not pay Sabah. The Sabah government clearly doesn’t buy the framing either — they’re opposing the stay. When the party being ‘helped’ by your legal action opposes your legal action, your explanation has a credibility problem.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a well-worn pattern. In October 2025, when the High Court first ruled, Anwar said the government would ‘study and consult the AG’ before deciding the next course of action — standard bureaucratic stalling language. In November 2025, Putrajaya announced it would not appeal the 40 per cent ruling, earning headlines and goodwill. Now, months later, it turns out there IS a court action — just not technically an ‘appeal on the 40 per cent.’ This is the same playbook used on other reform promises: announce the popular position loudly, then use procedural complexity to dilute or delay it quietly.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a government of transparency, justice, and institutional reform. Sabah waited 50 years for the courts to affirm what the Malaysia Agreement 1963 already guaranteed. The High Court gave you 180 days. You’ve spent much of that time explaining why you need to go back to court. If the 40 per cent is truly ‘case closed,’ prove it: withdraw the stay application, comply with the court order before the April 15 deadline, and pay Sabah what it is owed. Anything less is just another press conference.
