Saifuddin Nasution Bans Books on Anti-Colonial History Using Authoritarian Law PH Promised to Repeal — Then U-Turns in a Week

2026-04-15
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-15

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry — led by PKR’s Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — issued a prohibition order banning two books published by independent bookstore Gerakbudaya: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah. Both recount the struggles of the Malayan Communist Party against British colonialism. A third book — a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong, funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government — was also banned. The Home Ministry cited “communist ideologies” as the basis, invoking the Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984. After immediate backlash from civil society groups, PH’s own coalition members, publishers, and international press freedom organisations including ARTICLE 19, Saifuddin announced on April 23 that the ministry was in the process of revoking all three bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not about communism — the CPM disbanded in 1989. Shamsiah Fakeh’s memoir had been in circulation for nearly 20 years, originally published by Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia (UKM). The ban exposed the gulf between PH’s reformist rhetoric and its governing instincts: a coalition that rode to power on promises of free expression and institutional reform used the same colonial-era censorship law that previous administrations wielded to silence dissent. The U-turn within a week didn’t redeem the government — it confirmed that the ban was indefensible from the start, and that the Home Ministry acted without consulting its own coalition partners or applying basic institutional judgment.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH promised to repeal or reform the Printing Presses and Publications Act. Instead, they’re still using it. The Home Ministry banned a book that the national university published and that has been on shelves since 2004 without triggering a single documented case of radicalisation or public disturbance. One of the banned books was funded by the Selangor government — led by Anwar’s own PKR. So PH’s Home Ministry banned a book that PH’s state government helped produce. The reversal isn’t a victory for reform; it’s proof that the ban was a bureaucratic reflex by a ministry that hasn’t changed its authoritarian DNA despite flying the Madani flag.

📜 Previous Cases#

This isn’t the first time Malaysia’s government has wielded book bans to control historical narratives. The same Shamsiah Fakeh memoir was suppressed in 2004 shortly after UKM published it. In August 2023, the same PPPA was used to raid Toko Buku Rakyat bookstore in KL and seize books. Malaysia banned 27 books as of March 2026 alone under the Madani government — five in 2023 and three in 2022. ARTICLE 19 and the Malaysian Bar have repeatedly called for the PPPA’s repeal, calls that PH itself supported while in opposition.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a civilisation built on reform, openness, and institutional integrity. Instead, your Home Ministry bans books about your own country’s anti-colonial history, using a law you swore to repeal, then reverses course only when your own coalition members and civil society shame you into it. If you can’t even manage a consistent position on a 20-year-old memoir, how do you expect Malaysians to trust you with the big reforms? Repeal the PPPA. Don’t just reverse individual bans when the backlash gets loud enough.
