Fahmi Says RM5.4 Billion Health and Education Cuts Are ‘Only Non-Critical Spending’ — While the Health Ministry Loses RM3 Billion

2026-04-30
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister & Government Spokesperson

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-30

📌 What Happened#

On April 30, Communications Minister and government spokesperson Fahmi Fadzil defended a Treasury directive ordering RM10 billion in operating expenditure cuts across all federal ministries, including RM3.06 billion from the Health Ministry and RM2.39 billion from the Higher Education Ministry. Fahmi told reporters after a Cabinet meeting that the proposed cuts target “only non-critical spending” and that “all core ministry expenditures will continue as approved under the 2026 budget.” The directive, dated April 29, was issued in response to the fiscal strain caused by the Iran conflict and ballooning fuel subsidy costs estimated at RM7 billion monthly.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The MADANI government has spent two and a half years promising to strengthen Malaysia’s healthcare system and expand access to higher education. Now, facing the fiscal squeeze of the Iran war, the Treasury’s first instinct is to slash the two ministries most critical to ordinary Malaysians — health and education — to fund fuel subsidies. SUHAKAM, the national human rights commission, issued a formal statement warning that “fiscal retrenchment in healthcare during periods of economic pressure” risks “exacerbating existing gaps in access, quality, and equity.” The cuts reveal PH’s real priorities: political survival through subsidies over long-term human capital investment.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Calling a RM3.06 billion cut to health “non-critical” is an insult to every Malaysian who queues for hours at a government clinic. Malaysia’s public healthcare system is already understaffed, underfunded, and stretched to breaking point. The framing is classic PH — reassure the public with slogans while the numbers tell a different story. If RM3 billion from health is “non-critical,” then either the health ministry’s budget was bloated by 10% with pointless spending (which PH never flagged before), or “non-critical” is a political euphemism for “we hope nobody notices.” Meanwhile, the government maintains RM7 billion monthly in fuel subsidies — including for the wealthy who don’t need them — because subsidy rationalisation is politically toxic before the next election.

📜 Previous Cases#

This isn’t the first time PH has sacrificed substance for political convenience. The government delayed targeted diesel subsidies for over a year before implementing them in mid-2024, then stalled on RON95 rationalisation when polls dipped. In 2023, the health ministry reported over 400 specialist vacancies and crumbling infrastructure in rural hospitals — PH’s response was committees and task forces. Now, instead of reforming subsidy spending — which PH itself promised during Budget 2024 — the government is cutting from the ministries that serve the bottom 40% while protecting the politically untouchable fuel subsidy bill.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a civilised, competent government that puts people first. Cutting RM3 billion from health and RM2.4 billion from education while blowing RM7 billion a month on untargeted fuel subsidies is neither civilised nor competent. It’s cowardice dressed up as fiscal management. If you cannot tell Malaysians the truth about subsidy reform, you have no business cutting the services they actually depend on. Stop hiding behind “non-critical spending” and start making the hard choices you promised in 2022.
