Anwar Says He ‘Won’t Betray the Mandate’ — While Quietly Planning Snap Elections to Dodge Fuel Subsidy Cuts

2026-05-01
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

On May 1, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told a Labour Day crowd that his government “will not betray the people’s mandate” as oil prices surged to US$115 per barrel — up from US$70 just three months earlier. He pledged to maintain the subsidised RON95 price at RM1.99 per litre. Meanwhile, Bloomberg reported on April 24 — citing unnamed sources — that Anwar is considering calling a general election by the third quarter of 2026, potentially as early as October, specifically to get ahead of politically toxic fuel subsidy cuts. Malaysia’s monthly fuel subsidy bill has surged nearly 10-fold to approximately RM7 billion since the US-Israeli war with Iran began in February, threatening to blow up the government’s budget deficit targets. Second Finance Minister Amir Hamzah Azizan acknowledged recent reforms haven’t fully eliminated the subsidy burden.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is the clearest signal yet that Anwar’s government knows the subsidy regime is unsustainable but plans to make voters pay only after securing a fresh mandate. The strategy is transparent: hold the line on RM1.99 RON95, call elections before the price hike, then raise fuel prices after winning. As political analyst Adib Zalkapli told Bloomberg, “the best time to dissolve parliament is before the government is forced to increase the prices of subsidized fuel.” This isn’t economic management — it’s electoral choreography. The same government that lectures Malaysians about “fiscal responsibility” and ordered RM5.4 billion in ministry budget cuts is simultaneously burning through RM7 billion a month in fuel subsidies to keep the political optics clean ahead of an election.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar stands at a podium on Labour Day and says he won’t betray the mandate. At the same time, his inner circle is gaming out the optimal election date to avoid being in office when the subsidy axe falls. That’s not loyalty to the mandate — it’s sophisticated mandate manipulation. If Anwar genuinely believed in fiscal reform, he’d implement targeted subsidies now, take the political hit, and let voters judge his record. Instead, the plan is to win first, cut later — the same playbook every Malaysian government has used. The man who promised “new politics” is running the oldest political trick in the book: promise everything before the election, deliver the pain after.

📜 Previous Cases#

Fuel subsidy politics is Malaysia’s eternal recurring nightmare. Najib Razak’s government cut fuel subsidies in 2014, contributing to the public anger that eventually unseated BN. PH’s first government in 2018 introduced a managed float for RON95 and RON97 but reimposed price ceilings when prices rose and political pressure mounted. Anwar’s own government in 2024 began reforming diesel subsidies — but only after extensive polling showed it wouldn’t cost too many votes. The pattern is always the same: subsidies are economically unsustainable, every government knows it, and every government delays cuts until after the next election.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reform. You promised transparency. You promised Malaysians would not be treated like children who can’t handle hard truths. Now you’re planning to call a snap election specifically to avoid telling Malaysians the truth about fuel prices. If the subsidy bill has surged 10-fold and your budget is bleeding, say so — and implement targeted subsidies now. Don’t hide behind RM1.99 RON95 as an electoral shield and then raise prices the moment the ballots are counted. Malaysians can see through this. If you won’t betray the mandate, stop planning your escape before the bill arrives.
