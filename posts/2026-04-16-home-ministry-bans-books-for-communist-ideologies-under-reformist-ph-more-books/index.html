Home Ministry Bans Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ Under Reformist PH — More Books Banned Under Anwar Than Any Recent Government

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry banned two books under the Printing Presses and Publications Act for allegedly containing “communist elements and ideologies.” One of the banned titles is linked to the memoirs of Shamsiah Fakeh, a prominent figure in Malaysia’s Malay Left history. The ban drew swift criticism from civil society, publishers, and opposition figures. A week later, on April 24, the ministry announced it would adopt a “more consultative approach” and engage publishers before issuing future bans — a clear sign of damage control after the backlash.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not an isolated incident. Under Anwar’s government, Malaysia has banned more books than in the previous six years combined, according to a 2025 DW investigation. The South China Morning Post, in a May 2 analysis, framed the bans as evidence that Anwar’s government has “tilted to the right” to court Malay voters — abandoning the reformist brand that brought PH to power. For a prime minister who once sat in prison and built his political identity on free expression and institutional reform, presiding over an accelerating censorship apparatus is a damning contradiction.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH came to power promising to dismantle the authoritarian reflexes of previous governments — the same PPPA that Mahathir, Najib, and Muhyiddin used to silence dissent. Instead of repealing or reforming the Act, PH is using it more aggressively than its predecessors. Banning books about Malaysia’s own history — including the Malay Left, which is a legitimate subject of academic inquiry — doesn’t protect national security. It protects political convenience. The ministry’s belated promise to “consult publishers” is the bureaucratic equivalent of apologising after the punch has already landed.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s history of book bans is long and inglorious. Under BN, books on LGBTQ themes, political Islam, and government scandals were routinely banned. PH promised to be different. In 2025 alone, the Home Ministry banned dozens of titles — nearly half related to LGBTQ content — surpassing the combined total of the previous six years. The pattern is clear: PH has inherited BN’s censorship infrastructure and, rather than dismantling it, has expanded its use. The Shamsiah Fakeh memoir ban is particularly ironic given that PH’s own base includes figures with historical ties to the Malaysian Left.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised a Malaysia Madani built on freedom, dignity, and justice. Instead, your Home Ministry is banning more books than Najib’s. You’re using the same colonial-era laws you once condemned. If your reform agenda can’t survive two books about a dead communist grandmother, then your reform agenda was never serious. Stop banning books. Repeal the PPPA. Or admit that Madani is just BN with better branding.
