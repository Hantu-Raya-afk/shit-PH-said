📌 What Happened

Anwar’s Term Limit Bill Fails By Two Votes Despite Supermajority — Government’s Answer? ‘We’ll Try Again In June’

📌 What Happened

On March 2, Malaysia’s Parliament voted down the Constitutional (Amendment) Bill 2026, which would have limited the Prime Minister’s tenure to two terms or 10 years. The bill received 146 votes — just two short of the 148 needed for a two-thirds majority. Anwar’s unity government holds at least 153 seats, meaning the bill should have passed easily. But enough coalition MPs simply did not show up. Law Minister Azalina Othman Said had championed the bill as a way to “bolster institutional integrity.” The following day, the government quietly delayed a vote on the attorney general-public prosecutor separation bill — another flagship reform promise.
