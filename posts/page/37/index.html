📌 What Happened

On April 29, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil — speaking as government spokesman after a National Economic Action Council (MTEN) meeting — announced that all ministries, agencies, statutory bodies, GLCs, and GLICs must scale down official events and trim operating expenditure. 📌 What Happened

Treasury Orders RM10 Billion in Budget Cuts — Health and Education Take the Biggest Hit While Subsidy Bill Explodes From RM5B to RM58.4B

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister & Finance Minister; Johan Mahmood Merican, Treasury Secretary-General

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened

On April 29, 2026, Treasury Secretary-General Johan Mahmood Merican issued a government directive ordering all federal ministries and agencies to cut their operating budgets for 2026, citing the impact of rising energy prices from the Middle East conflict. The directive proposed a staggering RM10 billion in total cuts, with the Health Ministry taking the biggest hit at RM3.06 billion and the Higher Education Ministry at RM2.39 billion. The reason: the government’s subsidy bill had exploded from the RM5 billion originally approved in Budget 2026 to a projected RM58.4 billion — a 1,068% overrun — driven by rising global oil prices. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, who doubles as Finance Minister, is directly responsible for the fiscal policy that budgeted RM5 billion for subsidies while maintaining expensive universal subsidy programmes that were supposed to have been reformed.

Treasury Slashes RM5.4 Billion From Health and Education — PH’s ‘Rakyat First’ Government Cuts Where It Hurts Most

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister & Finance Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened

On April 29, 2026, Malaysia’s Treasury issued a directive proposing RM5.4 billion in operating budget cuts targeting the Ministry of Health (RM3.06 billion) and the Ministry of Higher Education (RM2.39 billion). The cuts are part of a broader RM10 billion savings target across all federal agencies, triggered by the fiscal strain of the Iran war — specifically, ballooning fuel subsidy costs estimated at RM7 billion monthly. Ministries must submit revised budgets by May 15 to the National Budget Office. Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s Treasury framed this as “fiscal sustainability” — but the targets tell a different story about whose welfare this government actually prioritises.
