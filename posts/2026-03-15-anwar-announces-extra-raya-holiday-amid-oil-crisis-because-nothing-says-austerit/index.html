Anwar Announces Extra Raya Holiday Amid Oil Crisis — Because Nothing Says ‘Austerity’ Like Another Day Off

2026-03-15
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim announced an additional public holiday for Hari Raya Aidilfitri on March 15, 2026, speaking at the National Consumer Day celebration at Suria Sabah in Kota Kinabalu. The extra day off will fall on either Friday March 20 or Monday March 23, depending on the moon sighting for Syawal. Anwar framed it as a reward for the people’s “efforts and dedication” during Ramadan, saying the early announcement would help Malaysians plan their travel. The announcement was made alongside Domestic Trade Minister Armizan Mohd Ali, who simultaneously launched a 15-day festive price control scheme covering 22–24 essential goods.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This announcement lands in the middle of the worst economic pressure the Madani government has faced. Oil has topped $100/barrel. The monthly RON95 subsidy bill has ballooned from RM700 million to RM3.2 billion. The KLCI is trading below 1700. Cabinet just announced austerity measures days ago, including WFH reviews and expenditure cuts. The government told Malaysians not to panic-buy and to grow their own vegetables. And now, in the same week, the Prime Minister’s headline move is… an extra day off. The contrast between the crisis messaging and the festive populism is jarring. It signals a government that knows the mood is souring and is reaching for easy wins rather than hard decisions.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

An extra holiday is not governance. It is not reform. It is not a response to $100 oil, or a plan for when the subsidy runway runs out in “two to three months” (Johari’s own words). It is a press conference designed to generate feel-good headlines while the structural problems pile up. The same Prime Minister whose Cabinet deferred the WFH decision, tripled the fuel subsidy instead of reforming it, and told ministry staff to plant vegetables — now wants credit for giving people a Friday off. This is the political equivalent of handing out sweets at a house fire. The crowd might cheer, but the house is still burning.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysian prime ministers have a long history of using public holidays as crowd-pleasers. Najib Razak was particularly fond of surprise holidays after national sporting achievements or royal occasions — a tradition Anwar has now inherited despite positioning himself as the anti-Najib. More recently, Anwar’s own government announced the Raya open house ban (which Hannah Yeoh praised as “people-centred”) while simultaneously running donation photo ops. The pattern is consistent: when the policy cupboard is bare, reach for symbolism. The difference now is that the economic stakes are significantly higher, and the gap between gesture and substance is harder to hide.

🎯 PH Callout#

You came to power promising Malaysia Madani — a government of substance, competence, and institutional reform. Three years in, your term-limit bill failed because your own MPs didn’t show up. Your subsidy reform is dead. Your economy minister says you have a “two to three month” runway. And your response to the worst oil crisis in years is an extra Raya holiday announced at a shopping mall in Sabah. Malaysians don’t need another day off. They need a government that can tell them the truth about what’s coming and what the plan is. If the best you can offer during a crisis is a long weekend, then you have confirmed what your critics have been saying: this government is better at campaigning than governing.
