Loke Admits DAP Failed to Get RCI on MACC — Promises to ‘Follow Up’ While His Own Cabinet Killed It

2026-03-13
#Politics 

Said by: Anthony Loke Siew Fook, DAP Secretary-General and Transport Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-13

📌 What Happened#

On March 13, DAP secretary-general and Transport Minister Anthony Loke appeared on the online programme The Keywords and admitted what everyone already knew: DAP failed to persuade the cabinet to establish a Royal Commission of Inquiry (RCI) into the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission (MACC) and the explosive ‘corporate mafia’ allegations surrounding chief commissioner Azam Baki. Loke’s spin? Investigations are ‘underway’ through a multi-agency task force, and DAP will ‘continue to follow up.’ This after weeks of DAP loudly demanding an RCI — from Gobind Singh Deo’s February 25 statement, to the CEC resolution on March 4, to Teresa Kok’s March 12 insistence that an RCI is superior to the task force.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a defining moment for DAP’s credibility as the reform wing of the unity government. Since the Bloomberg exposé on MACC’s alleged role in forced corporate takeovers, DAP has positioned itself as the party demanding accountability — calling for an RCI, issuing CEC statements, and having multiple MPs speak up. But when it mattered — at the cabinet table — they lost. The government chose a task force of executive agencies investigating each other, which Teresa Kok herself called a ‘conflict of interest.’ Loke’s admission confirms that DAP’s leverage inside the coalition is performative: loud enough to satisfy the base, weak enough to lose every fight that counts.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Loke is framing DAP’s failure as progress. ‘Investigations are underway’ is not what DAP demanded — DAP demanded an independent, royally-empowered RCI precisely because the existing agencies cannot credibly investigate MACC. The task force is MACC’s colleagues investigating MACC. Loke knows this. Gobind knows this. Kok said it publicly. Yet the party’s response to losing the cabinet fight is not to escalate — it’s to promise they’ll ‘follow up.’ Follow up with what? Another CEC statement? Another interview? DAP has been reduced to a party that demands accountability in press conferences and accepts its absence at the cabinet table.

📜 Previous Cases#

This pattern is not new. DAP pushed for the PM term limit bill — it failed in the Dewan Rakyat on March 2 when BN and opposition MPs didn’t back it, and DAP’s response was to ask for a retabling in June. DAP pushed for the separation of Attorney General and Public Prosecutor roles — referred to a select committee, timeline unknown. DAP pushed for local government elections — nowhere. The party’s reform agenda reads like a wish list that the coalition partners tolerate in public and veto in private. Each time, DAP accepts the loss and repackages it as ‘we’re still pushing.’

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians you were the reform coalition. You said Madani meant institutional accountability. Your own chairman, Gobind Singh Deo, called MACC’s denial ‘insufficient’ and demanded an RCI with full powers. Your own MP Teresa Kok called the task force a conflict of interest. And when your own secretary-general brought it to cabinet, the answer was no — and you accepted it. If DAP cannot win the RCI fight inside its own government, what exactly is the point of DAP being in government? ‘Follow up’ is not a reform strategy. It’s a coping mechanism.
