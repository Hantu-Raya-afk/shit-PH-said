Anwar Tells Everyone to ‘Focus on Economy’ While His Own MB Lost Majority — Democracy Can Wait, Apparently

2026-05-01
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, speaking to reporters after Friday prayers in Rawang on May 1, urged all parties to “remain calm” over the Negeri Sembilan political crisis and to “prioritise economic recovery” instead of escalating political disputes. He said the situation must be handled in accordance with the state constitution and the decree of the Yang di-Pertuan Besar, Tuanku Muhriz Tuanku Munawir, who directed Menteri Besar Aminuddin Harun to continue in office — despite 14 UMNO assemblymen having withdrawn confidence in him.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is Anwar’s playbook whenever his coalition faces an internal legitimacy crisis: invoke economic urgency as a shield against political accountability. A Menteri Besar who has lost majority support is being propped up not by democratic mandate but by royal decree and federal backing. Instead of confronting the fact that his own coalition partner’s state government has collapsed from within, Anwar is telling everyone to look away and talk about GDP. This sets a dangerous precedent — that coalition stability matters more than representative legitimacy, and that the federal government will use the economy as rhetorical cover whenever democratic norms become inconvenient.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar built his entire political career on the principle that governments must command majority support — that’s literally why he spent decades fighting BN. Now, when his own MB loses majority in a state assembly, the man who marched under “reformasi” tells everyone to respect the royal decree and focus on the economy. If PN or BN had done this — propping up a minority MB while telling critics to shut up — PH would have been screaming about backdoor governments from every rooftop in Kuala Lumpur. The hypocrisy is breathtaking: democratic mandates matter when you’re in opposition, but when you’re in power, suddenly the economy is more important than the consent of the governed.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time Anwar has used economic urgency to deflect from political crises. When the Azam Baki MACC scandal erupted earlier in 2026, the government’s response was to pivot to economic narratives and accuse critics of “destabilisation.” Anwar himself told Parliament in March 2026 about an alleged foreign-backed plot to topple the government — framing legitimate criticism as sabotage. The pattern is consistent: when the coalition is under pressure, invoke external threats or economic imperatives to delegitimise internal dissent. Malaysian politics has seen this movie before — Mahathir did it, Najib did it, and now Anwar is doing it with the same script and a different slogan.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised “kerajaan rakyat” — a government of the people. A government of the people does not prop up a leader who has lost the confidence of the people’s elected representatives and then tell everyone to talk about something else. If you believe in democratic legitimacy, prove it. Either restore Aminuddin’s majority through legitimate means, or let the democratic process take its course. Hiding behind a royal decree while lecturing the nation about economic recovery is not reform — it’s the same old Malaysian politics with better PR. You don’t get to define when democracy matters and when it doesn’t.
