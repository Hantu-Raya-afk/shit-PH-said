Anwar Begs Billion-Ringgit Companies for RM40 Pay Rises ‘With Compassion’ — After 2.5 Years, His Wage Policy Is Still ‘Please Be Nice’

2026-05-01
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

At the national Workers’ Day celebration at Bukit Jalil on May 1, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim called on employers to be “more considerate and compassionate” toward workers. He singled out companies recording RM1–2 billion in profits that “still find it difficult to approve even a RM40 salary increase.” He explicitly said he was not proposing anything that would cause companies losses or hurt competitiveness — only asking leaders to “show greater compassion.” He touted the government’s previous minimum wage hike from RM1,200 to RM1,500 and the GLC floor of RM3,000 as evidence of action.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Malaysia is in the middle of an energy crisis that is hammering the rakyat. Fuel subsidies have ballooned to RM7 billion a month. The Treasury just ordered RM5.4 billion in cuts to health and education. And on Labour Day — the one day a year when the government is supposed to stand unambiguously with workers — the PM’s policy offering is a polite request to corporate Malaysia to please be nicer. No new legislation. No enforcement mechanism. No structural wage reform. Just vibes.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar has been PM for over two and a half years. If billion-ringgit companies won’t give workers RM40 more a month, that’s not a compassion problem — it’s a structural one. You don’t fix it with speeches. You fix it with progressive wage models, sectoral minimum wages, profit-sharing mandates, or stronger collective bargaining rights. But Anwar won’t touch any of that because it would upset the corporate donors and GLCs that keep his coalition funded. So instead, he stands at a podium and performs empathy. The man who won’t retable his own budget is asking CEOs to search their conscience. That’s not governance. That’s a TED talk.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is a pattern. In 2023, Anwar promised a “comprehensive wage policy” during his first Budget speech — it never materialised beyond the minimum wage adjustment. In 2024, he told Parliament the government would “study” progressive wage models — no policy followed. Every Labour Day, the PM delivers emotional rhetoric about workers’ dignity. Every other day, the government prioritises corporate incentives, investment headlines, and GLC appointments over labour reform. The minimum wage increase from RM1,200 to RM1,500 was announced in 2022 under existing policy momentum — not a Madani breakthrough.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised a government that would be different. Two and a half years in, your labour policy is “please be compassionate.” RM40 a month — that’s what you’re begging for on behalf of Malaysian workers while your government bleeds RM7 billion a month on fuel subsidies it refuses to restructure. If you genuinely believe workers deserve better, legislate it. Put it in the budget you refuse to retable. Stop performing solidarity on Labour Day and start governing like you mean it.
