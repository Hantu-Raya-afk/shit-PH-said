Malaysia Drops 7 Places in Press Freedom Index Under Fahmi’s Watch — The Minister Who Called RSF Rankings ‘Not the Gold Standard’ Now Presides Over the Crackdown#

📌 What Happened#

Malaysia dropped seven places — from 88th to 95th out of 180 countries — in the 2026 World Press Freedom Index released by Reporters Without Borders (RSF) on April 30. The country’s overall score fell from 56.09 to 52.73, with deterioration across key indicators including the legal and social dimensions. The Malaysian Media Council (Majlis Media Malaysia) on May 3 flagged the decline and called for stronger protections for journalists and media independence. Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil, who oversees the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC), has previously dismissed the RSF index as “not the gold standard” — a position that looks increasingly convenient as Malaysia slides further down the rankings under his watch.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Pakatan Harapan came to power in 2022 promising reform, media freedom, and an end to the use of draconian laws against critics. Instead, the government has escalated the use of MCMC to summon, investigate, and charge individuals for social media posts — from influencers sharing news reports to citizens commenting on fuel prices. Fahmi himself announced in April 2026 that one person would be charged and five compounded over “fake energy crisis claims,” after MCMC identified 96 pieces of “false content” on fuel and electricity prices. The 7-place drop is not an accident. It is the measurable outcome of a government that treats criticism as disinformation and uses state agencies to enforce silence.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Fahmi’s pre-emptive dismissal of the RSF index as “not the gold standard” tells you everything. When you’re sliding, you don’t fix the problem — you discredit the measurement. This is the same playbook used by every authoritarian-leaning government in the region. PH promised to repeal oppressive laws like the Sedition Act and the Communications and Multimedia Act. Instead, Fahmi’s ministry has weaponised those exact laws to go after critics, summon influencers, and flag social media posts as “false content.” The phrase “free speech doesn’t mean freedom to offend” — uttered by Fahmi in February 2026 — is not a legal principle. It is a permission slip for selective enforcement, and the RSF rankings now reflect that reality.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s press freedom trajectory under PH has been a story of initial gains followed by steady erosion. The country jumped 19 places in 2025 (to 88th) after post-GE15 optimism, but has now given back 7 of those places in a single year. The Centre for Independent Journalism (CIJ) Malaysia has documented a pattern of MCMC summons targeting critics, including influencer Ratu Naga who was summoned in April 2026 for sharing a news report. In April 2026, Anwar called for a crackdown on “fake news” about fuel prices, and MCMC has been the enforcement arm. Globally, RSF notes press freedom is at a 25-year low, but Malaysia’s decline is self-inflicted — driven by its own government’s choices.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians a new Malaysia — one where people could speak freely, where the press could hold power to account, and where MCMC wouldn’t be used as a political weapon. Three and a half years later, Malaysia has dropped to 95th in press freedom, your Communications Minister dismisses the rankings instead of addressing them, and your MCMC is summoning citizens for sharing news articles. “Malaysia Madani” was supposed to mean civility and progress, not compliance and silence. Every summons, every charge, every “fake news” takedown is a broken promise. The RSF index isn’t perfect, Minister Fahmi — but it’s measuring something real, and the numbers are going in the wrong direction.
