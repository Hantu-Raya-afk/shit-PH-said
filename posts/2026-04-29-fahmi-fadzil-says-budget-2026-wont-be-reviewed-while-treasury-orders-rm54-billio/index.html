Fahmi Fadzil Says Budget 2026 Won’t Be Reviewed — While Treasury Orders RM5.4 Billion in Cuts and Sets a May 15 Deadline

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, Communications Minister Datuk Fahmi Fadzil told reporters at his weekly briefing that the government has “no intention of tabling Budget 2026 in Parliament again,” despite the Treasury having just ordered all ministries, departments, and agencies to review their operating expenditure and propose budget cuts by May 15. Fahmi described the ongoing adjustments as “optimising existing spending without altering the overall budget framework” — a claim made the same week leaked Treasury directives suggested RM5.4 billion in cuts to health and higher education alone, and as the government’s monthly fuel subsidy bill ballooned from RM700 million to RM7 billion due to the US-Iran conflict’s disruption of global energy supplies.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This isn’t just spin — it’s institutional dishonesty about the fiscal state of the country. When you order every ministry to slash spending by a deadline, that is a budget review by any definition. Fahmi’s semantic acrobatics — insisting there’s no review while a review is literally underway — erodes public trust at exactly the moment Malaysians need transparent governance. The energy crisis has turned Malaysia’s fiscal assumptions upside down, with subsidies now consuming nearly ten times what was originally budgeted, yet the government’s official line is that nothing has fundamentally changed. This is the same administration that promised transparency and accountability as its founding principles.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Call it what you want — “optimising,” “adjusting,” “reviewing operating expenditure” — when Treasury sends a directive telling every ministry to propose cuts by a hard deadline, that is a budget review. Fahmi’s claim that the “overall budget framework” remains unchanged is technically meaningless: if you’re slashing RM5.4 billion from health and education, the framework has changed for every Malaysian who depends on public hospitals and universities. The refusal to retable the budget in Parliament is not prudence — it’s avoidance. A retabling would mean debate, scrutiny, and accountability. Instead, PH is doing its cuts behind closed doors, through Treasury directives, while the Communications Minister tells the press there’s nothing to see here.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time PH has engaged in budget doublespeak. When the diesel subsidy rationalisation was announced, the government insisted it was not a “subsidy cut” but a “targeted subsidy.” When the WFH directive was issued for civil servants in April, it was framed as an “energy conservation measure” rather than an admission that the government couldn’t afford to keep the lights on in its own buildings. Malaysian governments have a long history of rebranding austerity as reform — Najib’s GST was sold as “broadening the tax base,” Muhyiddin’s pandemic spending was “stimulus” — but PH promised to be different. They’re not.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians you’d be the transparent government. You said the era of backroom deals and opaque fiscal management was over. Now your Communications Minister — your official spokesperson — is telling the country that a RM5.4 billion cut isn’t a budget review. If the budget is sound, retable it. If it’s not, say so and explain the trade-offs. Malaysians can handle hard truths. What they can’t handle — and what they voted you in to fix — is being lied to with a smile. “Optimising existing spending” is not governance. It’s copywriting.
