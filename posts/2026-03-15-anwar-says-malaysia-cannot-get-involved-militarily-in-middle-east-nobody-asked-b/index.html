Anwar Says Malaysia ‘Cannot Get Involved’ Militarily in Middle East — Nobody Asked, But Thanks for Clarifying

2026-03-15
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

At the National Consumer Day 2026 event in Kota Kinabalu on March 15, Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim declared that Malaysia “cannot get involved in what is happening in the Middle East, especially through military intervention.” He said Malaysia, as a “free country,” can voice its objections to war and violence, and stated: “We object to the attacks by Israel and the United States on Iran.” He acknowledged that the closure of the Strait of Hormuz would have “serious” implications that are “likely to worsen” and that “the cost of living would definitely rise.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

The Middle East conflict has sent oil past $100/barrel, forced the Cabinet into emergency session, ballooned RON95 subsidies to RM2–3.2 billion a month, crashed the KLCI below 1700, and triggered austerity measures across government. Malaysians are not asking their Prime Minister to send troops to the Gulf — they are asking what happens when subsidies run out, whether fuel prices will spike, and how the government plans to shield the economy. By volunteering that Malaysia won’t intervene militarily, Anwar answered a question precisely nobody was asking, while sidestepping the ones that actually matter.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is deflection dressed as statesmanship. No serious person — not the opposition, not the public, not a single commentator — has called for Malaysian military involvement in the Middle East. It is a classic strawman: set up an absurd position nobody holds, knock it down, and look decisive while doing nothing about the real crisis. In the same breath, Anwar acknowledged that the cost of living “would definitely rise” — and offered zero new measures to address it. His big announcement at the same event? An extra public holiday for Hari Raya. The country is staring down a fiscal crisis, and the PM is handing out days off and reassuring people he won’t invade Iran.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is a recurring pattern. During the early months of the Gaza conflict in 2023–2024, Anwar was criticised for prioritising rhetorical solidarity — grand speeches at the UN, emotional press conferences — over concrete diplomatic or economic action. His government’s response to the current oil crisis has followed the same playbook: special Cabinet meetings that produce “reviews,” ministers telling people not to panic, and the PM making sweeping moral declarations that sound good but change nothing. The Madani government’s crisis management consistently favours optics over substance.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia a government that governs — not one that performs. The country doesn’t need to be told you won’t send troops to the Gulf. It needs to know what happens when the RM3.2 billion monthly subsidy bill becomes unsustainable. It needs a coherent energy transition plan, not one cobbled together three years late because a war forced your hand. It needs leadership that addresses the question on every Malaysian’s mind — “Am I going to be okay?” — not one that answers the question on nobody’s mind. Stop performing. Start governing.
