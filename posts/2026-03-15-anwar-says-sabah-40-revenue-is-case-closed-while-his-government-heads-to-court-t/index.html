Anwar Says Sabah 40% Revenue Is ‘Case Closed’ — While His Government Heads to Court to Challenge the Judgment

2026-03-15
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister and Finance Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told a Madani Iftar event at Universiti Malaysia Sabah in Kota Kinabalu that the federal government’s commitment to Sabah’s 40 per cent revenue entitlement under the Malaysia Agreement 1963 is ‘final’ and ’not subject to appeal.’ He declared: ‘No negotiations. We are committed to honour our commitment of 40 per cent to the people of Sabah. Period.’ He repeated the line at a separate event, calling it ‘case closed.’ Yet in the same breath, he confirmed the government is going to court — not to challenge the 40 per cent, he insists, but to ‘seek clarification’ on parts of the October 2025 High Court judgment that he says unfairly questioned the legacies of past national and state leaders. A stay of execution hearing is set for March 31. The Sabah government has said it will oppose the stay.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Sabah’s 40 per cent revenue entitlement is one of the most politically charged issues in Malaysian federalism. The High Court affirmed Sabah’s constitutional right in October 2025 and gave Putrajaya 180 days — until April 15, 2026 — to act. Anwar’s public posture is categorical: case closed, no appeal. But the legal posture tells a different story. Filing any court action — even labelled as ‘seeking clarification’ — keeps the matter alive, creates procedural uncertainty, and gives the federal government room to delay. For Sabahans who have waited decades for what they are owed, the gap between Anwar’s words and Putrajaya’s legal filings is not a technicality. It is the entire issue.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

You cannot say ‘case closed, period’ and then go to court. That is not how ‘closed’ works. Anwar’s framing — that the court action is merely to ‘protect the credibility of past leaders’ — is a lawyerly dodge. If the 40 per cent is truly settled, then the judgment’s commentary on Tunku Abdul Rahman, Tun Razak, or Tun Mustapha is academic. You do not spend legal resources and political capital on academic footnotes unless you are keeping options open. The stay of execution application is the tell: if Putrajaya genuinely accepted the judgment, there would be no need to stay it. Sabah’s opposition to the stay confirms they see through the framing too. Anwar is trying to have it both ways — play the hero in Kota Kinabalu while his lawyers do the opposite in Putrajaya.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time Putrajaya has used procedural mechanisms to delay Sabah and Sarawak’s MA63 entitlements while publicly professing commitment. Previous administrations — BN, PH 1.0, PN — all made grand promises on MA63 during visits to East Malaysia and then slow-walked implementation once back in KL. The Special Cabinet Committee on MA63, set up under PH 1.0 in 2018, produced more press conferences than outcomes. Anwar himself promised during GE15 campaigning that his government would be ‘different’ on Sabah and Sarawak’s rights. Three years in, the pattern is familiar: categorical rhetoric in Sabah, qualified action in court.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Sabah you would be different. You told them you would honour MA63 without games, without delays, without the old Putrajaya playbook. So honour it. Drop the court action entirely — not just the 40 per cent appeal you say you are not making, but the ‘clarification’ application too. If the judgment’s language about past leaders bothers you, write an op-ed. Do not use the courts to keep a live legal avenue open while telling Sabahans the case is closed. They have heard ‘period’ before. From every prime minister who came before you. Make yours actually mean something.
