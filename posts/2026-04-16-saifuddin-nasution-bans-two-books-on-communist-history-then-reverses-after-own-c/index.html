Saifuddin Nasution Bans Two Books on Communist History, Then Reverses After Own Coalition Revolts — 27 Books Banned Under Anwar in 2026 Alone

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Ministry of Home Affairs issued a prohibition order banning two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Regimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Regimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — citing them as a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” The ban was signed under the Printing Presses and Publications Act (PPPA), the same draconian colonial-era law that PH once promised to repeal. One of the banned books is the memoir of Shamsiah Fakeh, a Malay nationalist and women’s rights leader whose granddaughter is a DAP Member of Parliament — an ally of Anwar himself. After immediate backlash from within PH’s own coalition, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution announced on April 23 that the government would reverse the ban and introduce a new review process. By then, Malaysia had already banned 27 books in 2026, exceeding the 24 banned in all of 2025.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not about communism. Nobody in Malaysia is building a Leninist vanguard party in 2026. This is about a government that promised reform and press freedom using the exact same censorship tool — the PPPA — that it once denounced as authoritarian overreach under Najib and Muhyiddin. The SCMP described Anwar’s government as having “tilted to the right” to court Malay voters. ARTICLE 19, the international press freedom organisation, explicitly called on Malaysia to reverse the ban and repeal the PPPA entirely. The accelerating pace of book bans under Anwar — more in the first quarter of 2026 than in the whole of 2025 — signals that MADANI’s commitment to intellectual freedom is not just hollow; it is actively deteriorating.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar Ibrahim spent decades as the face of Malaysia’s reform movement. PH’s GE15 manifesto explicitly promised media freedom and the repeal of oppressive laws, including the PPPA. Instead of repealing it, his Home Minister is using it at an accelerating rate — banning the grandmother’s memoir of one of his own allies, no less. The ban was so indefensible that PH’s own MPs publicly revolted against it within hours, forcing a humiliating U-turn within a week. But the reversal does not fix the problem: the PPPA remains intact, the precedent of using it is reinforced, and the government demonstrated it will reflexively reach for censorship first and think later. The process is the punishment — publishers like Gerakbudaya face disruption and legal costs even when the ban is reversed.

📜 Previous Cases#

This fits a clear pattern. DW News reported in late 2025 that more books had been banned under Anwar’s government than in the previous six years combined. In 2025, the Home Ministry banned 24 publications, with 21 citing “public morality” — a censorship rate that mirrors the worst of Najib Razak’s era, when 45 books were banned in 2017 alone. The PPPA has been a weapon of authoritarian convenience for every Malaysian government since independence, and Anwar’s administration has shown no interest in giving it up despite years of promising to do so. The IFEX coalition noted that the pattern of enforcement increasingly targets identity, expression, and historical discourse rather than genuine security threats.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised to repeal the PPPA. You promised media freedom. You promised Malaysia Madani would be different. Instead, your Home Minister is banning books faster than Najib did, reversing bans only when your own coalition threatens to embarrass you, and leaving the law intact for the next minister to abuse. Twenty-seven books banned in barely three months of 2026. At this rate, you will set a record for censorship under a government that ran on the promise of ending it. If you cannot repeal a colonial-era censorship law after nearly four years in power, stop pretending you are a reform government.
