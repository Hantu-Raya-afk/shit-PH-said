Saifuddin Nasution Bans Historical Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Reverses After Backlash, Proving PH’s Reform Promises Run on Autopilot

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 15–16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry under Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail issued prohibition orders banning two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — claiming they contained “communist elements and ideologies” that posed a threat to “national security.” The publisher, Gerakbudaya, was served the order directly, and SIRD, another publisher, was placed under investigation. Within days, civil society groups, PH’s own coalition lawmakers, and international press freedom organisations slammed the move as censorship. By April 23, Saifuddin announced the ministry was “in the midst of revoking” the ban on all three books (including a Mao Zedong translation funded by PKR-led Selangor), and pledged to consult publishers before future bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This episode is a litmus test for PH’s reformist credentials — and PH failed it comprehensively. The Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984, the law used to ban these books, is the same colonial-era censorship tool that PH has repeatedly promised to repeal or reform since its days in opposition. Instead of dismantling it, Saifuddin’s ministry deployed it against historical works that had circulated freely for nearly two decades. The SCMP’s May 2 analysis noted that Anwar’s government has “tilted to the right” to court Malay voters — and this book ban fits that pattern perfectly. When your own coalition members call your ban indefensible, you have a governance problem, not a communist one.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH came to power promising to expand civic freedoms, protect intellectual discourse, and dismantle authoritarian-era laws. Banning books about historical figures — one of whom was the grandmother of an Anwar ally — is not “protecting national security.” It is performative authoritarianism from a government too insecure to trust its own citizens with history. The reversal within a week makes it worse, not better: it proves the ban was never based on any serious security assessment but on bureaucratic reflex or political pandering. A government that bans first and thinks later is not a reformist government. It is the same old machinery with a new coat of paint.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not an outlier. Under Anwar’s watch, Malaysia has banned more books than in previous years combined. DW reported in 2025 that more books were banned in the first half of that year than in the preceding six years. The Sedition Act — another law PH promised to repeal — remains on the books and actively enforced. PAS has been cautioning against “provocative” enforcement of the Sedition Act, yet PH continues to wield these tools. The pattern is consistent: PH inherits authoritarian tools, promises reform, then uses those tools when politically convenient, reverses when caught, and calls the reversal “progress.”

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians a “New Malaysia.” You promised to repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act. You promised intellectual freedom. Instead, your Home Minister banned books about your own country’s history, got embarrassed by your own coalition partners, and reversed course only after international press freedom organisations called you out. The reversal is not reform — it is damage control. Repeal the law. Until you do, every ban you impose and every ban you reverse is just proof that your reform agenda is a slogan, not a policy.
