Finance Ministry Says RM5.4B Cuts to Health and Education Are ‘Non-Critical’ — RM3 Billion From Hospitals Isn’t Essential?

2026-04-30
#Economy 

Said by: Finance Ministry / Treasury (under Amir Hamzah Azizan, Finance Minister II)

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-30

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, the Treasury proposed RM5.4 billion in operational spending cuts targeting the Ministry of Health (RM3.06 billion) and the Ministry of Higher Education as part of a broader RM10 billion austerity drive across all federal agencies. The cuts — framed as a response to the Iran conflict’s impact on fuel subsidies — include postponing events, limiting overseas trips, saving on utilities, delaying recruitment for “non-critical positions,” and optimising agency reserves. On April 30, the Finance Ministry issued a statement insisting that “essential services will not be disrupted” and that adjustments only involve “non-critical items.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

Slashing RM3 billion from the health ministry’s operating budget while the country faces a strained public healthcare system is not a technocratic adjustment — it’s a political choice with real consequences. Hiring freezes and deferred recruitment mean longer wait times, fewer staff in already-overwhelmed hospitals, and delayed expansion of clinics. Malaysia’s public healthcare system has been under stress since the pandemic, with doctors and nurses regularly reporting burnout and understaffing. The cuts come while the government bears RM7 billion a month in fuel subsidies and refuses to retable Budget 2026, suggesting the administration would rather squeeze hospitals than revisit its own spending assumptions.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The Finance Ministry’s claim that RM5.4 billion in cuts to health and education budgets are “non-critical” is an insult to anyone who’s waited six hours at a government hospital or watched a university delay critical research. “Postponing events” and “limiting overseas trips” don’t add up to RM3 billion — that figure necessarily bites into staffing, procurement, and operational capacity. The government is using the Iran crisis as cover for a fiscal crunch it created by refusing to reform subsidies earlier, and the people paying the price are patients and students — not ministers, not GLCs, not the political class.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time a Malaysian government has raided health and education budgets during a fiscal crunch. Under BN, the 2015 oil price collapse led to mid-year budget revisions that slashed development spending on schools and hospitals, while political spending continued. PH itself, in its first stint in power (2018–2020), promised to raise health spending to 4% of GDP — a target never met. The Madani government’s own Budget 2026 already drew criticism for insufficient healthcare allocations before the Iran crisis forced additional cuts.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reform — not austerity that punches down. Pakatan Harapan came to power pledging better public services, stronger institutions, and a government that serves the rakyat. Cutting RM3 billion from hospitals while keeping RM7 billion a month in untargeted fuel subsidies flowing is not governance — it’s cowardice dressed up as fiscal discipline. If the crisis is serious enough to slash health budgets, it’s serious enough to retable the budget. The fact that you won’t tells the rakyat everything about your priorities.
