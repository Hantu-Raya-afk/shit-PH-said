Johari Declares US-Malaysia Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — After PH Signed a Lopsided Deal and Got Bailed Out by a Court Ruling

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, 2026, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) between Malaysia and the United States is now ’null and void.’ The deal, signed by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and President Donald Trump in Kuala Lumpur in October 2025, was rendered inapplicable after the US Supreme Court struck down Trump’s broad tariff authority under IEEPA on February 20, 2026. Johari told reporters the US ‘cannot impose tariffs on a blanket basis’ and said Washington is now shifting to Section 122 and Section 301 trade enforcement tools — including investigations into 60 countries, Malaysia among them, over forced labour and excess industrial capacity.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The ART was the centrepiece of PH’s US trade strategy. Under the deal, Malaysia accepted 19% tariffs on goods (down from an initial 47% threat), granted deeper market access, and made policy concessions to Washington. Four PKR MPs had already urged Putrajaya to suspend ratification after the Supreme Court ruling. Now the deal is dead — but the US is still investigating Malaysia under Section 301 for forced labour practices and industrial dumping, meaning the pressure hasn’t gone away. Malaysia’s RM233 billion in annual exports to the US remain exposed, particularly in E&E, palm oil, and rubber gloves. The trade deal is gone, but the trade threat isn’t.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Johari is framing this like Malaysia walked away from the table. It didn’t. The US Supreme Court pulled the table out from under everyone. PH signed a deal that Wong Chen, their own PKR MP, called ‘controversial and lopsided.’ They accepted punishing tariffs, made concessions, and called it diplomacy. Now a court ruling — not Malaysian strategy, not PH negotiating skill — voided the whole thing. And instead of acknowledging that the government got lucky, Johari casually tells reporters ‘it’s null and void’ as if this was always the plan. Meanwhile, the US has already launched Section 301 investigations that could hit Malaysia harder than the ART ever did. Getting saved by a judge in Washington is not a trade policy.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a pattern of PH reactive trade positioning. When Trump first imposed tariffs, Malaysia scrambled — Tengku Zafrul led negotiations that critics said gave away too much. The government celebrated the ART signing as a diplomatic win. Then Bloomberg revealed forced labour concerns in Malaysian exports, and the opposition demanded explanations. Johari told reporters just yesterday that MITI would ‘clarify’ forced labour allegations to the US — three years into a government that promised labour reform. Malaysia has faced repeated US Customs and Border Protection actions against glove manufacturers and palm oil producers over forced labour. Each time, the government’s response is reactive: explain, clarify, promise reform. The structural problems remain.

🎯 PH Callout#

You signed a deal your own MPs called lopsided. You accepted tariffs that punished Malaysian exporters. You made concessions to a US administration that was using trade as a weapon. And when a court ruling bailed you out, you acted like it was business as usual. Here’s what you haven’t answered: why did you sign that deal in the first place? What concessions did Malaysia make that are now public knowledge — and what concessions haven’t been disclosed? The Section 301 investigations are coming. Forced labour in Malaysian supply chains is still a live issue. Your RM233 billion export relationship with the US is still at risk. Stop acting like the crisis is over. It just changed shape.
