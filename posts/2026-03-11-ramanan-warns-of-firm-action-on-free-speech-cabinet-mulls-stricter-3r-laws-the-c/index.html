Ramanan Warns of ‘Firm Action’ on Free Speech, Cabinet Mulls Stricter 3R Laws — The Coalition That Promised to Repeal the Sedition Act

2026-03-11
#Politics 

Said by: R. Ramanan, Human Resources Minister / Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened#

On March 9, 2026, Human Resources Minister Datuk Seri R. Ramanan issued a statement warning that the government would take “firm action” against anyone who “deliberately threatens national peace,” particularly on matters involving religion, race, and the monarchy (3R). He said freedom of speech “should not be misused to incite or divide society.” Two days later, on March 11, Communications Minister Datuk Fahmi Fadzil confirmed that the Cabinet had decided “firm action must be taken on matters related to 3R” and that the government was actively considering amending existing laws or introducing new, stricter legislation to tackle 3R issues. By March 15, Deputy Communications Minister Aaron Ago Dagang said all ministries would play a role in handling 3R matters.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not a minor policy tweak — this is a PH-led government publicly signalling that it will create or tighten laws restricting speech on the most sensitive topics in Malaysian politics. The 3R framework — religion, race, and royalty — has historically been the instrument of choice for every Malaysian government that wanted to silence critics, jail activists, and chill public discourse. The Sedition Act, which PH explicitly promised to repeal in its 2022 manifesto, is itself a 3R enforcement tool. Instead of repealing it, the government is now proposing to build on it. This represents a fundamental betrayal of PH’s civil liberties platform and a direct threat to the already shrinking space for public debate in Malaysia.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The same coalition that marched against the Sedition Act, that promised to protect press freedom, that campaigned on civil liberties and human rights — is now sitting in Cabinet discussing how to make 3R laws stricter. Ramanan’s statement that “freedom of speech should not be misused” is the exact formulation used by every authoritarian government in the region to justify speech crackdowns. Meanwhile, this government has already used Section 124B against C4’s CEO for questioning the MACC, investigated journalists, and threatened social media users. The Heidy Quah case — where a social rights advocate was prosecuted under the Communications and Multimedia Act — saw the Federal Court partly reverse a landmark Court of Appeal ruling protecting expression, with barely a murmur from PH. You don’t get to prosecute critics under existing laws and simultaneously propose new ones while claiming to be the reform coalition.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a deep history of using vague speech laws to suppress dissent. The Sedition Act 1948, which PH pledged to abolish, remains fully intact. The Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (Section 233) has been used to charge critics, satirists, and activists. SOSMA, which PH also promised to repeal, is still on the books. In 2024, activist Heidy Quah was charged for a social media post about detention centre conditions. Fahmi himself, as Communications Minister, has previously warned social media users about “irresponsible” posts. Every Malaysian government — BN, PN, PH — has found that once in power, speech laws are too useful to give up.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told voters you were the coalition of reformasi, of free speech, of civil liberties. You marched against the Sedition Act. You called for SOSMA’s repeal. You said Malaysians deserved the right to speak freely without fear of prosecution. And now, three years in, your Cabinet is not discussing how to repeal those laws — it is discussing how to make them stricter. When Ramanan says “firm action” and Fahmi confirms the Cabinet is looking at new legislation, every journalist, activist, and ordinary Malaysian who dared speak up on sensitive issues knows exactly what that means. You have become the thing you said you would dismantle.
