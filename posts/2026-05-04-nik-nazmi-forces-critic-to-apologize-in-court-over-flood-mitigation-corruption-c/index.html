Nik Nazmi Forces Critic to Apologize in Court Over Flood Mitigation Corruption Claims — The Reform Coalition That Championed Free Speech Now Sues It Into Silence

2026-05-04
#Politics 

Said by: Nik Nazmi Nik Ahmad, Former Natural Resources & Environmental Sustainability Minister (PKR, Setiawangsa MP)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-05-04

📌 What Happened#

On May 4, 2026, former Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability Minister Nik Nazmi Nik Ahmad — still a PKR MP for Setiawangsa — settled a defamation suit against former PKR member Badrul Hisham Shaharin (Chegubard) at the Kuala Lumpur High Court. Chegubard read out a public apology in open court, formally retracting allegations he made in four Facebook posts in December 2023 that accused Nik Nazmi of corruption linked to flood mitigation projects, including claims that an open tender process was rigged and project costs had ballooned to RM16.6 billion. Nik Nazmi filed the suit on January 15, 2024, claiming the posts were malicious and damaged his credibility. The matter was concluded via consent judgment — Chegubard admitted his allegations were “false and baseless.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a sitting PH lawmaker using the court system to compel a public critic into retracting corruption allegations — the exact tactic PH spent years in opposition denouncing when wielded by BN and UMNO against activists, journalists, and whistleblowers. Flood mitigation is one of the most corruption-prone sectors in Malaysian public procurement, and Chegubard’s original allegations — whether accurate or not — were the kind of public-interest scrutiny that reformist parties are supposed to welcome, not litigate into submission. The forced public apology sends a chilling message to anyone else considering raising questions about how billions in flood mitigation funds are spent.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Pakatan Harapan built its brand on being the coalition that would protect free speech, defend whistleblowers, and welcome accountability. Their 2018 manifesto explicitly promised to repeal or amend laws used to silence critics. Instead, a PKR MP is now using defamation suits — the private-sector equivalent of sedition charges — to extract courtroom apologies from people who questioned government spending. Nik Nazmi claims the Finance Ministry approved RM11.8 billion for 33 flood mitigation projects and that Chegubard’s RM16.6 billion figure was wrong. Fine — publish the tender documents. Release the audit trail. Prove it with transparency, not with lawyers. When your response to a corruption allegation is a lawsuit instead of a Freedom of Information disclosure, you’ve already told the public everything they need to know about your relationship with accountability.

📜 Previous Cases#

This fits a growing PH pattern of using legal mechanisms to suppress criticism. On the same day, Anthony Loke’s political secretary Ho Weng Wah filed a separate police report against the same Chegubard over posts about the Negeri Sembilan crisis — invoking the “3R” (race, religion, royalty) card. Earlier, the MACC under PH-appointed leadership has itself been accused of weaponising its powers for corporate manipulation. PH’s track record in opposition was built on defending figures like Zunar, Fahmi Reza, and Maria Chin Abdullah from exactly this kind of legal intimidation. Now in government, they’ve adopted the same playbook — just swapping the Sedition Act for civil defamation suits and police reports.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians a “New Malaysia” where citizens could question power without fear. Your 2018 manifesto said you would protect whistleblowers and expand civic freedoms. Three years into your second run in government, your MPs are suing critics into public apologies over flood mitigation spending — one of the most notoriously opaque areas of Malaysian procurement. If Nik Nazmi’s hands are clean, prove it with open data, not court orders. Every forced apology extracted from a critic is a betrayal of the people who voted PH specifically because they were tired of being told to shut up.
