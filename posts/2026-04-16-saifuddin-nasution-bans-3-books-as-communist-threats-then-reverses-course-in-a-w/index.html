Saifuddin Nasution Bans 3 Books as ‘Communist Threats’ — Then Reverses Course in a Week After His Own Coalition Called It Embarrassing

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail’s ministry declared a ban on two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — citing them as threats to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” A third book, Mao Zedong: China dalam Dunia Abad ke-20, a Malay translation of an academic study funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was banned separately around the same time. Within days, a wave of criticism from civil society, publishers, academics, and even PH’s own coalition members forced Saifuddin to announce on April 23 that the ministry was in the process of revoking all three bans and that he would personally review future ban decisions.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This wasn’t some obscure bureaucratic hiccup — it was the Home Ministry of a supposedly reformist government banning books in 2026 under a law that requires no justification, then scrambling to reverse course after the political cost became obvious. One of the banned books was a memoir of a figure linked to an ally of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim himself. The Mao Zedong translation was literally funded by PKR’s own Selangor government. The episode exposed that PH’s reform credentials are paper-thin: the institutional machinery of censorship remains intact, the Home Minister either didn’t know what his own ministry was banning or didn’t care until it blew up, and the reversal was driven by embarrassment, not principle.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is the same coalition that spent a decade in opposition railing against BN-era censorship, book seizures, and the abuse of the Printing Presses and Publications Act. Anwar Ibrahim — the man who lectured at Georgetown, Oxford, and St Andrews on democratic values and intellectual freedom — now presides over a government that bans historical memoirs as “communist threats” in an age when every one of these texts is a Google search away. The reversal doesn’t earn points; it proves the ban was indefensible from the start. Saifuddin’s admission that the previous banning process was flawed is an indictment of his own ministry’s competence. You don’t get credit for putting out a fire you started.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long and inglorious history of book bans — the Printing Presses and Publications Act has been used to suppress everything from Marxist theory to moderate Islamic scholarship. In August 2023, enforcement officers raided Toko Buku Rakyat in KL and seized a book on Karl Marx that wasn’t even on the banned list. As of March 2026, Malaysia had already banned 27 books under the Madani government alone — five in 2023, three in 2022, and more since. PH’s own MPs, including PKR’s Tan Kar Hing, publicly reminded Anwar of his academic career and questioned why a self-declared reformist government was suppressing historical texts. The pattern is clear: PH inherited BN’s censorship tools and, rather than dismantling them, quietly kept using them.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a civilised, progressive Malaysia built on knowledge, openness, and institutional reform. Instead, your Home Ministry is banning books that your own state government funded, written about figures from your own political heritage, and then reversing the bans only when the embarrassment becomes unbearable. If you believe in “reformasi” and the marketplace of ideas, repeal the provisions of the Printing Presses and Publications Act that allow bans without justification. Don’t just promise to “personally review” future bans — that’s not reform, that’s one man’s whim replacing another’s. Dismantle the machinery of censorship, or admit you never intended to.
