Johari Says US-Malaysia Trade Deal Is ‘Null and Void’ — After PH Spent a Year Defending the Lopsided Deal It Couldn’t Cancel Itself

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) between Malaysia and the US is “null and void.” The deal — signed by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and President Donald Trump in October 2025 during the ASEAN summit in Kuala Lumpur — was rendered inapplicable after the US Supreme Court in February 2026 struck down Trump’s broad tariff powers, ruling that tariffs require specific justification and cannot be imposed on a blanket basis. Johari told reporters: “It is not on hold. It is no longer there, it’s null and void.” He added that the US is now shifting to Section 301 and Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 to investigate 60 countries, including Malaysia, for excess capacity, forced labour, environmental violations, and trade distortions.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The ART was PH’s flagship trade achievement — the deal that supposedly protected Malaysian exporters from Trump-era tariffs of up to 47%, negotiated down to 24%, then to 19% under the agreement. It gave 1,711 Malaysian products zero-tariff access. PH defended this deal against sustained criticism that it was lopsided: Malaysia made deep concessions on market access for US goods across chemicals, machinery, vehicles, dairy, poultry, pork, rice, and fuel ethanol, while the US merely lowered its punitive tariff to 19%. Now that the deal is void, Malaysia is back at square one — and facing a potentially worse landscape of Section 301 investigations targeting forced labour and dumping. The RM233 billion in annual exports to the US is exposed, and PH has no replacement framework.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH didn’t cancel this deal because it grew a spine. A US court did it for them. For months, critics — including four PKR MPs from PH’s own coalition — urged Putrajaya to suspend ratification of the ART, calling it lopsided and unfavourable. PKR’s Wong Chen publicly called it “controversial and lop sided.” PH ignored its own backbenchers, defended the deal, and kept the ratification process alive. Now Johari stands at a podium declaring it “null and void” as though the government made a strategic decision, when in reality, the US Supreme Court made the decision for Malaysia. The only thing PH successfully negotiated was the privilege of giving away market access for a deal that a foreign court would kill before it was even ratified.

📜 Previous Cases#

This fits a pattern of PH trade policy that is reactive, not strategic. When Trump first imposed tariffs, Malaysia scrambled. When the ART was signed, PH celebrated it as a diplomatic win while economists flagged the concessions as excessive. When four PKR MPs broke ranks to demand suspension, party leadership did nothing. This is the same government that took three years to even begin addressing forced labour allegations in electronics, palm oil, and gloves — issues now forming the basis of the US Section 301 investigations that could hit Malaysian exporters harder than the ART ever would have. PH’s trade policy is consistently one step behind events it should be anticipating.

🎯 PH Callout#

You signed a deal your own MPs called lopsided. You defended it against internal criticism. You let a foreign court cancel what you should have renegotiated or walked away from months ago. And now, instead of a deal — even a bad one — Malaysia faces open-ended Section 301 investigations into forced labour and trade distortions with no framework, no leverage, and no strategy. You had a year to build a better trade position. You spent it defending a deal that doesn’t exist anymore. The RM233 billion question is: what’s your plan now?
