Anwar’s Own PM Term Limit Bill Fails by Two Votes — A Supermajority That Can’t Even Pass Its Own Reforms

2026-03-02
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister / Azalina Othman Said, Minister in PM’s Department (Law and Institutional Reform)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-02

📌 What Happened#

On March 2, 2026, Malaysia’s Dewan Rakyat voted on the Constitutional (Amendment) Bill 2026, which would have capped the Prime Minister’s tenure at 10 years — two terms. The bill, tabled by Minister Azalina Othman Said and championed by PM Anwar Ibrahim himself, needed a two-thirds majority of 148 votes to pass. It got 146. Two votes short. A total of 44 Perikatan Nasional MPs abstained, 32 MPs were absent — and critically, eight government and independent MPs failed to show up. PH claimed all its MPs were present. The math didn’t add up.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This was not some backbench private member’s bill. This was a flagship reform promise from PH’s 2022 General Election manifesto — a direct response to Mahathir’s 22-year grip on power. Anwar was widely believed to command 154 MPs, comfortably above the 148 threshold. This vote was the first formal test of that claim, and it failed. The bill’s defeat exposed what analysts at CNA called “significant coordination gaps and uneven political commitment” within the unity government. If Anwar cannot whip his own coalition on a reform he personally tabled, what does that say about every other institutional reform PH promised?

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar told Parliament the amendments would “strengthen institutional reforms and ensure leadership renewal.” Beautiful words. Then the cameras caught him mid-laugh, chatting with his deputy, as the numbers were read out. Eight of his own coalition or allied MPs did not even bother to show up. This is the man with a so-called supermajority — 154 seats — who could not marshal 148 votes for his own bill. Either the supermajority was always fiction, or Anwar’s whip operation is so incompetent it cannot count to 148. Either way, the reform-minded urban voters who put PH in power just watched their signature constitutional promise die on the floor of the Dewan Rakyat, two votes short, while the PM laughed.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long history of promised reforms that die quiet deaths. The IPCMC (Independent Police Complaints and Misconduct Commission) was first proposed in 2005 — it took until 2022 to get a watered-down version. The PM term limit was itself inspired by the damage of Mahathir’s 22-year tenure, which PH ran against in both 2018 and 2022. In the Pakatan Harapan 1.0 government (2018–2020), institutional reforms were repeatedly deprioritised in favour of coalition management. The pattern is clear: PH makes reform promises to win elections, then discovers that reform is hard when your coalition includes the same people who benefited from the old system.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians in your 2022 manifesto that you would cap the PM’s tenure. You had the numbers. You had the bill. You had the floor. And you still lost — by two votes — because you could not even get your own people to show up. Don’t blame PN for abstaining; they were never going to vote for your bill and you knew it. The failure is internal. Fix your coalition discipline, or stop making constitutional promises you cannot keep. The rakyat did not vote for reform slogans. They voted for reform outcomes. Two votes short is not a near-miss — it is proof that your supermajority is a myth.
