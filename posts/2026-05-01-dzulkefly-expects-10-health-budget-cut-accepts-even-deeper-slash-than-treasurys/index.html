Dzulkefly Expects 10% Health Budget Cut — Accepts Even Deeper Slash Than Treasury’s Initial RM3B Proposal While Insisting ‘Core Services Safe’

2026-05-01
#Health 

Said by: Dzulkefly Ahmad, Health Minister

Category: Health

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

Health Minister Datuk Seri Dr Dzulkefly Ahmad revealed on May 1, 2026 that he expects a cut of approximately 10% to the Ministry of Health’s RM46.5 billion budget for 2026 — a figure significantly higher than the RM3.06 billion (6.6%) initially proposed by the Treasury in its April 29 directive. Speaking after the Treasury ordered all federal ministries to trim operating expenditure due to the fiscal impact of the US-Israeli war on Iran, Dzulkefly maintained that “essential core services” including patient treatment, emergency care, and the supply of medicines would be protected, while acknowledging that non-core areas would face adjustments.

🔥 Why It Matters#

A 10% cut to the Health Ministry’s budget would leave MOH with approximately RM41.85 billion — less than its RM45.3 billion allocation in 2025, effectively reversing a year of budget growth. This comes at a time when Malaysia’s public hospitals are already chronically understaffed, contract doctors remain in limbo, and the Federation of Private Medical Practitioners’ Associations Malaysia (FPMPAM) has publicly warned that cuts of this scale will “worsen patient outcomes and strain health workers.” SUHAKAM, the national human rights commission, issued its own statement on May 1 urging a “rights-based response” to the proposed cuts. The government is essentially asking the Health Ministry to absorb the cost of a geopolitical crisis it didn’t cause — while fuel subsidies balloon to RM7 billion per month.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Dzulkefly’s assurance that “core services won’t be affected” is the kind of ministerial hand-waving that collapses under scrutiny. When you cut 10% of a health budget that was already stretched thin, the definition of “core” and “non-core” becomes a political exercise, not a medical one. Overseas training for doctors is “non-core” — until you need specialists. Official functions are “non-core” — until coordination between agencies breaks down. Staff recruitment is “not yet decided” — until the next ward closes because there aren’t enough nurses. The Health Minister is not pushing back against the cuts; he’s pre-emptively accepting them and packaging the damage as prudent management. That’s not leadership. That’s compliance dressed as competence.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time Malaysia’s health system has been asked to tighten its belt while the political class keeps spending. Budget 2026 itself already slashed funding for the Madani Medical Scheme by 50%, cut health education by 23%, and reduced allocations for the National Centre for Excellence in Mental Health by 12% — even as the overall MOH envelope grew modestly. In 2023, Dzulkefly’s predecessor faced similar backlash when contract doctors staged walkouts over job insecurity. The pattern is consistent: health gets the headline allocation, then gets quietly hollowed out through operational cuts, hiring freezes, and deferred maintenance.

🎯 PH Callout#

You campaigned on building a compassionate, people-first government. You promised that healthcare would be a pillar of the Madani agenda. Now your Health Minister is quietly accepting a 10% budget cut — bigger than what Treasury even asked for — and telling the rakyat not to worry because “core services” are safe. If RM7 billion a month can be found to subsidise fuel, why is the Health Ministry being asked to sacrifice RM4.65 billion? The rakyat didn’t vote for a government that cuts hospitals to fund petrol. Push back, or admit that “reformasi” doesn’t extend to the Treasury.
