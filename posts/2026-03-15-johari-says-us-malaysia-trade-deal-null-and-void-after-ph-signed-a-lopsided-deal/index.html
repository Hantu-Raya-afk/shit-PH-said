Johari Says US-Malaysia Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — After PH Signed a Lopsided Deal That Got Killed by a US Court, Not Malaysian Spine

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) between Malaysia and the United States is now “null and void.” The bilateral trade deal — signed on October 26, 2025 by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and then-US President Donald Trump in Kuala Lumpur — collapsed after the US Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that Trump lacked authority under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act to impose sweeping tariffs. Johari told reporters: “It is not on hold. It is no longer there, it’s null and void.” The US has since imposed a uniform 10% tariff on all trading partners under Section 122, and launched fresh Section 301 investigations into forced labour and industrial policy — including against Malaysia.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The ART was one of the Madani government’s most controversial economic moves. Malaysia initially faced tariff exposure of up to 47%, which was negotiated down to 24% and eventually 19%, with zero-tariff privileges granted to 1,711 Malaysian products. In return, Malaysia offered deeper market access and policy concessions to Washington. Critics — including opposition MPs and trade analysts — called it lopsided from the start. Now that the US Supreme Court has struck down the reciprocal tariff framework, every US trading partner pays just 10% — meaning Malaysia made painful concessions for a deal that delivered worse terms than the post-ruling default. Malaysia’s RM233 billion in annual exports to the US remain exposed to Section 301 probes on forced labour and industrial subsidies. The deal didn’t buy Malaysia protection; it bought embarrassment.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Johari is framing this like a routine legal development — “the court ruled, the deal is void, we move on.” But the real story is that PH signed a lopsided trade deal under pressure, made concessions that critics warned about at the time, and then got bailed out not by their own negotiating skill or strategic foresight but by the US judiciary. Every country that didn’t rush to sign a deal with Trump is now paying the same 10% rate as Malaysia — without having surrendered policy concessions. The government celebrated the ART as a diplomatic win in October 2025. Tengku Zafrul, who led negotiations, called it historic. Now it’s “null and void” and nobody in government wants to talk about what Malaysia already gave away. That’s not governance; that’s getting lucky after making a bad bet.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time a Malaysian government signed a trade deal that aged poorly. The TPPA under Najib was abandoned after the US pulled out. But the ART was uniquely rushed — negotiated and signed within weeks during the ASEAN Summit, with minimal parliamentary scrutiny. Civil society groups, trade unions, and even PKR backbenchers raised concerns at the time about the lack of transparency and the scope of concessions. DAP’s Wong Chen publicly called the deal “lop-sided” and urged caution. The pattern is familiar: sign first, celebrate, then quietly hope nobody notices when the terms turn out to be bad. With ART, they got lucky. The question is whether any of the policy concessions Malaysia offered have already been implemented — and whether those can be reversed now that the deal is dead.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians the ART was a triumph of Madani diplomacy. You said Anwar’s personal rapport with Trump secured a better deal for Malaysia. You paraded the signing ceremony as proof that PH could handle great-power politics. Now that deal is dead — killed not by Malaysian pushback, not by PH standing firm, but by the US Supreme Court doing what your government wouldn’t: saying no to Trump’s overreach. Every country that refused to rush into a deal is now paying 10%. Malaysia is paying 10% too — but only after making concessions it didn’t need to make. The rakyat deserves a clear answer: what exactly did Malaysia give away under ART, and can you get it back? Or are we stuck with the concessions from a deal that no longer exists?
