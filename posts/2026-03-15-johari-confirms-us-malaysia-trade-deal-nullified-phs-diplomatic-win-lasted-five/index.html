Johari Confirms US-Malaysia Trade Deal Nullified — PH’s ‘Diplomatic Win’ Lasted Five Months

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, 2026, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Malaysia-US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART), signed by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and US President Donald Trump in October 2025, has been nullified. “It is not on hold. It’s null and void,” Johari told reporters. The US Supreme Court ruled that Trump’s blanket tariffs were unlawful, striking down the tariff framework the ART was built on. The deal had committed Malaysia to providing “significant preferential market access” for US products across chemicals, machinery, vehicles, dairy, poultry, pork, rice, and fuel ethanol. In exchange, the US maintained a 19% reciprocal tariff on Malaysian goods, with zero rates only for selected products. Johari said the US is now relying on Section 122 of its Trade Act of 1974, allowing temporary tariffs of up to 10%. Four PKR MPs had earlier urged Putrajaya to suspend all ratification steps following the court ruling. Meanwhile, the US has launched Section 301 investigations into 60 countries, including Malaysia, over excess production capacity and forced labour.

🔥 Why It Matters#

When Anwar signed this deal in October 2025, the government sold it as proof that Malaysia could navigate great-power competition and secure favourable terms with Washington. Five months later, the deal is void — not because Malaysia renegotiated or walked away, but because a US court struck down the legal basis it rested on. Malaysia had already signalled its willingness to open wide for US agricultural and industrial imports and remove quantitative restrictions on US goods. Now the deal is gone, but every future negotiating partner knows exactly how cheaply Malaysia was willing to deal. To make it worse, Malaysia now faces the same Section 301 scrutiny and Section 122 tariff threat as 60 other countries, with no preferential treatment to show for the months of negotiations. The US has since announced a global 15% tariff to replace the scrapped system — meaning Malaysia may end up paying less than the 19% in the original deal, without having given away anything.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The ART was criticised as lopsided from day one. Malaysia opened its markets for US rice, dairy, poultry, pork, and industrial goods while the US kept a 19% tariff wall, offering zero rates only on cherry-picked items. PKR’s own Wong Chen MP publicly called it “controversial and lopsided.” When your own backbencher is calling your signature trade deal one-sided, and the other party’s courts then void it entirely, you haven’t negotiated — you’ve been played. PH had no control over the US legal challenge and apparently no contingency plan for it. The government staked diplomatic capital, trade concessions, and political credibility on a deal that was always one judicial challenge away from evaporating. And now Johari is telling reporters it’s “null and void” like it’s just another news cycle, not a five-month diplomatic embarrassment.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s trade negotiation track record under PH has been a series of bold announcements followed by quiet retreats. The CPTPP was ratified with promises of economic transformation — the structural reforms it required remain largely unimplemented. PH initially positioned Malaysia as neutral in US-China trade tensions, only to sign a deal that clearly aligned with Washington’s framework. Previous governments made similar mistakes — the 1MDB-era infrastructure deals with China locked Malaysia into unfavourable terms, and PH rightly criticised those. But the pattern is the same: sign the headline, celebrate the photo op, deal with the consequences later. The ART is now Exhibit A of PH’s own version of that playbook.

🎯 PH Callout#

You flew to Kuala Lumpur’s ASEAN summit, signed the deal with Trump on stage, held the press conferences, and told Malaysians this was proof your government could deliver on the world stage. Five months later, a US court voided it and you’re back to square one — except now the whole world knows what Malaysia was willing to give away. You opened your agricultural markets, committed to removing import barriers for US products, and accepted a 19% tariff on your own goods. What did you get? A piece of paper that didn’t survive its first legal challenge. Where is the post-mortem? Where is the accountability for a trade strategy that bet everything on a single agreement with no fallback? Four of your own MPs told you to suspend ratification after the court ruling — you should have seen this coming. You owe Malaysians an explanation, not another round of ’everything is under control.'
