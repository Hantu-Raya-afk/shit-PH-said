Saifuddin Nasution Bans Three Books as ‘Communist Threats’ — Then Reverses After Backlash, Proving PH’s ‘Reform’ Is Just Improv

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister & PH Secretary-General

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 15–16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry under Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — who is also PKR member and PH Secretary-General — issued prohibition orders banning three books: Shamsiah Fakeh’s memoir Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10, Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10), and a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong titled Mao Zedong: China dalam Dunia Abad ke-20. The ministry cited “communist elements and ideologies” that posed a danger to national security. The Mao Zedong book was published under Selangor state government funding — a state led by Anwar’s own PKR. Within a week, after a firestorm of criticism from civil society groups, publishers, and PH’s own coalition MPs — including DAP’s Jamaliah Jamaluddin, who is Shamsiah Fakeh’s granddaughter — Saifuddin announced the government was revoking all three bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This ban-then-reversal exposed the deepening tension between Anwar’s reformist branding and his government’s actual governance instincts. As the South China Morning Post noted on May 2, the episode is part of a broader rightward tilt by PH to court Malay voters ahead of GE16. When a government that promised intellectual freedom and institutional reform reflexively bans books — including one funded by its own state government — then reverses under pressure without any structural accountability for how the ban was issued, it reveals that PH’s reform agenda is not principled policy but improvised damage control. PKR MP Lee Chean Chung pointed out the devastating irony: these books were never banned by BN or PN, but were banned by the PH-led government.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is a government that marched to Putrajaya on promises of press freedom, institutional reform, and open discourse. Anwar Ibrahim himself was a university lecturer who railed against censorship under Mahathir and Najib. Now his own Home Ministry — led by his own party’s secretary-general — is banning memoirs of historical figures and academic texts about foreign leaders using the same Printing Presses and Publications Act that BN wielded for decades to muzzle dissent. The fact that one banned book was funded by PKR-led Selangor makes it absurd beyond parody: the government literally banned its own state’s publication. And the reversal was not principle — it was embarrassment management. No one was held accountable for issuing the bans. No structural reform to the Publications Act was announced. Just a quiet walk-back and hope the news cycle moves on.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long, inglorious history of book bans under successive governments. Under BN, titles on May 13, reformasi, corruption, sexuality, and religion were routinely prohibited using the same act. Independent publisher Gerak Budaya has documented a growing “Hall of Banned” titles. By March 2026, the Madani government had already banned 27 books — five in 2023 alone, and the pace has not slowed. PH in opposition screamed about BN’s censorship and promised reform of repressive laws. Now in government, they are using the same law, the same ministry, the same apparatus — just with a different letterhead and a faster U-turn when caught.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a civilised Malaysia built on intellectual openness and institutional reform. You promised to review repressive laws. You promised that the days of banning books and silencing ideas were over. Instead, your Home Ministry reflexively bans books about historical figures, panics when caught by your own MPs and civil society, and reverses course with zero accountability. Saifuddin Nasution is your party secretary-general — this is not some rogue bureaucrat, this is your own leadership. If you cannot even manage a book prohibition without tripping over your own contradictions within a week, how can Malaysians trust you with actual institutional reform? Repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act, or stop pretending you ever intended to.
