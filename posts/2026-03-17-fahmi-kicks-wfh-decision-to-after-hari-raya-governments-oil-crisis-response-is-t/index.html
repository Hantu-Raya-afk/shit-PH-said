Fahmi Kicks WFH Decision to After Hari Raya — Government’s Oil Crisis Response Is to Study, Review, Then Delay

2026-03-17
#Politics 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister (Government Spokesperson)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-17

📌 What Happened#

On March 17, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil told reporters after a Cabinet meeting that a final decision on implementing work-from-home (WFH) arrangements for the public sector will only come after the Hari Raya Aidilfitri holidays. Fahmi said ministers had reviewed an ‘initial report’ on flexible working arrangements but that ‘several aspects still need to be refined.’ The matter will be referred to the National Economic Action Council (MTEN) via the Chief Secretary to the Government for a post-Raya decision. This WFH proposal was first floated as an austerity measure in response to the oil crisis triggered by the US-Iran conflict in West Asia, after PM Anwar directed the Chief Secretary and PSD director-general to study it.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is the government’s flagship ‘austerity’ response to an oil crisis that is bleeding RM3.2 billion a month in fuel subsidies — and after weeks of buildup, the answer is: we’ll decide later. Thailand implemented public sector WFH on March 3. The Philippines followed days later. Malaysia’s Cabinet sat on it for a week, received a report, and punted it past the holidays. The pattern is unmistakable: announce a study, review the study, delay the decision, repeat. Meanwhile, civil servants still commute on subsidised fuel, businesses plead for relief, and the government’s only concrete crisis response remains telling the rakyat to ‘reduce energy usage.’

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The WFH policy is not rocket science. It’s a logistical coordination exercise that governments across Southeast Asia executed within days of the crisis escalating. Malaysia has done it before — during the ASEAN Summit in October 2025, certain civil servants worked from home to ease traffic congestion. The infrastructure exists. The precedent exists. What doesn’t exist is the political will to make a decision before a holiday. Fahmi’s assurance that ‘frontline services won’t be affected’ only underscores how uncontroversial this should be — if healthcare and education are excluded, what exactly needs weeks more of ‘refinement’? The answer: nothing. This is bureaucratic delay dressed up as due diligence.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is textbook Madani governance: announce with urgency, study with leisure. The PM tenure limit bill was promised, tabled, and then failed in Parliament because the government couldn’t whip its own coalition. The AG-PP separation bill faced revolt from PKR’s own MPs. The Ombudsman Bill and Freedom of Information Bill, promised in January, remain in legislative limbo. On the economic front, Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah ruled out a stimulus package on March 16, telling Malaysians to ‘reduce energy usage’ instead. Deputy Education Minister Wong Kah Woh floated bringing back online schooling (PdPR) as a cost-saving measure the same week. The crisis playbook is consistent: talk about action, delay action, hope the crisis resolves itself.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised a ‘reformist, responsive government.’ You told Malaysians you were different from the governments that came before — faster, more accountable, more competent. A regional oil crisis hits, your neighbours act within days, and your response after three weeks is ‘we’ll decide after Raya.’ Every day of delay is another day of RM100+ million in avoidable fuel subsidy costs. If this is what ‘MADANI governance’ looks like under pressure — study committees, initial reports, post-holiday timelines — then the slogan was never about reform. It was about optics.
