Saifuddin’s Home Ministry Bans Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Scrambles to Reverse It After PH’s Own Coalition Revolts

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry — led by PKR’s Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — issued prohibition orders against two books, declaring them a danger to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” One of the banned books was a memoir of the grandmother of a political ally of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. A separate ban was also placed on a Malay-language translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong, which had been funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government. Civil society groups including Article 19 and Gerak Budaya immediately condemned the bans. Gerak Budaya, the independent publisher that received the prohibition order, announced it would challenge the decision through legal channels.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is the Madani government using the same authoritarian playbook it once opposed. The Printing Presses and Publications Act — the legal instrument behind these bans — is a colonial-era law that PH promised to reform or repeal. Instead, Saifuddin’s ministry wielded it against books about history, not security threats. The backlash came not just from NGOs but from within PH’s own coalition, with ruling party MPs publicly questioning the basis for the ban. When your own side revolts over a censorship decision, it signals that the reformist brand is cracking from the inside. This is the latest in a pattern of the Anwar government tilting rightward to court conservative Malay voters, as analysts told the South China Morning Post.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A government led by a man who spent years in prison — partly on politically motivated charges — is now banning books about history because they contain “communist elements.” The Cold War ended three decades ago. The Communist Party of Malaya disbanded in 1989. Anwar built his political identity on openness, intellectual freedom, and reform. His Home Affairs Minister then bans a grandmother’s memoir and an academic text, only to scramble to reverse course days later after the backlash became politically inconvenient. The reversal doesn’t redeem the decision — it proves it was never about national security. It was about performative conservatism, clumsily executed and immediately abandoned when the cost became apparent.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long, ugly history of book bans under the PPPA. The BN government banned books for decades — from works on the Malayan Emergency to Islamist texts to comic books deemed subversive. PH, while in opposition, routinely condemned these bans as authoritarian overreach. During its brief 2018-2020 government, PH signalled it would reform media laws, but never got around to repealing the PPPA. Now, in its second stint in power, PH is not just failing to repeal the law — it is actively using it. The April 2026 ban-then-reverse mirrors the same pattern seen under previous governments: ban first, think later, reverse when embarrassed.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a civilisation built on openness, knowledge, and reform. You promised to repeal oppressive laws. Instead, your Home Affairs Minister is banning history books about grandmothers and academics, using the same colonial-era law that was used to silence your own supporters. The reversal doesn’t earn you credit — it exposes the ban as politically motivated and intellectually bankrupt. If you believe in ideas, stop restricting them. Repeal the PPPA or stop pretending you’re different from the governments you replaced.
