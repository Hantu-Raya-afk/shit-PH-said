Johari Confirms Malaysia-US Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — PH Spent Political Capital Defending a Deal That No Longer Exists

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed on March 15 that the Malaysia-US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) is “null and void.” The deal, signed by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and President Donald Trump on October 26, 2025, was rendered inapplicable after the US Supreme Court in February 2026 struck down Trump’s broad tariff measures as unconstitutional. “It is not on hold. It is no longer there, it’s null and void,” Johari told reporters. The US is now pivoting to Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, imposing a temporary 10% tariff, with Section 301 investigations launched into 60 countries — including Malaysia — covering excess capacity, forced labour, and environmental violations.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The ART was sold to Malaysians as a diplomatic masterstroke — proof that PH could manage the Trump administration and protect Malaysia’s RM233 billion in annual exports to the US. The government spent months defending the deal against critics who called it lop-sided and favourable to Washington. Four PKR MPs even urged the government to suspend ratification after the Supreme Court ruling. Now the entire framework is gone, and Malaysia is back to square one — except this time facing potentially harsher scrutiny under Section 301, which targets forced labour practices and dumping. The political capital PH invested in this deal has evaporated alongside the agreement itself.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Let’s be clear about what happened. PH negotiated Malaysia’s tariff exposure down from 47% to 24% to 19% under the ART — and celebrated it as a win. But 19% is still 19%. The deal maintained significant tariffs on Malaysian goods while granting zero-tariff access to only 1,711 products. Critics within PH’s own coalition flagged it as unbalanced. Now it’s void, and Malaysia faces a blanket 10% tariff under Section 122, with the threat of worse under Section 301. The government didn’t secure a durable trade framework — it signed a deal built on the shaky legal foundations of Trump’s executive overreach, and now it has nothing to show for it except a photo op in Kuala Lumpur.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time a Malaysian government has been caught flat-footed by US trade policy shifts. During the original Trump tariff wars in 2018-2019, Malaysia scrambled to diversify its export markets but made limited structural progress. The BN government faced similar criticism for its handling of TPP/CPTPP negotiations. PH, which came to power in 2022 promising a more strategic foreign policy, initially resisted Trump’s tariff demands before ultimately signing the ART under pressure. The pattern is consistent: Malaysia reacts to US trade policy rather than building durable, diversified trade relationships that insulate the economy from Washington’s political cycles.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians the ART was a smart deal. You told them you negotiated hard. You told them 19% was a victory. Now the deal is dead, Section 301 is coming, and Malaysia’s RM233 billion export relationship with the US is more vulnerable than before you signed anything. Three years of “reformasi” trade policy, and your signature achievement with Washington is a nullified agreement and an incoming investigation into forced labour in Malaysian supply chains. If PH had spent half the energy on structural reforms — cleaning up labour practices, diversifying export markets, building genuine trade resilience — that it spent defending this deal, Malaysia would be in a far stronger position today. Instead, you bet on Trump’s legal authority holding up, and you lost.
