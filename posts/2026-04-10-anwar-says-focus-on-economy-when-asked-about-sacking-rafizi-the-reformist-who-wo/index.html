Anwar Says ‘Focus on Economy’ When Asked About Sacking Rafizi — The Reformist Who Won’t Reform His Own Party

2026-04-10
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister / PKR President

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-10

📌 What Happened#

On April 10, PKR president and Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim was asked by reporters whether the party should sack former deputy president Rafizi Ramli, who had just received a second show-cause letter from PKR’s disciplinary board and publicly challenged the party to expel him. Anwar brushed aside the question, saying: “Nevermind, I just think everyone should calm down as focusing on the economic problem is more important than on a personal problem.” This came after months of Rafizi openly accusing Anwar of surrounding himself with “yes-men,” protecting vested interests, defending MACC chief Azam Baki, and failing to deliver on reforms. Human Resources Minister Ramanan had already called Rafizi a “failed minister” with an “unhealthy agenda to divide the party.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

Anwar built his entire political career on the principle that internal dissent is healthy and that leaders must be held accountable — from his defiance of Mahathir in 1998 to the Reformasi movement. Now, when his own former deputy raises substantive criticisms about cronyism, MACC abuse, and a culture of sycophancy within PKR, Anwar’s response is to wave it away as a “personal problem” and tell everyone to calm down. This isn’t statesmanship — it’s avoidance. The message to every PKR member is clear: criticise the president at your own risk, and don’t expect an answer on the substance.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Rafizi’s criticisms are not “personal problems.” He alleged that MACC officers targeted PKR candidates who challenged Anwar’s daughter Nurul Izzah in party elections. He accused the government of extending Azam Baki’s contract despite abuse reports. He called out a “culture of sycophancy” that silences internal reform voices. These are institutional and governance concerns — exactly the kind of issues Anwar built a movement around. Dismissing them as personal grievances while the party’s disciplinary board issues show-cause letters is the opposite of reform. You can’t champion free speech and accountability in public while punishing it in private.

📜 Previous Cases#

PKR has a long and ugly history of purging internal critics. Azmin Ali’s faction was expelled after the Sheraton Move. Rafizi himself was once sidelined before being brought back when Anwar needed his support base. The pattern is consistent: dissent is tolerated when it serves the president’s interests and crushed when it doesn’t. CNA analysts noted that sacking Rafizi could backfire given his sizable support among PKR MPs and grassroots, which explains the paralysis — Anwar can’t discipline Rafizi without fracturing the party, but he can’t address his criticisms without admitting they have merit.

🎯 PH Callout#

Anwar Ibrahim, you spent 25 years telling Malaysians that leaders who silence critics are authoritarians. You told us Reformasi meant accountability from the top. Now your former economy minister — the man you handpicked — is raising specific, documented concerns about MACC abuse, cronyism, and a yes-man culture in your party, and your response is “calm down, focus on the economy.” If Rafizi’s allegations are false, prove it. If they’re true, act on them. “Let’s see” and “calm down” are not reform. They’re what every Malaysian PM has said before you.
