Fahmi Calls RM10 Billion Budget Slash ‘Streamlining’ — Budget 2026 Won’t Be Re-Tabled Despite Subsidy Bill Ballooning 290%

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister & Government Spokesperson

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, 2026, Communications Minister and government spokesperson Fahmi Fadzil confirmed at a weekly press briefing that all federal ministries had been ordered by the Finance Ministry to review and cut their operating expenditures for 2026. The directive, sent by Treasury Secretary-General Johan Mahmood Merican, proposed RM10 billion in savings — including RM3.06 billion from the Health Ministry and RM2.39 billion from Higher Education — after the government’s subsidy bill ballooned from RM15 billion originally budgeted to a projected RM58.4 billion, driven by fuel subsidies surging from RM700 million to RM7 billion per month due to the Iran-US conflict disrupting oil supplies through the Strait of Hormuz.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not routine belt-tightening — it is emergency austerity masquerading as administrative efficiency. The government’s entire fiscal framework for 2026 has been blown apart. Health and education, the two pillars PH promised to protect, are taking the biggest absolute cuts. Fahmi’s insistence that Budget 2026 does not need to be re-tabled in Parliament is a deliberate attempt to avoid legislative scrutiny of what amounts to a complete rewrite of national spending priorities. When your subsidy bill increases by 290%, your budget is no longer your budget — but PH wants to pretend Parliament doesn’t need to know.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Fahmi called this “streamlining programmes and their implementation.” That’s spin so thick you could spread it on roti canai. When you slash RM3 billion from healthcare during a cost-of-living crisis, that’s not streamlining — that’s austerity with a PR makeover. The Finance Ministry’s own assurance that “essential services won’t be affected” is meaningless when the cuts target exactly the ministries that deliver essential services. PH came to power promising transparency, fiscal responsibility, and parliamentary accountability. Now they’re rewriting the budget by executive directive and telling the public it’s just housekeeping.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long history of governments avoiding parliamentary re-tabling of budgets when fiscal assumptions collapse. The Najib administration similarly resisted re-tabling when oil prices crashed in 2014-2015, preferring to manage cuts through executive orders — the exact playbook PH now follows. Fahmi himself, when in opposition, was among the loudest voices demanding fiscal transparency and parliamentary oversight of government spending. The irony is structural: every Malaysian government discovers the convenience of executive budget management once it’s in power.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised “Malaysia Madani” — a government of transparency, good governance, and institutional reform. You promised that Parliament would be the centre of accountability. Now you’re cutting RM10 billion from the national budget without going back to the Dewan Rakyat, and your spokesperson calls it “streamlining.” If the subsidy bill has quadrupled, if healthcare and education are losing billions, if the entire fiscal basis of Budget 2026 has collapsed — Parliament deserves to know, debate, and decide. Anything less is the same executive overreach you spent decades railing against.
