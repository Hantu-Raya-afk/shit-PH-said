Saifuddin Nasution’s Ministry Bans Books on Anti-Colonial History, Then U-Turns After Backlash — 27 Books Banned Under ‘Reformist’ MADANI in 2026 Alone

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry (KDN), headed by PKR’s Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, issued a prohibition order banning two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Regimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Regimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — citing “communist elements and ideologies” that allegedly threatened public order and national security. Shamsiah Fakeh was a Malay nationalist, feminist, and anti-colonial fighter whose memoir was first published by UKM in 2004 — over two decades ago. By March 2026, Malaysia had already banned 27 books under the MADANI government, surpassing the 24 books banned in all of 2025. After immediate backlash from civil society groups, PH’s own lawmakers — including Bandar Utama assemblyperson Jamaliah Jamaluddin, Shamsiah’s granddaughter — and international press freedom organisations like ARTICLE 19, Saifuddin announced on April 23 that he was “in the process of revoking” the ban on three books, but only after the damage to Malaysia’s reformist image was done.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not an isolated incident — it is a pattern. The MADANI government, which came to power promising democratic reform, transparency, and respect for civil liberties, is now banning more books per year than its predecessors. The use of the Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 — a colonial-era law that gives the Home Minister “absolute discretion” to ban publications — directly contradicts PH’s 2022 election promises to repeal repressive legislation. When your own coalition MPs are publicly embarrassing your ministry for censorship, and when international human rights organisations are issuing formal condemnations, the “reformist” branding is no longer credible.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A government that claims to champion “Malaysia MADANI” — civil Malaysia — does not ban books about its own history. Shamsiah Fakeh fought the British. Her memoir has been available for 22 years. The idea that a 2004 academic publication suddenly threatens “national security” in 2026 is not just absurd — it’s insulting. The real reason is political cowardice: the PH government, desperate to out-conservative its Malay-Muslim rivals in PAS and PN, is signalling ideological compliance to the right-wing base by dusting off Cold War-era censorship tools. The U-turn after backlash only proves the ban was never about principle — it was reflex authoritarianism, corrected only when the PR cost became unbearable.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a long pattern of Malaysian governments weaponising the Printing Presses and Publications Act. Under BN, books on May 13, reformasi, and LGBTQ+ issues were banned. Under Muhyiddin’s PN government, censorship continued. PH promised to be different. They are not. In 2025, the MADANI government banned 24 books. In 2026, they exceeded that number by March. Gerakbudaya, the publisher affected, maintains a “Hall of Banned” documenting decades of censorship — and PH is now a prolific contributor. The minister’s “I’ll personally review future bans” pledge is performative: the structural problem is the law itself, which PH promised to repeal but has instead weaponised.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised to repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act. You promised institutional reform. You promised to protect academic freedom and civil liberties. Instead, you are banning more books than the governments you replaced. Saifuddin’s U-turn is not accountability — it is embarrassment management. If you cannot even protect a 22-year-old memoir about a Malay independence hero from your own ministry’s censors, what exactly does “Malaysia MADANI” mean? Repeal the law. Stop the bans. Or stop pretending to be reformists.
