Fahmi Delays WFH Decision for Civil Servants Until After Hari Raya — The Government That Told You to ‘Reduce Energy Usage’ Can’t Even Decide on Working From Home

2026-03-17
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister (Government Spokesperson)

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-17

📌 What Happened#

On March 17, Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil announced after a post-Cabinet meeting that the government’s decision on implementing work-from-home for civil servants would be delayed until after Hari Raya Aidilfitri. This came just four days after FMT reported on March 13 that the decision was supposed to be made on Tuesday, March 17. Fahmi said “several aspects still need to be refined” and the matter would go to the National Economic Action Council (MTEN) via the Chief Secretary to the Government. He stressed frontline services like healthcare and education would not be affected.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Malaysia is in the middle of an oil crisis triggered by the US-Iran conflict. Global fuel prices are surging, the government is spending RM3.2 billion a month on subsidies, and Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah just told Malaysians to “reduce energy usage.” Neighbouring countries — Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines — have already rolled out WFH arrangements for their public sectors. Malaysia’s government, which employs 1.7 million civil servants, cannot even make this basic administrative decision without kicking it past a holiday. This is not a complex policy reform. It is a scheduling decision that other nations made weeks ago.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The government set its own deadline — March 17 — and then missed it. Not because the policy is complicated, but because “several aspects still need to be refined.” This is the same government that told the rakyat to cut energy consumption, the same government that asked companies to let employees work from home, and the same government that had Wong Kah Woh floating PdPR for students two days earlier. They can tell everyone else to adapt, but can’t make a decision about their own workforce before a holiday break. The message is clear: urgency is for you, not for them.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a well-established Madani pattern of performative urgency followed by bureaucratic delay. The PM term limit bill was supposed to be a “quick win” — it failed by two votes on March 2 because the government couldn’t even ensure its own MPs showed up. Amir Hamzah’s response to the oil crisis was to “monitor developments.” The Sabah 40% revenue ruling was declared “case closed” while the government simultaneously filed court challenges. PH governs by announcement, not execution.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told the rakyat to “reduce energy usage.” You asked the private sector to implement WFH. You floated sending kids back to Zoom school. But when it came to your own 1.7 million civil servants, you couldn’t make a decision before cuti Raya? Thailand decided. Vietnam decided. The Philippines decided. Malaysia’s government — the one that promised to lead by example — decided to decide later. Selamat Hari Raya. The rakyat will be tightening their belts while Putrajaya takes a holiday from its own crisis management.
