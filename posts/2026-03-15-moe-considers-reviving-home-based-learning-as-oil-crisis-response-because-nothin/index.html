MOE Considers Reviving Home-Based Learning as Oil Crisis Response — Because Nothing Says ‘Austerity’ Like Sending Kids Back to Their Laptops

2026-03-15
#Education 

Said by: Mohd Azam Ahmad, Education Director-General (under Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek)

Category: Education

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, Education Director-General Datuk Dr Mohd Azam Ahmad announced the Ministry of Education will hold a special meeting next week, chaired by Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek, to review the implementation of Work From Home and Home-Based Teaching and Learning (PdPR) mechanisms as part of the government’s austerity response to the global oil crisis. When asked whether the savings policy would extend to schools and students — effectively reviving the COVID-era PdPR system — Mohd Azam said “further study is required” and that the ministry would “examine whether there is a rationale to involve schools.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

PdPR was one of the most damaging policies of the pandemic era, widening the digital divide, crushing learning outcomes for B40 students, and triggering widespread parental burnout. MOE’s own data from 2021–2022 showed alarming learning losses. Now, three years into PH governance, the government is floating the possibility of reviving it — not because of a pandemic, but because of an oil crisis the administration failed to prepare for. If PdPR returns, it will hit the same communities PH promised to uplift: the rural poor, single-income families, and students without reliable internet access.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The government is treating an economic crisis like a public health emergency, reaching for the same blunt instruments that traumatised millions of students and families during COVID. The DG’s language is pure bureaucratic deflection: “further study,” “various angles,” “ensure effective rollout.” Translation: nobody has a plan, but nobody wants to say no to the PM’s austerity directive either. Meanwhile, Thailand mandated WFH on March 10 and Vietnam followed immediately — Malaysia is still “reviewing.” The idea that shutting schools saves money is the kind of logic that only makes sense if you’ve never calculated the long-term cost of educational disruption.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s PdPR experiment during COVID was widely documented as a failure for equity. A 2022 World Bank report flagged significant learning losses among Malaysian students, particularly in rural Sabah and Sarawak. The Education Ministry itself acknowledged gaps in digital access — in 2021, reports showed students climbing trees and hills to get internet signal. Fadhlina’s predecessor, Radzi Jidin under PN, was criticised for the same lack of preparedness. PH promised to fix education. Instead, they may be about to repeat the same mistakes, this time without even a virus as an excuse.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised “Malaysia Madani” — a civilised, progressive Malaysia that invests in its people. Reviving PdPR as an austerity measure is the opposite of that promise. If your oil crisis response is to send kids home to stare at screens they may not have, while you spend RM2 billion per month on fuel subsidies you promised to reform, then your priorities are broken. Fix the structural problems — targeted subsidies, energy transition, fiscal reform — instead of making children pay for your government’s failure to prepare.
