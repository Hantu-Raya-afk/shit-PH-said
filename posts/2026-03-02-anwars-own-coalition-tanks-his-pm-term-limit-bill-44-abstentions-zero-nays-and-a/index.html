Anwar’s Own Coalition Tanks His PM Term Limit Bill — 44 Abstentions, Zero ‘Nays’, And A Reform Agenda That Can’t Survive Its Own Allies

2026-03-02
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-02

📌 What Happened#

On March 2, 2026, Malaysia’s lower house narrowly rejected the Constitution (Amendment) Bill 2026, which would have limited the prime minister’s tenure to two terms or 10 years. The bill received 146 votes — just two short of the 148 needed for a two-thirds supermajority. Of the 222 MPs, 44 abstained and 32 were absent. Not a single MP voted against the bill. The reform was tabled by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim himself, who argued it was “a preventive measure to curb wrongdoing by reducing the risk of prolonged concentration of power and the possibility of corruption or abuse of trust.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not a minor legislative hiccup. Anwar commands a supermajority — the Unity Government holds more than two-thirds of the seats on paper. The failure exposes a fundamental weakness: coalition discipline has eroded to the point where the PM cannot pass his own flagship reform bill. Analysts noted this reflects “looser discipline” within the ruling bloc and signals that major reforms may be dead in the water as the next general election approaches. If Anwar cannot rally his own coalition for a bill nobody openly opposed, what hope is there for tougher reforms on procurement, anti-corruption, or institutional governance?

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar sold the Madani government on a platform of institutional reform. Term limits were supposed to be the easy win — a popular, symbolic gesture that costs sitting politicians nothing since most won’t serve 10 years anyway. And yet 44 MPs couldn’t even bother to vote yes. They didn’t oppose it publicly — that would require spine. They just stayed silent, abstained, or disappeared. This is a coalition that can pass budgets and survive confidence votes but somehow cannot muster two extra votes for a reform its own leader championed for weeks. The message is clear: PH’s reform agenda is a branding exercise, not a legislative priority.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a pattern of PH promising structural reform and delivering rhetoric instead. The Anti-Party Hopping Law (2022) was hailed as a landmark but was later weakened by court interpretation. The IPCMC (Independent Police Complaints and Misconduct Commission) was promised since PH’s 2018 manifesto and eventually shelved. Anwar himself promised a royal inquiry into MACC misconduct, then called it “premature.” Every time PH is tested on whether reform is more than a slogan, the answer has been the same: not yet, not now, maybe later.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysia that Madani meant reform. You told them this government would be different from the decades of BN rule that concentrated power without limits or accountability. And when the moment came to put one of the simplest reform bills on the books — one that YOUR coalition publicly championed, one that ZERO MPs opposed on the record — you couldn’t even whip two extra votes. If you can’t pass a bill that nobody objects to, stop pretending you have a reform agenda. You have a marketing department.
