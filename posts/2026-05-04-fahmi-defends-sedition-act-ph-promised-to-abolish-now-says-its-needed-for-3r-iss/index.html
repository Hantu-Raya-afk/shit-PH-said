Fahmi Defends Sedition Act PH Promised to Abolish — Now Says It’s ‘Needed’ for 3R Issues

2026-05-04
#Politics 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister & Government Spokesperson (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-05-04

📌 What Happened#

On May 4, 2026, Communications Minister Datuk Fahmi Fadzil defended the continued use of the Sedition Act 1948, stating it remains “necessary” for cases involving race, religion, and royalty (3R) but should not target political criticism. Speaking during a live Facebook session titled “Tanya — Jangan Sentap!”, Fahmi said PM Anwar Ibrahim was “setting a new tone” by reserving enforcement action for issues affecting public order, national unity, and the institution of the Malay Rulers — while insisting political matters should be handled through political channels.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Pakatan Harapan’s GE15 manifesto explicitly pledged to repeal the Sedition Act, a colonial-era law long weaponised against journalists, activists, academics, and opposition politicians. It was one of PH’s marquee reform promises — the kind that won them the urban vote. Now, 2.5 years into government, their own spokesperson is not only defending the law’s existence but actively rebranding its use as legitimate governance. This is not reform. This is a rebrand. The Sedition Act hasn’t changed; only PH’s rhetoric around it has.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The entire point of abolishing the Sedition Act was that governments always claim they only use it for “legitimate” purposes — to protect harmony, to defend the Rulers, to preserve unity. Every administration before PH said the same thing. UMNO said the same thing. The BN government said the same thing when it charged Anwar’s own allies under the very same Act. PH’s promise wasn’t to “use the Sedition Act more responsibly” — it was to repeal it, because the law itself is the problem. Fahmi dressing it up as “we only use it for 3R” is exactly the kind of authoritarian housekeeping PH was supposed to dismantle, not inherit.

📜 Previous Cases#

In October 2024, Anwar himself defended his administration’s human rights record in an interview with Mehdi Hasan on Zeteo, acknowledging that Sedition Act investigations had increased under his government but claiming the law was used to protect the constitutional monarchy. Before that, PH’s own leaders — including Lim Kit Siang, Mat Sabu, and multiple PKR MPs — were themselves charged under the Sedition Act by BN-era prosecutors. The irony is not subtle: the party that bled under the Sedition Act now bleeds others with it.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised to repeal the Sedition Act. It was in your manifesto. Your leaders called it a “draconian relic.” Your activists were jailed under it. Now you’re its most articulate defenders. If you believe the Sedition Act is necessary, say so plainly and retract the promise. If you still believe it should be repealed, then why is your Communications Minister on Facebook telling the nation it’s needed? You cannot be the party of reform and the party of selective enforcement at the same time. Pick one.
