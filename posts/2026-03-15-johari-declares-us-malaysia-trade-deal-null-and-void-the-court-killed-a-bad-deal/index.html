Johari Declares US-Malaysia Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — The Court Killed a Bad Deal Malaysia Should Never Have Signed

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) signed between Malaysia and the United States in October 2025 is now “null and void.” The deal collapsed after the US Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that President Donald Trump’s reciprocal tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) were unconstitutional. “It is not on hold. It’s null and void,” Johari told reporters after handing out Hari Raya Aidilfitri aid to constituents. The US has since pivoted to Section 122 of its Trade Act 1974, imposing a uniform 10% tariff on all trading partners, and launched Section 301 investigations into 15 countries including Malaysia over excess production capacity and forced labour.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This deal was the centrepiece of the Anwar government’s US trade strategy — signed with great fanfare when Trump visited Kuala Lumpur for the ASEAN summit in October 2025. Under the ART, Malaysia accepted tariffs of 19% on its goods (down from an initial 47%) in exchange for market access concessions. Four PKR MPs publicly urged Putrajaya to suspend ratification even before the court ruling. Now the deal is dead, and Malaysia gets the same 10% tariff as every other country — meaning all the concessions Malaysia offered bought nothing. Perikatan Nasional is demanding a special parliamentary sitting to scrutinise what commitments were made and what concessions were given away for a deal that no longer exists.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Johari is framing this as a clean break — “null and void,” case closed. But the question nobody in government wants to answer is: why did Malaysia sign a lopsided deal that maintained 19% tariffs while giving away market access and policy concessions? The deal didn’t collapse because Malaysia stood up for itself. It collapsed because an American court did what the Madani government wouldn’t — call out Trump’s trade bullying as illegal. And Malaysia isn’t free from US trade pressure: Section 301 investigations into forced labour and excess capacity are already underway, targeting the same industries — electronics, palm oil, gloves — that PH promised to reform. Three years of “reformasi” trade policy, and the best outcome was a bad deal dying on its own.

📜 Previous Cases#

This isn’t Malaysia’s first rodeo with lopsided trade pressure. Under previous administrations, Malaysia faced US Withhold Release Orders on rubber gloves and palm oil over forced labour allegations — restrictions that cost billions in market access. PH came to power promising institutional reform and a more assertive trade posture. Instead, the Anwar government signed the ART under pressure, with Tengku Zafrul leading negotiations that even PH’s own backbenchers called unfair. The pattern is consistent: Malaysia negotiates from weakness, accepts bad terms, and then claims victory when circumstances change.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised Malaysia would stand tall on the world stage. Instead, you signed a trade deal that your own MPs called lopsided, accepted 19% tariffs while giving away concessions, and now celebrate because an American court cleaned up your mess. The deal is dead — but the concessions you offered, the precedents you set, and the institutional weakness you showed are all still alive. Perikatan is right to demand answers. What exactly did Malaysia give away? And what is the plan now that the US is launching Section 301 investigations into forced labour — the same forced labour problem PH promised to fix three years ago? Stop framing luck as strategy.
