Saifuddin Nasution Bans Books on ‘Communist Ideologies’ Under Malaysia’s ‘Reformist’ Government — Then U-Turns After His Own Coalition Revolts

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry, led by PKR’s Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, banned two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — citing communist ideologies and national security threats. A third book, a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was also banned. The Shamsiah Fakeh memoir recounts the life of a Malay feminist who fought British colonialism. After immediate backlash from civil society groups and PH’s own coalition members, Saifuddin announced on April 23 that the ban on all three books would be revoked.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not a one-off embarrassment — it is a pattern. Malaysia has banned 27 books as of March 2026, already exceeding the 24 books banned in all of 2025, under a government that campaigned on democratic reform, transparency, and civil liberties. The ban-then-reverse cycle exposes a government that reflexively censors first and thinks later, raising serious questions about whether Anwar’s MADANI framework is anything more than branding. International observers, including DW, SCMP, and Article 19, have flagged the trend as evidence of democratic backsliding under a supposedly reformist administration.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is Anwar Ibrahim’s government banning a memoir about a Malay feminist anti-colonial fighter — and a Mao Zedong translation funded by his own party’s state government — on the grounds of “communist ideology.” The same Anwar who built his career lecturing at Georgetown, Oxford, and Johns Hopkins on democratic values. PKR MP Tan Kar Hing had to publicly remind his own party president of his academic credentials. The reversal a week later does not redeem the action — it confirms that the ban was never based on genuine national security concerns but was a performative nod to conservative Malay sentiment, abandoned the moment it became politically costly.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s Printing Presses and Publications Act — a colonial-era law — has been weaponised by every government since independence. Under BN, books on May 13, reformasi, and governance were routinely banned. PH promised to reform or repeal the Act when they first took power in 2018 but never did. Now in their second stint, they are using the same law at an accelerated rate. Gerakbudaya, the publisher of both banned books, maintains a “Hall of Banned” — a growing catalogue of titles suppressed by successive Malaysian governments, now including one that claims to champion civil liberties.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia MADANI — a civilised Malaysia built on respect, compassion, and trust. Banning books about anti-colonial history, then scrambling to undo the ban when your own MPs revolt, is not civilised governance. It is cowardice dressed as caution. If you cannot even protect the intellectual freedom of your own coalition’s academic output, what exactly are you reforming? Repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act — the one you promised to repeal in 2018 — or stop pretending you are any different from the governments you replaced.
