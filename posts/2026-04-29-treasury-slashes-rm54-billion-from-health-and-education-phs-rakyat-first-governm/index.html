Treasury Slashes RM5.4 Billion From Health and Education — PH’s ‘Rakyat First’ Government Cuts Where It Hurts Most

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister & Finance Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, 2026, Malaysia’s Treasury issued a directive proposing RM5.4 billion in operating budget cuts targeting the Ministry of Health (RM3.06 billion) and the Ministry of Higher Education (RM2.39 billion). The cuts are part of a broader RM10 billion savings target across all federal agencies, triggered by the fiscal strain of the Iran war — specifically, ballooning fuel subsidy costs estimated at RM7 billion monthly. Ministries must submit revised budgets by May 15 to the National Budget Office. Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s Treasury framed this as “fiscal sustainability” — but the targets tell a different story about whose welfare this government actually prioritises.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Health and education are not discretionary line items — they are the foundation of human capital and social mobility, the very things PH promised to protect. Economists have warned that cutting healthcare during a cost-of-living crisis, when public hospitals are already understaffed and overworked, risks long-term damage to the nation’s health outcomes. SUHAKAM itself issued a statement urging a rights-based approach, calling the proposed reductions “particularly concerning.” The government that promised Malaysia Madani — a civilisational state — is now hollowing out the institutions that make civilisation function.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH came to power promising better healthcare, expanded education access, and fiscal responsibility. Now, faced with external shocks, the Treasury’s first instinct is to cut the two ministries that serve the rakyat most directly. The fuel subsidy — which the government itself acknowledged was poorly targeted — continues to bleed billions monthly, yet the structural reform Anwar promised (targeted subsidies) remains unfinished after 2.5 years. Instead of fixing the subsidy leak, they’re plugging it by draining the hospital and the university. That’s not fiscal management — that’s fiscal cowardice.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not PH’s first retreat from its own reform promises. The targeted fuel subsidy — announced, delayed, re-announced, and delayed again — has become the defining symbol of this administration’s inability to execute hard policy. Meanwhile, the RM710 million PACE employment package announced on May 1 looks generous on paper but pales against the RM5.4 billion being quietly stripped from health and education. Malaysia has a long history of governments prioritising politically convenient spending over structural investment — PH was supposed to be different.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians you would build a better country — not just a cheaper one. You promised that under Madani, the rakyat would come first. But when the fiscal pressure hit, the first things you cut were hospitals and universities. Not bloated ministries. Not duplicative agencies. Not the political patronage networks that drain billions. Health and education — the places where the poorest Malaysians have nowhere else to go. If this is your idea of ‘rakyat didahulukan,’ then you have no idea what that phrase means.
