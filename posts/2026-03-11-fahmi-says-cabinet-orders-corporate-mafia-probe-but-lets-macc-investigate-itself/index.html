Fahmi Says Cabinet Orders ‘Corporate Mafia’ Probe — But Lets MACC Investigate Itself and Ignores RCI Calls

2026-03-11
#Politics 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-11

📌 What Happened#

On March 11, Communications Minister Datuk Fahmi Fadzil announced that the Cabinet had ordered enforcement agencies — including the police (PDRM), Securities Commission (SC), Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission (MACC), and Inland Revenue Board (LHDN) — to carry out a “detailed probe” into allegations of a “corporate mafia” network operating within MACC. The order came weeks after Bloomberg published a damning investigation titled Who’s Watching Malaysia’s Anti-Corruption Watchdog? alleging that MACC officers had pressured corporate figures in takeover cases. Notably absent from Fahmi’s announcement: any mention of a Royal Commission of Inquiry (RCI), despite growing calls from MPs — including PH’s own Teresa Kok.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The “corporate mafia” allegations strike at the heart of Malaysia’s anti-corruption infrastructure. If MACC officers were weaponising their powers for private corporate interests, every investigation the commission has run is tainted by association. The Cabinet’s response — ordering the same agencies, including MACC itself, to investigate — is an institutional conflict of interest so obvious it barely needs explaining. When your anti-corruption body is accused of corruption, letting it participate in its own probe is not accountability. It is theatre.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Fahmi’s announcement was a masterclass in looking busy while doing nothing structural. The Cabinet “noted” calls for an RCI and then proceeded to ignore them. Teresa Kok — a six-term PH MP from Seputeh — publicly stated that an RCI “provides greater transparency, accountability and public confidence” and is “the most credible mechanism” for allegations of this scale. Her own government’s response? A multi-agency task force where one of the agencies under investigation is also doing the investigating. Azam Baki, meanwhile, has sued Bloomberg for RM100 million instead of answering the substance of the allegations. The message from Putrajaya is clear: we will investigate everything except the things that matter.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a rich tradition of letting scandal investigations die in inter-agency purgatory. The 1MDB saga dragged through multiple task forces before an actual change of government forced real accountability. Azam Baki’s own share ownership scandal in 2022 was “referred” to the Securities Commission, which quietly closed the case. The Pandora Papers revelations involving Malaysian politicians led to precisely zero prosecutions. PH came to power in 2018 and again in 2022 promising to break this pattern. Instead, they have perfected it.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians a new standard of governance. You built your brand on fighting corruption. Now, when your own anti-corruption chief is accused of enabling a corporate mafia — by Bloomberg, not some anonymous blog — your answer is to let the accused agency investigate itself and sue the journalist. Teresa Kok, your own MP, told you an RCI is the credible path. You ignored her. If PH cannot even hold its own institutions accountable when the evidence is this public and this damning, then “Malaysia Madani” is just “Malaysia Biasa” with better branding.
