Anwar Lectures Corporate Malaysia on ‘Fair Wages’ — Two Days After His Treasury Slashed RM10 Billion From Health and Education

2026-05-01
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister & Finance Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim used the national Workers’ Day celebration on May 1, 2026 to publicly shame corporate Malaysia, singling out companies recording RM1-2 billion in profits that “still find it difficult to approve even a RM40 salary increase to workers.” He boasted about Malaysia’s “strong growth, record-high investments, low unemployment, and a robust ringgit performance” and demanded that the private sector match profits with compassion. This was delivered just two days after his own Treasury, on April 29, issued a directive ordering all federal ministries to slash their 2026 operating budgets — including RM3.06 billion from the Health Ministry and RM2.39 billion from Higher Education — as part of a RM10 billion austerity package triggered by the Iran war’s impact on fuel subsidies.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The juxtaposition is staggering. The Prime Minister stood at a podium lecturing the private sector about caring for workers while his own government had just signed off on the largest budget cut to public health and education in recent memory. SUHAKAM warned that the health cuts risk “exacerbating existing gaps in access, quality, and equity in healthcare services.” CodeBlue reported the Health Ministry would be left with less than its 2025 allocation. DAP’s own Lim Guan Eng warned the cuts could “jeopardise public health, education, safety and crime prevention.” When your own coalition partner is sounding the alarm, the Workers’ Day sermon about corporate compassion rings hollow.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar wants corporate Malaysia to find RM40 per worker while his government can’t protect RM3 billion for public hospitals. The hypocrisy isn’t subtle — it’s structural. The man who controls the federal purse as both PM and Finance Minister chose to cut healthcare for millions of Malaysians who depend on public hospitals, then pivoted 48 hours later to moralize about private sector greed. The budget cuts aren’t some abstract fiscal exercise: university hospitals that already raised fees by up to 233% in 2025 now face further funding reductions. Workers don’t just need higher wages — they need affordable healthcare and education that their government is actively defunding.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is a recurring pattern. Anwar has consistently used public platforms to project empathy while implementing policies that contradict the message. He urged “modest Aidilfitri celebrations” while presiding over a RM58.4 billion subsidy bill that ballooned 290% from the original RM15 billion allocation. He warned Malaysians to “prepare for gradual rises in daily expenses” in April while offering no structural safety net beyond telling people to budget better. The Workers’ Day speech follows the same script: acknowledge the problem, blame someone else (this time corporates), and avoid accountability for the government’s own choices.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised MADANI would put the rakyat first. You promised fiscal responsibility. You promised that unlike previous administrations, your government would protect the vulnerable. Then you cut RM3 billion from health and RM2.4 billion from education — the two sectors that matter most to the B40 families you claim to champion — and used Workers’ Day to lecture other people about compassion. If you want corporate Malaysia to care about workers, start by not gutting the public services those workers depend on. The podium isn’t a shield, Prime Minister. The numbers are the numbers.
