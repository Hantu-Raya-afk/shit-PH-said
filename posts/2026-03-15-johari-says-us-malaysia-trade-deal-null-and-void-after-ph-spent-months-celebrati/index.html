Johari Says US-Malaysia Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — After PH Spent Months Celebrating It as a Diplomatic Triumph

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) between Malaysia and the United States is now “null and void.” The declaration came after the US Supreme Court ruled on February 20 that President Donald Trump lacked authority under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act to impose sweeping tariffs — effectively pulling the legal foundation out from under the bilateral deal Malaysia signed with Washington in October 2025.

🔥 Why It Matters#

The ART was one of PH’s signature diplomatic achievements — or so the government told us. When the deal was inked, ministers lined up to take credit for Malaysia being among the first countries to strike a reciprocal trade agreement with the Trump administration. It was sold as proof that the Madani government could navigate great-power competition and protect Malaysian exporters. Now, barely five months later, the deal is worthless — not because of anything Malaysia did, but because the US courts found the entire tariff regime illegal. The opposition is now demanding a special parliamentary sitting to examine what commitments or concessions Malaysia may have made during negotiations, and whether any of those concessions survive the deal’s collapse.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH staked political capital on a deal built on the legal authority of a president whose own Supreme Court said he overstepped. Nobody in Cabinet apparently paused to ask: what happens if the US courts strike this down? The government celebrated a trade agreement anchored to executive overreach, and now has nothing to show for it. Worse, Johari’s public statement was matter-of-fact — as if a voided international trade agreement is routine housekeeping. No accountability for the months of diplomatic effort, no explanation of what Malaysia may have conceded in negotiations, and no acknowledgment that the entire exercise was built on sand.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time a PH government has been caught flat-footed by international developments it failed to anticipate. The Madani administration’s track record on trade policy has been reactive rather than strategic — from the delayed response to EU deforestation regulations affecting palm oil, to the scramble over US forced labour allegations in Malaysian supply chains. The pattern is consistent: celebrate the headline, ignore the fine print, and scramble when reality catches up.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians this deal proved you could play on the world stage. You paraded it as evidence of your diplomatic credibility. Now it’s gone, and your response is a shrug. Where is the post-mortem? Where is the parliamentary briefing? What did Malaysia offer in those negotiations — and do those offers still bind us even though the deal doesn’t? The rakyat deserves answers, not a one-line confirmation that the agreement is dead. If you want to be taken seriously as economic managers, start by taking your own failures seriously.
