Johari Declares US-Malaysia Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — After PH Spent Months Defending the Lopsided Deal It Signed

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On 15 March 2026, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) between Malaysia and the United States is now ’null and void.’ The deal collapsed after a US Supreme Court ruling on 20 February 2026 struck down the broad reciprocal tariffs imposed under the Trump administration’s International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). ‘It is not on hold. It is no longer there, it’s null and void,’ Johari told reporters. The ART was signed on 26 October 2025 in Kuala Lumpur by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and then-President Donald Trump, with former MITI Minister Tengku Zafrul leading the negotiations for Malaysia.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not a story about good governance. This is a story about a government that signed a deal it couldn’t defend, defended a deal it couldn’t justify, and is now celebrating its death as though it had nothing to do with it. Under the ART, Malaysia traded deep market access and policy concessions to avoid tariffs that started at 47% and were negotiated down to roughly 19%, with zero-tariff privileges on 1,711 products. Critics — including Tommy Thomas, Dr Wee Ka Siong, and multiple PH backbenchers — called it the ‘worst agreement since independence’ and ‘an act of surrender.’ The deal lacked a sovereignty safeguard that even Cambodia’s version included. PH defended it for months. Now that the US Supreme Court killed it, Johari acts like Malaysia dodged a bullet — but it was PH that loaded the gun.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Johari’s ’null and void’ declaration is being framed as clarity and decisive leadership, but let’s be precise about what actually happened: Malaysia did not walk away from the deal. Malaysia did not renegotiate. Malaysia did not invoke the exit clause Anwar himself once said was available. The deal died because of an American court ruling that had nothing to do with Malaysia. And now, instead of the ART, the US is pursuing Section 301 and Section 122 investigations — covering dumping, forced labour, environmental violations, and export subsidies — that could impose new tariffs on RM233 billion worth of Malaysian exports. The ‘relief’ is temporary, the exposure is arguably worse, and PH’s fingerprints are all over the original mess.

📜 Previous Cases#

This follows a pattern. Zafrul admitted in November 2025 that the ART was lopsided, then said the ‘first draft was worse’ — as if that were a defence. Anwar himself said Malaysia could exit the deal ‘at any time,’ but never pulled the trigger. The government commissioned a cost-benefit analysis in January 2026 that flagged ‘concerns’ but recommended staying in. PH backbenchers like Wong Chen publicly called the deal ‘controversial and lopsided’ while the coalition whip kept them in line. Every step of the way, PH chose political convenience over principle — and the rakyat was told to trust the process.

🎯 PH Callout#

You signed a deal that your own allies called lopsided. You defended it when critics warned it compromised sovereignty. You commissioned a study that said it was problematic and then ignored the findings. You promised an exit clause but never used it. And now that an American court killed the deal for you, you’re acting like you planned this all along. Here’s the truth: you didn’t cancel the ART — the US Supreme Court did. And the replacement — Section 301 investigations into forced labour, dumping, and environmental violations — is potentially worse for Malaysia’s export economy. If PH’s trade policy is ‘wait for foreign courts to fix our mistakes,’ then the rakyat deserves to know that’s the plan.
