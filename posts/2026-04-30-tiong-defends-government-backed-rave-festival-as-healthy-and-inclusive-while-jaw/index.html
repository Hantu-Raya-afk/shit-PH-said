Tiong Defends Government-Backed Rave Festival as ‘Healthy and Inclusive’ — While JAWI Calls It Immoral and Treasury Slashes Billions From Health

2026-04-30
#Social 

Said by: Tiong King Sing, Tourism, Arts and Culture Minister

Category: Social

Date: 2026-04-30

📌 What Happened#

Tourism, Arts and Culture Minister Datuk Seri Tiong King Sing defended the Rain Rave Water Music Festival 2026 — a government-backed three-day water rave in Bukit Bintang running April 30 to May 2 — against mounting criticism from opposition parties, civil society, and even a federal religious agency. Tiong called the backlash “false and baseless,” condemned critics on social media platform Threads as “inflammatory and defamatory,” and insisted anyone who doesn’t like the event can “simply choose not to participate.” On the same day the festival opened, the Federal Territories Islamic Religious Department (JAWI) issued a formal advisory calling the event immoral and urging organisers to review the concept. The festival is part of Visit Malaysia Year 2026.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This isn’t just a culture war skirmish — it’s a governance priorities problem. The unity government is simultaneously telling ministries to cut operating budgets, slashing RM5.4 billion from health and education, and ordering “soft austerity” across the board. Yet a government-backed rave festival in central KL gets the green light, police deployment of 500 officers, and a full-throated ministerial defence. When the Tourism Minister’s answer to public concern is “don’t come if you don’t like it” while the Health Minister is figuring out which hospitals get defunded, the government has a credibility problem on spending priorities that no amount of Visit Malaysia branding can fix.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Tiong’s response is textbook ministerial deflection: brand all criticism as politically motivated, attack the messenger, and refuse to engage with the substance. He called critics “inflammatory” — the same week JAWI, a federal government agency under the Prime Minister’s Department, publicly called his event immoral. So which is it? Is the criticism baseless, or does the government’s own religious department count as an inflammatory agitator too? Tiong’s real problem isn’t PAS or PKR — it’s that his ministry is running a party while the rest of government tells Malaysians to tighten their belts. The cognitive dissonance isn’t just tone-deaf; it’s the kind of optics that turns “we’re all in this together” austerity messaging into a punchline.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not the first time Visit Malaysia Year programming has generated controversy. The VM2020 campaign under Muhyiddin’s administration was derailed by COVID-19 after the government had already committed significant spending to tourism events. More recently, Tiong himself drew criticism for defending the Tourism Malaysia-Starbucks partnership, telling critics to stop “politicising” tourism initiatives — a pattern of dismissing legitimate public scrutiny as political interference. Malaysian governments have a long history of prioritising prestige events and splashy campaigns while basic public services deteriorate — from Najib-era mega-projects to the current coalition’s refusal to retable the budget despite a RM43 billion subsidy blowout.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a government that listens, that prioritises the rakyat’s welfare, that governs with integrity and fiscal responsibility. Instead, your Tourism Minister is spending government resources on a water rave in Bukit Bintang and calling anyone who questions it a liar, while your Finance Ministry tells hospitals to find RM3 billion in “non-critical” cuts. Your own JAWI contradicts your own minister on the same day. This isn’t governance — it’s a coalition so busy defending every individual minister’s pet project that nobody’s minding the store. If austerity means anything, it means the government leads by example. Cancel the next vanity event, redirect the resources, and stop telling Malaysians their concerns are “baseless” while you throw a party they’re paying for.
