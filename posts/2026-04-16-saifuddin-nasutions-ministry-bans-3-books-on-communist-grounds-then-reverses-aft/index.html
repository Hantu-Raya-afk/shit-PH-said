Saifuddin Nasution’s Ministry Bans 3 Books on ‘Communist’ Grounds — Then Reverses After Backlash, Proving the Madani Government Censors First, Thinks Later

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry — under PH’s Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — issued prohibition orders on two books published by independent bookstore Gerakbudaya: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Regimen ke-10 and Komrad Asi Regimen 10: Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah, both dealing with the Malayan Communist Party’s anti-colonial struggle. A third book — a Malay translation of NYU professor Rebecca Karl’s academic work on Mao Zedong — was banned around April 22. The bans were issued under the Printing Presses and Publications Act, which requires no explanation. After a week of fierce backlash from civil society, ARTICLE 19, PKR’s own MP Tan Kar Hing, and political veteran Rais Yatim, Home Minister Saifuddin announced on April 23 that the ministry was in the process of revoking all three bans and pledged to consult publishers before future bans.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This episode exposes a pattern of authoritarian reflexes inside a government that markets itself as reformist. Malaysia has banned 27 books as of March 2026 — already surpassing the 24 books banned in all of 2025, and dwarfing the 5 titles banned in 2023 and 3 in 2022. The SCMP’s May 2 analysis notes Anwar’s government has “tilted to the right” to court Malay voters, and the book bans are a visible symptom: a government that promised intellectual freedom, transparency, and “Malaysia Madani” is instead wielding colonial-era censorship laws more aggressively than its predecessors. The U-turn doesn’t redeem the action — it reveals a ministry that acts on instinct, not principle.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar Ibrahim built his entire political brand on being the champion of free thought, democratic reform, and intellectual openness. PKR’s own MP Tan Kar Hing had to publicly remind Anwar that he once lectured on these very topics. One of the banned books was the memoir of Shamsiah Fakeh — the grandmother of an Anwar ally — making the ban not just illiberal but personally absurd. Rais Yatim, a political veteran not known for radical views, said the bans “contradict Harapan’s promises and undermine Madani government credibility.” The fact that the government banned first, faced backlash, then reversed course doesn’t show responsiveness — it shows there was no policy thinking in the first place. You don’t get credit for putting out a fire you started.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long, ugly history with censorship — the Printing Presses and Publications Act dates to the colonial era and has been used by every administration to suppress inconvenient material. Under Mahathir 2.0, the promise of repealing the PPPA was floated and abandoned. Under Anwar, the trend has accelerated: DW News documented the escalation across 2023–2026, noting the Madani government is banning books at a rate that surpasses BN-era norms. ARTICLE 19 has called on Malaysia to repeal the PPPA entirely. The pattern is consistent: every Malaysian PM promises reform, then discovers censorship is a convenient tool once in power.

🎯 PH Callout#

You campaigned on “Malaysia Madani” — civil, compassionate, reformist. You promised to expand democratic space, not shrink it. You said you would repeal oppressive laws, not use them more than your predecessors. Banning academic books about history — then reversing only after international embarrassment — is not governance, it’s damage control. If your Home Ministry can ban three books in a week without consulting anyone, what does that say about your institutional discipline? If your own MPs have to remind you of your own past, what does that say about your principles? Stop using colonial-era laws to ban books about colonialism. Repeal the PPPA or admit you never intended to.
