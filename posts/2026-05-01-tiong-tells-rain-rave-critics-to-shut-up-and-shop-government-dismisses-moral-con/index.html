Tiong Tells Rain Rave Critics to Shut Up and Shop — Government Dismisses Moral Concerns Over Its Own Water Festival

2026-05-01
#Social 

Said by: Tiong King Sing, Tourism, Arts and Culture Minister

Category: Social

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

Tourism, Arts and Culture Minister Datuk Seri Tiong King Sing on May 1 told critics of the Rain Rave Water Music Festival (RRWMF) to stop “politicising” the event, which has drawn objections from religious authorities, opposition parties, and even members of the ruling coalition. The three-day water-themed music festival, held in Bukit Bintang from April 30 to May 2 as part of Visit Malaysia 2026, was co-organised by Tourism Malaysia and event company The Fame. When pressed on objections from the Federal Territories Islamic Religious Department (JAWI), which warned the event could undermine moral standards, Tiong responded: “If you feel your religion does not permit it, then do not come.” He also deployed whataboutism, pointing to a Songkran festival in PAS-led Kedah on April 18 and asking why there were no calls for that menteri besar’s resignation.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This isn’t just about a water festival — it’s about how Anwar’s government handles criticism of its own initiatives. The RRWMF was not a private event. It was co-organised by Tourism Malaysia, a government agency under Tiong’s ministry, as part of the official Visit Malaysia 2026 campaign. When citizens, religious authorities, coalition partners (Umno Youth’s Dr Akmal Saleh, PKR members), and PAS raised concerns — some about moral standards, others about the use of public resources during austerity — the government’s response was to label all criticism as “politicising.” This is the same government that preaches “Malaysia Madani” and inclusive governance. Telling people to shut up or stay home is neither civil nor inclusive.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Tiong’s “don’t come if you don’t like it” line is a masterclass in dismissiveness. The critics aren’t objecting because they personally don’t want to get wet — they’re questioning why a government agency is co-organising a water rave in the middle of KL while the same government has ordered RM5.4 billion in austerity cuts to health and education. His whataboutism comparing the event to Kedah’s Songkran is dishonest: Songkran is a recognised cultural and religious celebration of the Siamese community; the RRWMF is a commercial entertainment event. Even the Malaysian Siam Association publicly clarified that the two are not comparable. Tiong also received a memorandum calling for his resignation over the festival — and his response was to question the critics’ consistency rather than address the substance of their concerns. This is a minister who thinks accountability is a political inconvenience.

📜 Previous Cases#

Tiong King Sing has form. As Tourism Minister, he has repeatedly prioritised spectacle over substance, treating VMY2026 as a personal showcase while questions about tourism revenue leakage, contractor transparency, and event procurement processes go unanswered. The broader pattern in Anwar’s government is clear: when criticised, PH circles the wagons and frames every objection as “politicisation” — the same rhetorical move Anwar himself deployed when telling everyone to “focus on the economy” while his own Negeri Sembilan MB was losing majority support. The unity government’s playbook is consistent: deflect, dismiss, and label critics as troublemakers.

🎯 PH Callout#

You came to power promising Madani governance — civil, inclusive, responsive. Your Tourism Minister just told Malaysians that if they have moral or religious concerns about a government-funded event, they should stay home. Your own coalition partners in Umno raised questions. Your own party members in PKR raised questions. JAWI raised questions. And the government’s answer is “stop politicising”? That’s not governance — that’s arrogance. If you can’t handle scrutiny of your own events, how are you going to handle scrutiny of your RM5.4 billion in budget cuts? Madani was supposed to mean something. Right now, it means “don’t ask questions.”
