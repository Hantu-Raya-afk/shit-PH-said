Saifuddin Nasution Bans Books About Anti-Colonial History for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Reverses a Week Later After His Own Coalition Revolts

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 15-16, 2026, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — a PKR man, supposedly a reformist — signed prohibition orders banning two books published by independent publisher Gerakbudaya: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Rejimen Ke-10, a memoir of a Malay feminist anti-colonial figure and grandmother to a PH ally, and Komrad Asi Regimen 10: Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah. The Home Ministry declared both books a “danger to national security” for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” Days later, a third book — a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government — was also banned. By April 23, after fierce backlash from civil society groups, publishers, lawmakers, and members of his own PH coalition, Saifuddin announced the government was “in the process of revoking” all three bans and pledged to personally review future prohibition decisions.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not a minor bureaucratic hiccup — it is a window into PH’s institutional rot. The books in question are historical accounts of anti-colonial resistance, not propaganda manuals. Banning them under a law inherited from British colonial censors (the Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984) is exactly the kind of authoritarian overreach PH spent years in opposition condemning. That the ban was signed by a PKR Home Minister — from the party of Anwar Ibrahim, the self-proclaimed champion of reformasi — makes the hypocrisy structural, not accidental. The fact that one of the banned books was funded by PKR’s own Selangor government is almost too absurd to be real. This episode, now analyzed by SCMP and international press freedom groups as part of a broader pattern of democratic backsliding under the Madani government, erodes whatever remains of PH’s reformist credibility.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

PH rode to power on promises of press freedom, institutional reform, and intellectual openness. Anwar Ibrahim personally spent decades decrying censorship under Mahathir and Najib. Now his own Home Minister is banning books about a Malay woman who fought the British — and calling it national security. The reversal a week later doesn’t redeem anyone; it confirms the ban was indefensible from the start. Saifuddin didn’t reverse because he saw the light — he reversed because the backlash made the position politically untenable. And his promise to “personally review” future bans is not reform; it’s the centralisation of censorship power in one man’s hands. The Printing Presses and Publications Act remains on the books. No structural change. No accountability. Just a U-turn dressed up as leadership.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is part of a well-documented pattern. Human Rights Watch’s World Report 2026 flagged Malaysia’s expanding censorship apparatus under PH, including 2024 amendments to the Communications and Multimedia Act that gave officials broader power to censor online content. The government has banned books on gender, sexuality, and now history. In 2025, the Home Ministry banned a book by moderate Muslim group G25 — an act so absurd it made international headlines. PH has maintained and actively used every repressive media law it inherited: the Sedition Act (which Anwar promised to repeal), the PPPA, the CMA. The pattern is clear: promise reform in opposition, wield the same tools in power.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised reformasi. You promised to repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act. You promised a Malaysia where ideas are debated, not banned. Instead, your Home Minister signed a prohibition order on a book about a Malay grandmother who fought colonialism — then reversed it only when the political cost became too high. This is not reform. This is damage control masquerading as governance. If Saifuddin Nasution cannot tell the difference between historical scholarship and national security threats, he has no business running the Home Ministry. And if PH cannot repeal the PPPA after 2.5 years in power, stop pretending you’re any different from the governments you replaced.
