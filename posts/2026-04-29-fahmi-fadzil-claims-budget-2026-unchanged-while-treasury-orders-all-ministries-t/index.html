Fahmi Fadzil Claims ‘Budget 2026 Unchanged’ While Treasury Orders All Ministries to Slash Spending by May 15

2026-04-29
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister & Government Spokesperson (PKR)

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, 2026, Communications Minister and government spokesperson Datuk Fahmi Fadzil confirmed at his weekly press conference that all ministries had received directives from the Ministry of Finance to review and cut operating expenditures for Budget 2026. Finance Ministry secretary-general Datuk Johan Mahmood Merican issued the formal directive the same day, ordering all ministries, departments, and agencies to propose spending cuts by May 15. The reason cited was the impact of the Middle East conflict — specifically the US-Israeli war on Iran — which has sent global energy prices soaring and forced Malaysia to spend billions more on fuel subsidies. In the same breath, Fahmi claimed that “the government has no intention of tabling Budget 2026 in Parliament again” and that the budget “remains unchanged” — insisting the adjustments are merely about “optimising existing spending.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is the clearest sign yet that PH’s economic management is under severe stress. CNA reported that the Finance Ministry suggested RM5.4 billion in budget cuts targeting the health and higher education ministries — two sectors PH repeatedly promised to prioritise. The directive to cut spending while claiming the budget is “unchanged” is fiscal doublespeak of the highest order. If you order every ministry to slash expenditure and submit cut proposals by a hard deadline, the budget has changed — you are just refusing to admit it to Parliament or the public. This comes on top of ballooning subsidy costs, with the government’s public subsidy bill already exceeding the RM5 billion originally approved for 2026. PH promised fiscal discipline and economic transformation under MADANI; what Malaysians are getting instead is austerity dressed up as optimisation.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Fahmi’s claim that Budget 2026 is “unchanged” while ordering cuts is the political equivalent of saying “the house is fine” while quietly calling the fire department. You cannot simultaneously tell every ministry to slash spending by May 15 and claim the budget framework is intact. The PM himself directed that “all official events, workshops, and large-scale activities” be conducted on a “moderate and prudent scale” — this is austerity language, plain and simple. PH campaigned on competence, transparency, and an end to the BN-era habit of hiding bad news from the rakyat. Three years in, the reflex is identical: repackage cuts as “reviews,” deny the obvious, and hope nobody reads the Treasury directive. Reuters literally reviewed the internal government documents — the jig is up, but the government spokesperson is still performing.

📜 Previous Cases#

This pattern of deny-and-deflect on fiscal matters is not new for PH. When fuel subsidy rationalisation hit ordinary Malaysians harder than expected, the government framed it as “targeted” savings rather than admitting the policy hurt the middle class. When the ringgit weakened through 2024-2025, Anwar repeatedly insisted the fundamentals were strong while Bank Negara scrambled behind the scenes. The Barisan Nasional governments before them did exactly the same thing — recall Najib’s infamous claim that GST was “good for the people” while cost of living soared. PH promised to be different; instead, they have adopted the same communications playbook: control the narrative, deny the pain, and hope the next news cycle buries the story.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised transparency. You promised fiscal responsibility. You promised that MADANI governance would be honest with the rakyat about the state of the nation’s finances. So be honest: if the Middle East crisis has blown a hole in your budget — and it clearly has — say so. Table a supplementary budget. Explain to Parliament where the cuts will fall and who will bear the cost. Do not hide behind a government spokesperson claiming “nothing has changed” while Treasury directives order RM5.4 billion in cuts to health and education. The rakyat are not stupid. They can see the fuel prices. They can feel the cost of living. The least you owe them is the truth — not a weekly press conference performance pretending everything is fine.
