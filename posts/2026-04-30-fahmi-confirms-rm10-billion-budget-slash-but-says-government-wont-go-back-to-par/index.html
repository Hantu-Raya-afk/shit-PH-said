Fahmi Confirms RM10 Billion Budget Slash But Says Government Won’t Go Back to Parliament — The Dewan Rakyat Approved It, the Cabinet Rewrote It

2026-04-30
#Economy 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister & Government Spokesperson

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-04-30

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, Malaysia’s Treasury issued a directive ordering all federal ministries, departments, and agencies to slash their 2026 operating budgets — a move expected to save RM10 billion — after the government’s subsidy bill ballooned from the originally approved RM15 billion to a projected RM58.4 billion due to the Iran war energy crisis. The next day, Communications Minister and government spokesperson Fahmi Fadzil confirmed the directive at a press briefing but stated there were “no plans by the government to re-submit its 2026 budget to parliament for now,” framing the massive spending overhaul as routine “recalibration” rather than what it plainly is: a wholesale rewrite of Parliament-approved expenditure.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Parliament approved Budget 2026 with specific allocations for health (RM46.5 billion), education, defence, and every other ministry. The Treasury directive effectively shreds those allocations — proposing RM3.06 billion in cuts from health alone, RM2.39 billion from higher education — without returning to the Dewan Rakyat for consent. This is not a minor administrative adjustment; it is the executive branch unilaterally overriding legislative spending authority. In any functioning democracy, a RM43.4 billion budget shortfall and RM10 billion in emergency cuts would trigger a supplementary budget debate. Instead, PH’s government spokesperson is telling Parliament it can sit this one out.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Pakatan Harapan spent years in opposition screaming about executive overreach, demanding parliamentary supremacy, and promising institutional reform. Their 2022 manifesto explicitly pledged stronger parliamentary oversight of public finances. Now, faced with their first real fiscal crisis, their instinct is identical to every BN and PN government before them: decide in Cabinet, announce through a spokesperson, and treat Parliament as a rubber stamp that already did its job months ago. Calling a RM10 billion slash “recalibration” is the kind of Orwellian rebranding that PH used to mock when Najib’s people did it. The word you’re looking for, Fahmi, is “austerity” — and it deserves a parliamentary vote.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long history of executive budget manipulation without parliamentary consent. Under Najib Razak, off-budget spending through 1MDB and other vehicles bypassed legislative scrutiny entirely. Under Muhyiddin’s PN government, emergency ordinances were used to authorise spending without Parliament even sitting. PH loudly condemned both practices. Now they’ve found their own workaround: issue a Treasury directive, call it recalibration, and let the Communications Minister confirm it at a presser instead of the Finance Minister defending it in the Dewan Rakyat. The mechanism is different; the contempt for Parliament is the same.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a civilised, institutionally reformed government. You promised fiscal transparency. You promised parliamentary supremacy over the executive. A RM10 billion emergency cut to the national budget is exactly the kind of decision that must go through Parliament. If your Treasury can unilaterally rewrite RM10 billion in approved spending and your spokesperson’s response is “no plans to re-table,” then what was the point of Budget 2026’s parliamentary approval in the first place? Table a supplementary budget. Let MPs debate where the axe falls. Or admit that your commitment to Parliament was always conditional on it being convenient.
