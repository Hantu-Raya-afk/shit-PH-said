Saifuddin Nasution Bans Books for ‘Communist Ideologies’ — Then Scrambles to Reverse It After His Own Coalition Revolts

2026-04-16
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-16

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, Home Affairs Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail’s ministry declared two books banned for containing “communist elements and ideologies” — one of them a memoir of Shamsiah Fakeh, grandmother to a DAP assemblyman, and the other titled “Komrad Asi.” A third book, a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by PKR-led Selangor, was also prohibited. Within days, civil society groups and PH’s own coalition members revolted, calling the bans baseless. By April 23, Saifuddin was in full retreat, announcing he was in the process of revoking all three bans. He admitted he only learned about the bans after enforcement had already taken place.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a government that came to power on the promise of reform, openness, and intellectual freedom. Banning books — including one authored by a party ally’s grandmother — is not just embarrassing, it is a direct contradiction of PH’s foundational identity. The SCMP called it evidence that Anwar’s government has “tilted to the right” to court Malay voters, and the incident feeds a growing narrative that PH will sacrifice liberal principles whenever conservative pressure mounts. Every reversal like this erodes what little reformist credibility the coalition has left.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A Home Affairs Minister who doesn’t know his own ministry is banning books until after enforcement is either incompetent or lying. Either the ministry is running on autopilot — issuing bans based on Special Branch reports without ministerial oversight — or the minister approved the ban, saw the backlash, and invented the “I didn’t know” excuse. Both options are damning. PH spent years in opposition screaming about BN’s book bans, Sedition Act abuse, and censorship of dissent. Now they’re the ones banning memoirs and academic texts for “communist elements” in 2026 — a McCarthyist excuse that would embarrass even Mahathir-era censors.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long, inglorious history of book bans. The KDN’s “Hall of Banned” list includes works on May 13, the reformasi movement, corruption, governance, sexuality, and religion. Under BN, PH politicians routinely condemned these bans as authoritarian overreach. Gerakbudaya, the publisher affected by the latest bans, has been a frequent target. What’s different now is that PH is doing the banning — and then pretending to be surprised when their own base calls them out. Saifuddin’s new “mechanism” to personally review bans before enforcement is an admission that the old system was broken, but also raises the question: why did it take a public humiliation to fix it?

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia Madani — a civilised, progressive nation. You cannot build Madani while banning memoirs and academic texts for “communist ideologies.” Saifuddin, you are the Home Affairs Minister of a reformist government, not a Cold War-era censor. If your own ministry can ban books without your knowledge, you have lost control of your portfolio. If you did know, you lack the courage to defend your decision. Either way, PH’s reformist brand took another hit — self-inflicted, as usual. Stop blaming the bureaucracy. You are the bureaucracy now.
