Anwar Tells Sabah ‘40 Per Cent, No Negotiations, Period’ — While the Rest of Malaysia Is Told to Tighten Belts and Grow Vegetables

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister and Finance Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, speaking at the Madani 2026 Iftar Ceremony at Universiti Malaysia Sabah on March 15, declared that the federal government’s commitment to Sabah’s 40 per cent revenue entitlement under the Malaysia Agreement 1963 (MA63) is ‘final’ and not subject to appeal. “No negotiations. We are committed to honour our commitment of 40 per cent of the state’s revenue to the people of Sabah. Period,” Anwar said. He added that while detailed discussions are needed on implementation, the policy decision is firm and has been declared in both Cabinet and Parliament.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not a governance announcement. It is an election speech. Sabah state elections are on the horizon, and Anwar is in Kota Kinabalu making the grandest fiscal promise he can — at an iftar event, no less, maximising both the religious and political optics. The 40 per cent revenue share is a legitimate constitutional entitlement that Sabahans have demanded for decades. But the timing and tone — “period,” “no negotiations” — are designed for headlines, not for Treasury spreadsheets. The question is not whether Sabah deserves it. It is whether a government drowning in a RM3.2 billion monthly subsidy bill, an oil crisis, and self-declared austerity can actually deliver.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar is making the boldest fiscal commitment of his premiership — potentially worth billions annually — at a campaign stop in Sabah, while the same week his Cabinet told Peninsular Malaysians to grow their own vegetables, his Finance Minister told citizens not to ‘speculate’ on fuel, and the KLCI crashed below 1700. The government just tripled its RON95 subsidy to RM2 billion a month. It cannot fund targeted subsidy reform. It cannot decide on a WFH policy. But it can stand in Kota Kinabalu and say ‘period’ about a multi-billion-ringgit revenue transfer with no implementation timeline, no fiscal framework disclosed, and no explanation of how this squares with the austerity measures announced days earlier. This is not fiscal governance. It is a blank cheque written at a campaign dinner.

📜 Previous Cases#

Every prime minister since Mahathir has promised Sabah and Sarawak their MA63 rights. Najib formed a special committee. Muhyiddin signed a framework agreement. Ismail Sabri promised ‘full implementation.’ None delivered the 40 per cent in full. Anwar himself has been prime minister for over three years and the revenue share is still being ‘discussed.’ The word ‘period’ has a short shelf life in Malaysian politics — it usually means ‘until the next election is over.’

🎯 PH Callout#

You came into power promising institutional reform, fiscal discipline, and an end to empty promises. Three years later, your oil crisis response is prayer, your food security plan is backyard gardens, and your subsidy bill has tripled. And now you fly to Sabah to promise billions more in revenue transfers with no public fiscal impact assessment, no timeline, and no mechanism — just the word ‘period’ at a Ramadan event. Sabahans deserve the 40 per cent. They also deserve a government honest enough to explain how it plans to pay for it while the country’s finances are on fire. If you cannot do both, you are not governing. You are campaigning.
