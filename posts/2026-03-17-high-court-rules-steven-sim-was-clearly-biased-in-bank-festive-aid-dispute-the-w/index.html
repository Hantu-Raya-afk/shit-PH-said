High Court Rules Steven Sim Was ‘Clearly Biased’ in Bank Festive Aid Dispute — The ‘Workers’ Champion’ Sided With the Banks

2026-03-17
#Politics 

Said by: Steven Sim Chee Keong, Entrepreneur and Cooperatives Development Minister (former HR Minister)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-17

📌 What Happened#

On March 17, 2026, the Kuala Lumpur High Court ruled that former Human Resources Minister Steven Sim Chee Keong failed to maintain neutrality when he intervened in a trade dispute between the Malayan Commercial Banks’ Association (MCBA) and the National Union of Bank Employees (NUBE) over 2024 Hari Raya festive aid payments. Justice Aliza Sulaiman found that Sim had met with MCBA representatives while the matter was undergoing conciliation at the Industrial Relations Department — after which the banks agreed to pay only a half-month bonus instead of the one-month payment the union had demanded. The judge said Sim had ‘improperly aligned his office with the association’ and ‘subverted the statutory process designed for impartial resolution.’ Sim, now Entrepreneur and Cooperatives Development Minister, and the MCBA have filed an appeal.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a sitting Cabinet minister — now in a different portfolio — being told by a High Court judge that he broke the trust of his office. The Industrial Relations Act exists precisely to keep ministers out of the ring when workers and employers are in dispute. Sim didn’t just lean in — he held a meeting with the employers’ side, and the result was a worse deal for workers. The ruling exposes a fundamental contradiction: a PH minister who built his brand on being the ‘minister of workers’ was caught by the courts doing the opposite. It also raises questions about how many other ministerial interventions in labour disputes have gone unchecked.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Sim’s response today — ‘I will always stand with workers’ — is remarkable in its audacity. A judge just ruled, with evidence, that he sided with banks over workers. His defence is not to address the ruling’s substance but to repeat the slogan louder. He told reporters that ’even employers have complained I am too aligned with workers’ — as if employer complaints are proof of pro-worker bona fides. Meanwhile, the actual workers — NUBE members who got half the festive aid they were entitled to — are the ones who had to take the government to court to get accountability. The man who calls himself a workers’ champion got caught holding the bosses’ hand behind closed doors. The court saw through it. Sim still hasn’t.

📜 Previous Cases#

This isn’t the first time Sim’s handling of the NUBE-MCBA dispute raised alarms. In April 2024, NUBE filed five police reports across Peninsular Malaysia alleging abuse of power by Sim over the same festive aid issue. The union also accused him of making ‘false statements’ to the public about the dispute. Earlier, the Industrial Court dismissed NUBE’s attempt to subpoena Sim as a witness, calling it an ‘abuse of process’ — a procedural win that shielded the minister from direct testimony. PH’s track record on labour is already thin: the promised repeal of anti-union provisions in the Employment Act has stalled, and Malaysia’s gig economy protections remain weaker than those in Thailand and the Philippines.

🎯 PH Callout#

You came into government promising to be different — to stand with workers, to respect institutions, to let due process work without political interference. A High Court judge just told one of your ministers that he did the exact opposite. Steven Sim didn’t just fail the neutrality test; he failed your test. If ‘Malaysia Madani’ means anything, it means a minister who gets caught siding with banks over workers in a statutory process faces consequences — not a portfolio shuffle and a press conference about standing with the people. The court did its job. Now do yours.
