Rafizi Cries ‘Political Persecution’ After MACC Summons Over RM1.1B ARM Deal — The Man Who Built His Career on Anti-Corruption Now Can’t Handle an Investigation

2026-05-01
#Politics 

Said by: Rafizi Ramli, Former Economy Minister (PKR, Pandan MP)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

Rafizi Ramli, former Economy Minister and PKR’s Pandan MP, announced on May 1 that he received a MACC summons to give a statement at its Putrajaya headquarters on Monday at 10am. The investigation concerns the RM1.1 billion joint venture agreement between the Malaysian government and UK-based semiconductor firm Arm Holdings, signed in March 2025 during his tenure as minister. Rafizi said the probe focuses on whether he pressured Cabinet to sign the deal hastily, and that he expects possible charges including criminal breach of trust or providing misleading information. He dismissed the investigation as politically motivated, noting the complaint was lodged by Perkasa, an NGO he claims has “never previously shown interest in corruption issues.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a former senior PH minister — one of the coalition’s supposed technocratic stars — being formally summoned by MACC over a billion-ringgit government deal, and his first instinct is to cry politics. This matters because PH built its entire brand on anti-corruption and institutional accountability. When MACC investigated BN politicians, PH cheered. When MACC investigates one of their own, suddenly the process is suspect. If PH cannot accept scrutiny of its own ministers by the very institutions it promised to strengthen, then its reform credentials are hollow. The ARM deal itself — RM1.1 billion for semiconductor IP — deserves proper scrutiny regardless of who signed it.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Rafizi’s preemptive “this is political” framing is textbook deflection. He hasn’t even given his statement yet, and he’s already setting up the narrative that any charges would be persecution, not prosecution. He dismisses the complainant (Perkasa) rather than addressing the substance of the allegation — that a billion-ringgit deal was rushed through Cabinet without proper sign-off from MITI and MOF. He says “no money is involved” as if the absence of a personal bribe means a RM1.1 billion deal can’t involve misconduct. Criminal breach of trust doesn’t require the accused to pocket cash — it requires a breach of duty over entrusted assets. The man who made his career exposing 1MDB financial trails should know this.

📜 Previous Cases#

The irony is staggering. Rafizi himself was charged under the Official Secrets Act and Banking and Financial Institutions Act for exposing the National Feedlot Corporation scandal in 2012 — and he wore those charges as badges of honour, proof that institutions were being weaponized against whistleblowers. Now that he’s on the receiving end of an MACC probe, the same man who demanded accountability suddenly finds the process politically motivated. This is also not the first time a PH figure has cried foul when investigated — the pattern of “accountability for thee, not for me” runs deep in Malaysian politics, and PH is proving itself no exception to the rule it once railed against.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysians a new political culture. You said the era of impunity was over. You said institutions would be free to investigate anyone, regardless of political affiliation. So here’s MACC doing exactly that — investigating a billion-ringgit deal signed by your minister — and your response is to call it political? If the ARM deal was clean, let the process prove it. If Rafizi has nothing to hide, his statement on Monday should clear him. But preemptively poisoning the well by framing a legitimate investigation as persecution is exactly the playbook you spent 20 years condemning. Be better — or admit you never meant any of it.
