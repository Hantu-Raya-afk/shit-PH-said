Saifuddin Nasution Bans Books on Communist History, Then U-Turns a Week Later — The ‘Reformist’ Home Minister Didn’t Even Know His Own Ministry Was Banning Books

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Ministry — under PH minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail — banned two books about the Malayan Communist Party: Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah. The ministry claimed the books could “undermine public harmony and order” by normalising communist ideology. A third book, a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was also banned. One week later, on April 23, Saifuddin announced he was reversing the bans — admitting he only found out about them after enforcement had already taken place, and that he had been forced to “firefight” the fallout.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a PH government using the same colonial-era Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 that it promised voters it would repeal. Pakatan Harapan campaigned on free speech, institutional reform, and ending authoritarian-era censorship — and its own Home Ministry is still banning books on autopilot, without the minister even knowing. The reversal was not a principled stand; it was damage control after backlash from PH’s own coalition members, civil society, and even MCA Youth, who publicly called out PH’s hypocrisy. The fact that the bureaucratic machinery of censorship runs independently of the minister exposes how little structural reform PH has actually delivered after 2.5 years in power.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Pakatan Harapan built its brand on being the coalition that would defend democratic freedoms. Before GE15, PH leaders loudly championed free expression and intellectual discourse. Now in government, the Home Ministry bans history books — including a memoir the government itself helped publish over two decades ago — and the minister’s defence is that he didn’t even know it was happening. That’s not reform. That’s negligence dressed up as a U-turn. If Saifuddin cannot control his own ministry’s censorship machinery, he has no business being Home Minister. And if PH cannot repeal the PPPA after 2.5 years, stop pretending you’re a reformist government.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long and ugly history of book bans under BN — titles by Kassim Ahmad, Syed Husin Ali, and even translations of academic works were routinely prohibited under the same PPPA. PH condemned these bans in opposition. During PH’s brief 2018-2020 Sheraton-era government, they promised to abolish the PPPA but ran out of time. Now, with a comfortable majority and 2.5 years in office, the Act remains untouched — and is being actively used by PH’s own ministry. The Shamsiah Fakeh memoir, which recounts the struggles of a Malay woman in the independence movement, had been in circulation for years without incident. Banning it now serves no security purpose; it only demonstrates that PH’s reform agenda is performative.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told us you were different. You told us the PPPA was a relic of Mahathir-era authoritarianism. You told us a PH government would never ban books for political convenience. And yet here we are: your ministry banned a book written by the grandmother of your own DAP assemblyperson, Jamaliah Jamaluddin, and the minister found out from the newspapers. This is not reformasi. This is the same old machinery with a new logo. Repeal the PPPA or stop calling yourselves reformists. The choice is yours — but the rakyat is watching.
