Wong Kah Woh Says MOE Reviewing PdPR for Students Amid Oil Crisis — Three Years of Reform, and Kids Might Go Back to Zoom School

2026-03-15
#Education 

Said by: Wong Kah Woh, Deputy Education Minister (DAP)

Category: Education

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

Deputy Education Minister Wong Kah Woh told reporters at a breaking-of-fast event at Taiping Lake Gardens on March 15 that the Ministry of Education will conduct a “comprehensive review” of PdPR — Teaching and Learning from Home — for school students and ministry civil servants. Wong said the ministry would ensure “any decision taken will not adversely affect pupils and students.” This follows Education Director-General Mohd Azam Ahmad’s confirmation the day before that Education Minister Fadhlina Sidek will chair a special meeting next week to review WFH and PdPR mechanisms, pending the Cabinet’s final decision. The DG acknowledged that the MOE would “leverage its experience with WFH during the Covid-19 pandemic” to develop a strategy.

🔥 Why It Matters#

PdPR — home-based learning — was one of the most traumatic policy experiences of the pandemic era. It widened the digital divide, hurt B40 families who couldn’t afford devices or broadband, and left a generation of students with documented learning losses that Malaysia is still trying to recover from. And now, three years into a government that promised institutional reform and competent governance, the response to an oil crisis is to potentially send children back to their screens. Not because of a virus, but because PH failed to diversify Malaysia’s energy sources, failed to reform subsidies, and failed to build fiscal buffers. The fact that this is even on the table — as a cost-saving measure — tells you everything about how thin the government’s crisis toolkit really is.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Let’s be clear about what’s happening here. The government tripled the RON95 blanket subsidy instead of reforming it. It deferred the WFH decision on Thursday. The PM announced an extra holiday on Saturday. And now the education ministry is floating the idea of sending kids home to save on electricity bills in government buildings. The DG himself admitted the savings logic is shaky — if a few staff still need to come in per department, the lights stay on anyway. This isn’t a plan. It’s a government scrambling through the back of the pandemic playbook because it has no forward-looking crisis response. Wong’s reassurance that PdPR “will not adversely affect pupils” rings hollow when every study from the pandemic period shows it already did.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s PdPR experience during 2020–2022 was widely documented as a disaster for equity. UNICEF Malaysia reported that students from lower-income households lost significantly more learning time. Teachers in rural Sabah and Sarawak reported students dropping off entirely due to lack of connectivity. The Education Blueprint itself acknowledged the digital divide as a structural weakness. When PH came to power in 2022, closing these gaps was supposed to be a priority. Instead, three years later, the same ministry is considering re-opening them — not because of a health emergency, but because global oil hit $100 and the government’s fiscal planning didn’t account for it.

🎯 PH Callout#

You spent three years telling Malaysia you would build a better education system. You launched the Malaysia Education Plan 2026-2035 in January with great fanfare. And now, two months later, your crisis response might include sending children home from school to save on the electricity bill. If PdPR was a necessary evil during a pandemic, bringing it back during an oil crisis is an admission of failure — failure to plan, failure to reform, and failure to protect the people who can least afford to absorb the cost of your government’s unpreparedness. Wong says MOE will make “the best decision for educators, parents and our children.” The best decision would have been making sure it never came to this.
