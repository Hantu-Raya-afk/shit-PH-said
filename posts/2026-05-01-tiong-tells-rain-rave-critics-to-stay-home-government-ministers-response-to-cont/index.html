Tiong Tells Rain Rave Critics to Stay Home — Government Minister’s Response to Controversy Is ‘Don’t Come If You Don’t Like It’

2026-05-01
#Social 

Said by: Tiong King Sing, Tourism, Arts and Culture Minister (GPS, PH-led unity government)

Category: Social

Date: 2026-05-01

📌 What Happened#

Tourism, Arts and Culture Minister Tiong King Sing spent the first days of May defending the government-backed Rain Rave Water Music Festival 2026 in Bukit Bintang — not by addressing critics’ concerns, but by telling them to shut up. On April 30, Tiong told the press: “If you feel your religion does not permit it, then do not come.” On May 1, via Malay Mail, he urged all parties to “stop politicising” the event and said: “Do not turn every programme into a political issue. If we do that, how can we develop the economy?” He pointed to a Songkran festival held in PAS-led Kedah as proof of “inconsistent” criticism, and declared the event — which drew over 50,000 people to Bukit Bintang — a “raving success.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

This was a government-funded event marketed as the centrepiece of Visit Malaysia Year 2026. Critics — including some within the government itself — raised legitimate concerns about safety, venue suitability in a dense commercial area, and whether public money should bankroll what even PAS secretary-general Takiyuddin Hassan described as “a large-scale disco.” Instead of engaging with any of this, the Tourism Minister’s entire communications strategy was dismissal and deflection. When a cabinet minister’s default response to public concern is “don’t come,” it signals a government that treats accountability as a nuisance rather than a feature of democratic governance.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The “stop politicising” line is the laziest move in the government playbook — everything is political when public money funds it. Tiong’s whataboutism with Kedah’s Songkran is textbook deflection: the existence of similar events elsewhere doesn’t answer questions about safety, crowd management, or spending accountability in Bukit Bintang. His “respect other people’s choices” framing is convenient — this is the same government that had the home ministry ban two more books in April alone for “communist ideologies.” Apparently “respect other people’s choices” only applies when the government is throwing the party, not when citizens choose what to read. FMT reported that even critics within the government raised concerns, but Tiong’s response was to lump them in with the opposition and call it “politicising.”

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysian ministers have a long tradition of treating public criticism as personal offence. Tiong himself earlier condemned “defamatory” social media posts about the festival on Threads, casting critical citizens as bad actors rather than engaged voters. This follows the broader PH-led government pattern of claiming reform credentials while acting indistinguishable from past administrations: dismiss critics, invoke economic growth, deflect to the opposition. The same government that promised transparency and accountability under Malaysia Madani now tells citizens that asking questions about government-funded events is “politicising.”

🎯 PH Callout#

PH came to power promising a government that listens. Two and a half years on, your Tourism Minister’s answer to valid public concern is “don’t come.” You campaigned on accountability and transparency — but your ministerial response to criticism of a publicly funded event is to call it “politicising.” If your coalition ministers can’t handle questions about a water festival without telling citizens to sit down, how are Malaysians supposed to trust you with the hard stuff? Stop hiding behind “don’t politicise” when what you mean is “don’t question.”
