Anwar Hides Behind Royal Decree to Keep PH in Power in Negeri Sembilan — After Losing Majority When 14 UMNO Assemblymen Walk Out

2026-05-04
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister & Finance Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-05-04

📌 What Happened#

On May 4, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim responded to the Negeri Sembilan political crisis by refusing to comment on UMNO’s decision to withdraw support from the PH-led state government, instead invoking the Ruler’s decree to maintain the status quo. “The Umno (Negeri Sembilan) statement is not yet clear. I do not wish to comment on it,” Anwar told reporters at a ministry assembly in Putrajaya. All 14 UMNO assemblymen in the state withdrew support for Menteri Besar Aminuddin Harun on April 27, citing his interference in a constitutional crisis involving traditional chieftains attempting to depose the state ruler Tuanku Muhriz. Rather than address the loss of majority, Anwar said: “We abide by the Ruler’s decree, which calls for stability and for the Menteri Besar to continue his duties.” DAP secretary-general Anthony Loke separately insisted the state government “remains intact and stable.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

A government that has lost its legislative majority is, by basic democratic logic, a government that should seek a new mandate or step aside. But PH — the coalition that rode to power on the promise of Reformasi and democratic governance — is now using royal authority as a shield to keep its man in the chief minister’s seat. This is the same political movement that spent decades railing against the use of royal prerogatives to override parliamentary democracy. The Negeri Sembilan crisis is a test case for whether PH’s commitment to democratic principles survives contact with the prospect of losing power. So far, the answer is no.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Anwar’s refusal to comment is not neutrality — it’s a calculated dodge. He knows that acknowledging UMNO’s withdrawal means conceding PH has lost its majority in the state. So instead, he wraps himself in the Ruler’s decree, effectively saying: “We don’t need a majority because the palace says we stay.” This is the Reformasi playbook turned inside out. When BN used royal institutions to buttress its grip on power, PH called it undemocratic. When PH does the same thing, it’s called “respecting the royal institution.” Anthony Loke’s insistence that the government is “stable” despite losing 14 assemblymen is the kind of reality-denial that would make a BN-era spin doctor blush.

📜 Previous Cases#

PH has a pattern of clinging to power despite losing coalitional support. At the federal level, the failure to pass the PM term-limit constitutional amendment in March 2026 exposed cracks in the ruling bloc’s discipline. At the state level, PH has relied on the unity government formula to maintain power even where its own support base is thin. The Negeri Sembilan crisis echoes the Sheraton Move of 2020 — except this time, PH is the party refusing to accept the democratic implications of a coalition collapse, rather than the party victimized by one.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Malaysia that Reformasi meant power belongs to the people, not the palace. You promised that governments should stand or fall by their legislative majority. Now 14 assemblymen have walked out in Negeri Sembilan and your response is: “The Ruler says we stay.” Pick one, Anwar — either you believe in parliamentary democracy or you believe in royal decree. You cannot invoke whichever one happens to keep you in power at any given moment. If you have lost the majority, prove you haven’t — on the floor of the state assembly, not behind the gates of the Istana.
