Anwar Says ‘Bring the Evidence’ on Stolen Funds Allegations — The PM Who Promised Transparency Now Demands Others Do the Investigating

2026-04-24
#Politics 

Said by: Anwar Ibrahim, Prime Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-24

📌 What Happened#

On April 24, 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim was confronted by reporters at the Temenggong Ibrahim Teachers Education Campus in Johor Bahru about a viral 49-second video by a woman named Sofia Rini Buyong, who alleged that a former officer of Anwar’s had been instructed to move stolen public money to Africa and possessed unexplained wealth worth “billions of ringgit.” Anwar dismissed the allegations outright, saying he would not entertain baseless claims from social media, asking “How do I entertain such allegations? If I have 1,000 comments, do I have to answer every one?” He challenged accusers to “bring the evidence,” adding that without proof, such allegations were merely a “breeding ground for lies.” Opposition politicians from PAS and Bersatu had called for an investigation. No evidence was provided in the video.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a sitting Prime Minister — the one who built his entire political career on anti-corruption rhetoric and institutional reform — being asked about serious financial misconduct allegations involving his own former staff, and his response is to wave it away and tell others to prove it. That is not how accountability works when you are the head of government. Anwar controls the MACC, the police, and the Attorney General’s Chambers. He does not need social media users to “bring evidence” — he has the entire enforcement apparatus of the state at his disposal. His refusal to even acknowledge the need for a check signals that allegations involving his own circle receive a different standard than those involving his opponents.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

This is the same Anwar Ibrahim who, when in opposition, demanded investigations into every allegation against BN and UMNO leaders — often on far thinner evidence than a named accuser making specific claims on video. The man who rode the Reformasi wave, who demanded Royal Commissions of Inquiry at the drop of a hat, who made “institutionalised corruption” his battle cry for two decades — now says he cannot be expected to respond to every allegation. The double standard is breathtaking. When it was Najib, Zahid, or Muhyiddin, the mere whiff of financial impropriety was enough for Anwar to demand blood. When it involves his own former officer, suddenly the burden of proof shifts entirely to the public, and the PM is too busy to care.

📜 Previous Cases#

This pattern is not new. In the MACC chief Azam Baki scandal, Anwar’s government delayed action for months despite overwhelming public pressure, only replacing Azam after protest rallies forced his hand — and even then, without pursuing criminal accountability. On the Ramanan-Bestinet migrant worker contract scandal, Anwar remained silent while his own PKR MPs demanded an investigation. On the corporate mafia allegations involving Victor Chin and multiple cabinet members, the government’s response has been denial and dismissal rather than proactive investigation. The Anwar administration has consistently treated allegations against its own people as nuisances to be swatted away, not accountability triggers to be acted upon.

🎯 PH Callout#

You came to power on the promise that Malaysia would never again have a Prime Minister who treated corruption allegations as political attacks to be deflected. You promised Malaysians that under your watch, no one — not even those closest to you — would be above scrutiny. So when a named individual makes a specific allegation about your former officer on video, the correct response is not “bring the evidence” — it is “I will ensure this is investigated.” That is what reform looks like. That is what you promised. If you cannot even muster the pretence of taking allegations seriously when they touch your own orbit, then your Reformasi brand is dead, and you are just another Prime Minister protecting his people. Malaysians deserved better. You said you would be better.
