Johari Declares US-Malaysia Trade Deal ‘Null and Void’ — The US Supreme Court Did the Walking, Not Him

2026-03-15
#Economy 

Said by: Johari Abdul Ghani, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister

Category: Economy

Date: 2026-03-15

📌 What Happened#

On March 15, 2026, Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani confirmed that the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) between Malaysia and the United States is now “null and void.” The deal, signed by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and US President Donald Trump on October 26, 2025 in Kuala Lumpur, was killed not by Malaysian spine but by the US Supreme Court, which on February 20 struck down Trump’s broad reciprocal tariffs as unconstitutional. Johari told reporters: “It is not on hold. It’s null and void.” The US has since imposed a uniform 10% tariff on all trading partners and launched Section 301 investigations into 60 countries, including Malaysia, over forced labour and excess production capacity.

🔥 Why It Matters#

Malaysia signed the ART under enormous pressure — initial tariff exposure was 47%, negotiated down to 24%, then 19% under the deal, with zero tariffs on 1,711 Malaysian products. In exchange, Malaysia offered deeper market access and policy concessions to Washington. Now that the deal is dead, Malaysia gets the same 10% tariff as countries that gave away nothing. The concessions Malaysia made during negotiations may have been for nothing. Worse, the US has already launched new Section 301 investigations targeting Malaysian exports worth RM233 billion — covering E&E, palm oil, oil and gas, and rubber gloves — over forced labour and dumping concerns. The opposition, led by PN secretary-general Takiyuddin Hassan, is demanding a special parliamentary sitting to explain the economic fallout.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The government is framing this as if they took decisive action. PKR MP Wong Chen celebrated on social media that Johari “cancelled the controversial and lop-sided US-Malaysia trade deal.” StratNews Global ran with “Malaysia Walks Out Of Trade Deal.” But Malaysia didn’t walk out of anything — the US Supreme Court pulled the rug. The ART was already widely criticised as lopsided when it was signed. PH signed it anyway, under a photo-op with Trump in KL, and now celebrates its death as vindication. This is the political equivalent of claiming credit for the rain after you failed to dig a well. And the real danger — Section 301 investigations, forced labour allegations, and RM233 billion in exposed exports — is barely being addressed.

📜 Previous Cases#

This is not PH’s first encounter with the gap between trade rhetoric and trade reality. The government spent years criticising BN-era trade deals as opaque and one-sided, then signed the ART with Trump under similar pressure and with limited parliamentary scrutiny. Johari himself was on record just days earlier (March 14) telling reporters the government could only “hold out for two or three months” on subsidy spending amid the Middle East oil crisis — the same minister now trying to project confidence on trade. Malaysia’s forced labour problem, flagged repeatedly by US customs through withhold release orders on gloves and palm oil, remains unresolved after three years of PH governance and reform promises.

🎯 PH Callout#

You signed the deal. You posed for the photos. You called it diplomacy. Now that the US Supreme Court killed it for you, you want credit for the cleanup? Here’s what the rakyat actually needs: a clear accounting of what concessions were made under the ART and whether any of them are still in play; a plan to address the Section 301 investigations before they hit RM233 billion in exports; and an honest explanation of why Malaysia’s forced labour record still isn’t clean after three years of your “reformist” government. Stop spinning court rulings as policy wins. Govern.
