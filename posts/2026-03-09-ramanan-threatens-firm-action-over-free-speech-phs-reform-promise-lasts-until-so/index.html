Ramanan Threatens ‘Firm Action’ Over Free Speech — PH’s Reform Promise Lasts Until Someone Uses It

2026-03-09
#Politics 

Said by: R. Ramanan, Human Resources Minister and PKR Vice-President

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-03-09

📌 What Happened#

On March 9, 2026, Human Resources Minister and PKR Vice-President Datuk Seri R. Ramanan issued a public statement warning that the government will take “firm action” against anyone who “deliberately threatens national peace,” particularly on matters involving religion, race, and the monarchy — the so-called 3R issues. Ramanan said there has been “a worrying rise in statements, social media posts and online comments aimed at provoking racial tensions” and warned that “freedom of speech should not be misused to incite or divide society.” The following day, he told reporters he would personally raise the issue of unnamed “provocateurs” at the March 11 Cabinet meeting. Entrepreneur and Cooperative Development Minister Steven Sim echoed the call, demanding police “act firmly against all perpetrators.”

🔥 Why It Matters#

PH came to power promising a new relationship between the state and free expression. They promised to review the Sedition Act, reform the Communications and Multimedia Act, and end the BN-era practice of using vague security laws to silence dissent. Three years later, a PKR vice-president is issuing blanket warnings about “firm action” over speech, citing “constitutional supremacy” — the same language the old regime used to justify crackdowns. The 3R framework has historically been Malaysia’s most effective tool for chilling public discourse: label any inconvenient conversation as a threat to “national harmony,” and the state machinery can move against critics without needing to prove actual harm. Ramanan’s statement is a textbook deployment of that framework.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Notice what’s missing from Ramanan’s statement: specifics. Who are the “provocateurs”? Under what law will “firm action” be taken? What is the threshold between legitimate speech and a prosecutable threat to “national peace”? These are the questions PH used to ask when they were in opposition. Now they are in government, and the answer is: trust us. The vague threat of “firm action” over undefined 3R provocations is not governance — it’s intimidation dressed up as concern for social harmony. If there are genuine criminal acts — vandalism, threats, incitement to violence — existing laws already cover them. Ramanan doesn’t need a Cabinet meeting for that. What he needs a Cabinet meeting for is a mandate to cast the net wider.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long and ugly history of using 3R sensitivity as a pretext for silencing inconvenient voices. Under BN, the Sedition Act was wielded against academics, journalists, activists, and opposition politicians — often for statements that were critical of government policy but framed as threats to racial or religious harmony. PH themselves were victims of this playbook. Anwar’s own allies were charged under these laws. The coalition promised repeal or reform of the Sedition Act — it has done neither. Instead, it now deploys the same rhetorical toolkit: vague warnings, unnamed provocateurs, appeals to “harmony,” and the implicit threat of state power behind it all.

🎯 PH Callout#

You spent two decades on the receiving end of 3R crackdowns. Your leaders were charged under the Sedition Act. Your supporters were investigated for social media posts. You told Malaysians you would end the weaponisation of vague security laws against free expression. And now your own vice-president is on national television warning of “firm action” over undefined provocations and telling citizens their freedom of speech “should not be misused.” That is the same sentence, in the same tone, from the same podium — just delivered by a different coalition. If you have evidence of criminal conduct, prosecute it under existing law. If you don’t, stop using the machinery of state to tell Malaysians to shut up.
