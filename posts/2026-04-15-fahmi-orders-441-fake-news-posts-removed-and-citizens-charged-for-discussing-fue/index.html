Fahmi Orders 441 ‘Fake News’ Posts Removed and Citizens Charged for Discussing Fuel Prices — Reformasi Has Become Represi

2026-04-15
#Politics 

Said by: Fahmi Fadzil, Communications Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-15

📌 What Happened#

Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil announced on April 15, 2026, that the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) had flagged 441 posts about the global energy crisis for removal from social media, opened 54 investigation papers against citizens, submitted 12 cases to the Attorney General’s Chambers, charged one person in the Sepang Sessions Court, and compounded five others at RM25,000 each — all under Section 233 of the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 for posting about fuel price increases, diesel costs, electricity tariffs, and energy policy during Malaysia’s worst energy crisis in years, with oil hitting US$115 per barrel.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is the same Pakatan Harapan coalition that rode into power in 2018 on promises of press freedom, media reform, and repealing oppressive laws like the Sedition Act and Section 233 of the Communications and Multimedia Act. Instead of repealing those laws, PH is now weaponising them at industrial scale against ordinary Malaysians who dare to question or discuss the government’s handling of soaring energy prices. The crackdown turns the concept of “misinformation” into a blunt instrument for managing political dissent during an economic crisis — exactly what BN did, and exactly what PH swore on every ceramah stage it would never do.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

The content being removed and prosecuted includes posts about “petrol price hikes,” “increases in electricity tariffs by TNB,” and “diesel costs” — topics of legitimate public concern during a genuine energy crisis where subsidies ballooned from RM15 billion budgeted to RM58.4 billion projected. Calling citizen discussion “fake news” while the government itself refuses to provide transparent, timely updates on energy policy is not combating misinformation — it is censoring inconvenient questions. When a government’s response to an energy crisis includes prosecuting people for talking about fuel prices and fining them RM25,000 each, the real fake news is the government’s claim to be reformist.

📜 Previous Cases#

PH has a well-documented pattern of reverting to the authoritarian reflexes it once condemned. The coalition failed to repeal the Sedition Act despite manifesto promises. Fahmi’s ministry has taken action against bloggers and social media users under Section 233 CMA throughout 2024 and 2025. The government repealed the Anti-Fake News Act 2018 with great fanfare during its first term, only to rely on equivalent powers through existing laws to achieve the same result — silencing dissent. Even during the Gaza flotilla crisis on April 30, Fahmi’s reflexive response included warnings against “misinformation,” showing that the censorship instinct is now hardwired into PH governance.

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised the rakyat would never fear speaking truth to power. You promised media freedom. You promised accountability and reformasi. Instead, Fahmi Fadzil’s ministry is running a prosecution factory against citizens who discuss fuel prices — during the worst energy crisis in a decade. 441 posts flagged. 54 investigations. Criminal charges. RM25,000 fines. For talking about petrol. The rakyat are not the threat to national stability — a government that treats public concern as a criminal offence is. If 441 posts about fuel prices constitute “fake news,” then PH’s entire reform manifesto is the biggest piece of fake news Malaysia has ever seen.
