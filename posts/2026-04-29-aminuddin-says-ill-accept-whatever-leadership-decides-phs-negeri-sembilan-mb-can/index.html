Aminuddin Says ‘I’ll Accept Whatever Leadership Decides’ — PH’s Negeri Sembilan MB Can’t Even Resign Without Permission

2026-04-29
#Politics 

Said by: Aminuddin Harun, Negeri Sembilan Menteri Besar (PKR Vice-President)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-29

📌 What Happened#

On April 29, 2026, Negeri Sembilan Menteri Besar Datuk Seri Aminuddin Harun — a PKR vice-president and PH’s man in the state — told reporters he would “abide by any decision made by the Pakatan Harapan leadership” regarding his position, two days after 14 UMNO-BN assemblymen withdrew support and left him leading a minority government with only 17 seats in the 36-seat state assembly. He insisted the absence of four UMNO exco members “did not affect the state administration” and said he had not engaged in talks with BN or PN representatives to shore up support.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a sitting chief minister admitting, on the record, that he cannot decide his own political fate — and instead asking his party bosses in Putrajaya to decide for him. Aminuddin lost his majority because he mishandled a royal crisis involving the state’s customary Undang system and the Yang di-Pertuan Besar, not because of some external conspiracy. PH’s failure to manage its own state government is now a test case for coalition governance, and the fact that UMNO president Ahmad Zahid Hamidi had to personally step in on April 30 to broker a deal with the 14 assemblymen — not PH’s own leadership — tells you everything about who actually runs the Unity Government.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A leader who cannot lead should resign — full stop. Instead, Aminuddin clings to his chair, insists the absence of four UMNO exco members “does not affect the state administration,” and tells reporters he’s “always open” to whatever the leadership decides. That is not leadership — that is a man waiting to be fired while pretending he is being cooperative. PH ran in 2022 on institutional reform and good governance. A chief minister who loses his majority because he botched a constitutional crisis with the state’s monarchy, then refuses to take responsibility, is the opposite of everything they promised.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long history of chief ministers clinging to power amid confidence crises — from the Perak constitutional crisis of 2009 where BN seized power from Pakatan Rakyat, to the Sheraton Move of 2020 that toppled PH at the federal level. The difference this time is that PH is on both sides of the failure: their man bungled the crisis, and their coalition partner UMNO is the one pulling the plug. When BN did this in the past, PH screamed about democratic mandates. When it happens to them, the playbook is “let the leadership decide.”

🎯 PH Callout#

You promised Reformasi. You promised institutional integrity. You promised that under Madani, governance would be clean, competent, and accountable. Your Negeri Sembilan MB lost a constitutional standoff with the state monarchy, lost his majority, and instead of resigning with dignity, he is asking you to decide for him. That is not reform — that is feudal politics wearing a reformist t-shirt. If Aminuddin cannot command a majority, he should go. If PH leadership cannot tell him that, then the party is no different from the patronage machines it replaced.
