Saifuddin Admits He Only Learned About Book Bans After They Were Enforced — Madani Government Banned 3 Books as ‘Communist Threats,’ Then U-Turned Under Pressure

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Minister (PKR)

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 15, Malaysia’s Home Ministry issued a prohibition order on two Malay-language books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari AWAS ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — declaring them dangers to national security for containing “communist elements and ideologies.” By April 22, a third book, a Malay translation of an academic study on Mao Zedong funded by the PKR-led Selangor state government, was also banned. Then on April 23, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail announced the government was reversing all three bans — and admitted he only learned about the prohibition orders after enforcement had already taken place.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is not an isolated incident. Reports indicate that Malaysia has banned more book titles in early 2026 than in all of 2025, with the pace of censorship accelerating under the Madani government. The bans were carried out under the Printing Presses and Publications Act, a colonial-era law that Anwar’s coalition once pledged to reform. The fact that a “reformist” government is using the same censorship tools as its predecessors — and at a faster clip — exposes the chasm between Madani slogans and actual governance. When international press freedom groups like ARTICLE 19 are publicly condemning your government’s actions, the reform brand is not holding.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

A government that markets itself on “compassion, respect, and reform” banned a memoir by a Malay feminist independence fighter — the grandmother of one of Anwar’s own DAP allies — as a national security threat. Then the Home Minister admitted he didn’t even know it happened until after the fact. Either Saifuddin has no control over his own ministry, or the bureaucracy is running on colonial autopilot while the minister looks the other way. The reversal only came after civil society groups, publishers, PEN Malaysia, and even PH’s own coalition members pushed back publicly. Reform by embarrassment is not reform — it’s damage control.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia’s book-banning habit runs deep. The Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 has been wielded by every government to suppress inconvenient texts. Under BN, books by opposition figures and controversial academics were routinely banned. Anwar’s own writings were once restricted under Mahathir’s government. The irony is inescapable: a man who was himself a victim of state censorship now leads a government that bans books at an accelerating pace. Even Mahathir’s brief PH1 government in 2018-2020 signalled intent to reform the Act — a promise never delivered, and one Anwar’s Madani government has also quietly shelved.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians that Madani meant a new era — respect for intellectual freedom, transparency, accountability. Instead, your Home Ministry bans books without your own minister knowing, uses a colonial-era law you promised to reform, and only reverses course when the backlash becomes too loud to ignore. Your own Selangor government funded one of the books you banned. Your own ally’s grandmother wrote another. If you cannot even control your own censorship apparatus, what exactly are you governing? Repeal the Printing Presses and Publications Act or stop pretending you are any different from the governments that came before you.
