Saifuddin Nasution Bans Books First, Asks Questions Later — Home Minister Admits He Only Found Out About His Own Ministry’s Ban After Enforcement

2026-04-23
#Politics 

Said by: Saifuddin Nasution Ismail, Home Affairs Minister

Category: Politics

Date: 2026-04-23

📌 What Happened#

On April 16, 2026, Malaysia’s Home Affairs Ministry issued prohibition orders on two books — Memoir Shamsiah Fakeh: Dari Awas ke Rejimen Ke-10 and Komrad Asi (Rejimen 10): Dalam Denyut Nihilisme Sejarah — along with a Malay translation of a study on Mao Zedong, citing “communist ideologies” as a threat to national security. One of the banned memoirs was authored by the grandmother of a DAP state assemblyman. The ban triggered immediate backlash from civil society, publishers, international press freedom groups including Article 19, and even members of Anwar’s own Pakatan Harapan coalition. By April 23, Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail announced the government was in the process of revoking all three bans — and admitted he had only learned about the prohibition orders after enforcement had already taken place.

🔥 Why It Matters#

This is a government that ran on reform, press freedom, and rolling back authoritarian-era censorship laws. Instead, its own Home Ministry used the Printing Presses and Publications Act — a Mahathir-era tool of control — to ban books about historical figures who have been dead for decades, without the minister even knowing. The fact that Saifuddin had to “firefight” his own ministry’s decisions exposes a governance vacuum at the heart of a portfolio that directly touches civil liberties. It also signals that the security apparatus within the Home Ministry operates on autopilot, issuing bans based on Special Branch reports with zero ministerial oversight — exactly the kind of unchecked enforcement PH promised to dismantle.

💩 Why It’s Bullshit#

Pakatan Harapan campaigned in 2022 on repealing or reforming oppressive laws, including the Printing Presses and Publications Act. Three and a half years later, not only is the Act still intact, but it is being actively used — and the minister responsible claims he didn’t even know. Saifuddin’s defence that the “previous process” forced him to firefight is an admission that he failed to reform the very mechanism he inherited. You don’t get to blame the system when you’ve been running it for over two years. The reversal is not a win for reform — it is damage control after getting caught. And the new “mechanism” where the minister will personally review bans? That should have been the default from day one.

📜 Previous Cases#

Malaysia has a long and ugly history of book bans under the Printing Presses and Publications Act, from Mahathir-era crackdowns on political dissent literature to the banning of comic books and academic texts deemed “sensitive.” PH itself criticised BN governments for using this exact law to suppress intellectual freedom. In 2018, the first PH government under Mahathir pledged to review banned publications — a promise that was quietly abandoned after the Sheraton Move. Now, under PH 2.0, the same playbook is running: ban first, review later, reverse only when the backlash gets loud enough.

🎯 PH Callout#

You told Malaysians you would be different. You said the days of banning books and criminalising ideas were over. Instead, your own ministry banned a dead woman’s memoir and an academic translation — and your minister found out from the newspapers. If Saifuddin Nasution cannot control what his ministry does with a censorship law that PH promised to reform, then either reform the law or admit you never intended to. “We’re revoking the ban” is not reform. It’s a U-turn after getting caught driving in the wrong direction.
